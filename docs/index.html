Targeted metabolic reprogramming of CD8 T cells restores immune regulation in lupus.

New todayProc Natl Acad Sci U S A · Oba S, Humbel MH, Lancia HH et al. · Full text
Abstract

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is characterized by pathogenic humoral autoimmunity, yet the mechanisms linking T cell dysfunction to aberrant B cell responses remain poorly defined. Here, we identify a CD8+ T cell-intrinsic metabolic defect that promotes humoral immune dysregulation in lupus. CD8+ T cells from patients with SLE exhibited elevated CD38 expression associated with reduced intracellular NAD+ levels, mitochondrial dysfunction, and impaired cytotoxic capacity. Pharmacologic inhibition of CD38 with the small-molecule inhibitor MK-0159 restored NAD+ metabolism and rescued cytotoxic function in human CD8+ T cells ex vivo. To selectively target this pathway in vivo, we developed anti-CD8 antibody-conjugated nanoparticles encapsulating MK-0159. In lupus-prone MRL/lpr mice, CD8-targeted delivery enhanced granzyme B expression, improved degranulation, reduced plasmablast responses and autoantibody production, immune complex deposition, and renal pathology. Importantly, targeted delivery restored CD8+ T cell function without broad immune perturbation. These findings identify CD38-driven NAD+ depletion as a reversible immunometabolic checkpoint limiting CD8+ T cell cytotoxic competence and support a model in which targeted restoration of cytotoxic fitness contributes to the control of humoral autoimmunity in SLE.

Bifunctional Pt/CeO2 Nanozyme-Mediated Metabolic Regulation Promotes NAD+ Regeneration and ROS Clearance to Ameliorate Age-Related Osteoporosis.

ACS Appl Mater Interfaces · Zhang H, Yang C, Zhou Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Age-related osteoporosis is characterized by progressive loss of bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, driven by chronic inflammation, redox imbalance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and osteoblast senescence that uncouples bone formation from resorption. During aging, declining nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) availability further exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and stress responses, suggesting that restoring NAD+ redox homeostasis could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Here, we reported a bifunctional nanozyme-mediated metabolic regulation strategy to promote NAD+ regeneration and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) for the treatment of age-related osteoporosis. The bifunctional nanozyme (Pt/CeO2) was constructed by in situ growth of platinum nanoparticles on cerium oxide nanorods and was designed for ROS scavenging and NAD+ regeneration via NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) oxidation. In senescent osteoblasts, Pt/CeO2 reduced ROS, restored mitochondrial integrity, and alleviated senescence, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation. In vivo results confirmed that systemic administration of Pt/CeO2 preserved trabecular architecture and increased bone mineral density in naturally aged mice. These findings demonstrated that coupling ROS scavenging with the restoration of NAD+ redox homeostasis via Pt/CeO2 nanozyme provided a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract osteoblast senescence and slow the progression of age-related osteoporosis.

Dual-layer regulation of macrophage grancalcin links diabetic redox-metabolic imbalance to impaired vascularized bone regeneration.

Redox Biol · Jin S, Song Z, Fang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Effective bone regeneration requires the timely transition of immune cells from inflammatory defense to regenerative support. However, how diabetic redox-metabolic stress sustains maladaptive immune states and drives regenerative failure remains incompletely understood. Herein, using a mouse jawbone defect model combined with single-cell RNA sequencing, metabolic assays and human peri-extraction gingival specimens, we identified a senescence-associated macrophage state characterized by elevated grancalcin (GCA). Functionally, recombinant GCA impaired osteogenic and endothelial responses, whereas GCA neutralization partially reversed the inhibitory effects of diabetic macrophage-conditioned medium, supporting extracellular GCA as a functional mediator of regenerative dysfunction. Mechanistically, diabetic macrophages exhibited enhanced glycolytic activity, impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and a reduced NAD+/NADH ratio. This redox imbalance lowered SIRT6 abundance, increased H3K9ac/H3K56ac enrichment at the accessible Gca regulatory locus and sustained Gca transcription. Meanwhile, hyperglycemia-triggered Ca2+ signaling promoted GCA recruitment to membrane-proximal regions and increased both total extracellular and extracellular vesicle-associated GCA. Local delivery of quercetin using a β-cyclodextrin/GelMA hydrogel attenuated macrophage GCA dysregulation, reduced senescence-associated macrophage features and improved vascularized jawbone regeneration. Collectively, this work defines a dual-layer paradigm controlling extracellular GCA bioavailability, thereby linking diabetic redox-metabolic perturbation to defective bone regeneration and supporting local targeting of macrophage GCA for diabetic bone-healing therapy.

Neurovascular Unit Redox-Metabolic Communication in Poststroke Cognitive Impairment: Spatiotemporal Dysregulation, Cellular Crosstalk, and Precision Intervention.

ReviewEur J Neurosci · Zhang Q, Chen Y, Bai M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is a dynamic syndrome driven by focal brain injury, pre-existing cerebrovascular burden, metabolic comorbidities, and reduced brain resilience. Increasing evidence suggests that persistent disruption of redox-metabolic communication within the neurovascular unit (NVU), rather than neuronal loss alone, promotes the transition from acute ischemic injury to chronic cognitive decline. This review summarizes metabolic reprogramming in BMECs, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, and neurons, emphasizing the intercellular transfer of metabolites, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondria. We propose that PSCI develops through a spatiotemporally dysregulated continuum involving vascular injury, cell-specific metabolic remodeling, maladaptive signal propagation, impaired axonal and synaptic energy support, and progressive network disconnection. Ischemia-reperfusion triggers bioenergetic failure, NADH/NAD+ imbalance, mitochondrial electron leakage, endothelial dysfunction, and capillary no-reflow. Subsequently, blood-brain barrier disruption, immunometabolic activation, iron and lipid dysregulation, and defective organelle quality control sustain white matter and synaptic injury. Therapeutic strategies should therefore be stage-specific: restoring microcirculatory perfusion and physiological redox signaling acutely, promoting inflammation resolution and metabolic repair subacutely, and combining vascular-metabolic modulation with neurorehabilitation chronically. Future studies should integrate cell type-specific interventions, metabolic flux tracing, spatial multi-omics, and longitudinal cognitive assessments to establish causal donor-carrier-recipient-stage-outcome relationships.

Abstract

Long COVID, chronic fatigue syndrome, and chronic lyme disease as Post-acute Infection Syndromes (PAIS): a translational synthesis.

New todayReviewJ Transl Med · Sewell PE, Reddy R · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) comprise a heterogeneous group of chronic, multisystem conditions characterized by persistent or relapsing symptoms following resolution of an acute infectious illness, in the absence of ongoing pathogen replication detectable by standard diagnostics. Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), Long COVID, post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, and related entities are increasingly recognized as part of a shared post-infectious disease spectrum. The COVID-19 pandemic has markedly expanded the global burden of PAIS and intensified investigation into their biological underpinnings. MAIN BODY: This narrative translational review integrates clinical, epidemiologic, genetic, immunologic, metabolic, and neurovascular evidence to propose a unified framework for PAIS pathophysiology and management. Recent genome-wide association studies support heritable susceptibility involving immune regulation, mitochondrial maintenance, and neuroinflammatory pathways. Diagnostic paradigms remain primarily clinical, though emerging multi-omic and cellular biomarkers show translational promise. Tiered diagnostic and evaluation strategies are discussed to guide clinicians while minimizing harm, particularly post-exertional malaise. CONCLUSIONS: PAIS represent biologically grounded, systems-level disorders with shared mechanisms and heterogeneous clinical expression. Integrating molecular insights with pragmatic clinical frameworks may accelerate development of precision-oriented diagnostic and therapeutic strategies to address the growing global impact of post-infectious chronic illness.

Novel Proteolyzed Aortic Extracellular Matrix Substrate Coating as a Model to Assess Aneurysmal Smooth Muscle Cell Behavior In Vitro.

Tissue Eng Part A · Krajewski T, Ramamurthi A · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAAs) are localized expansions of the abdominal aortic wall that grow slowly to potentially fatal rupture. AAAs result from irreversible loss of structural extracellular matrix (ECM) due to upregulated proteolytic activity. Surgery to treat AAAs is risky; therefore, new drug-based approaches are needed to slow aneurysm growth by reversing ECM pathophysiology. This necessitates the study of disease-relevant cells in vitro, in this case smooth muscle cells (SMCs); however, current in vitro models are limited due to SMC dedifferentiation on conventional plastic substrates, which cause a loss of disease-relevant features. This study generated a physiologically relevant culture model to assess matrix regenerative and reparative responses of AAA SMCs. These behaviors are more evocative of subprimary aneurysmal cells compared with traditionally cultured SMCs, demonstrating the capability of our native ECM substrate to modulate and promote pathophysiologically relevant SMC phenotypes in vitro. This model system offers a promising platform for screening aneurysmal SMC responses to therapeutics similar to expected in vivo outcomes.

AMOTL2: A key regulator of mechanotransduction in development, vascular disease and cancer.

ReviewCells Dev · Fonseca P, Zhang Y, Holmgren L · Full text
Abstract

Mechanical forces play a fundamental role in regulating cell behavior during development, tissue homeostasis, and disease. The angiomotin family protein AmotL2 has emerged as a central integrator of mechanotransduction, linking cell-cell adhesion, cytoskeletal tension, and nuclear architecture to transcriptional control. Through its ability to connect cadherin-based junctions to radial actin filaments and the nuclear lamina, AmotL2 establishes a continuous mechanical axis that enables cells to sense and respond to extracellular forces. This function is essential for epithelial organization, vascular morphogenesis, and endothelial responses to shear stress, in part through regulation of Hippo pathway effectors such as YAP and TAZ. In this review, we summarize the structural and mechanistic basis of AmotL2 function and discuss its roles in development, vascular biology, and cancer, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target in diseases driven by altered mechanotransduction.

Developing structure and strategies for analysis of circulating biomarkers to detect and monitor abdominal aortic aneurysms.

Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol · Lindsey ML, Shivam P, Nde PN et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a multifactorial disease characterized by progressive remodeling of the aortic wall. Current clinical management includes screening ultrasound for diagnosis, and routine aortic imaging to monitor growth in patients with small AAA and surgical exclusion of larger AAA. With the evolution of a diverse array of multianalyte assessments from peripheral blood, comprehensive assessments of circulating biomarkers in patients with AAA is possible. In the context of this dynamic disease process, circulating markers - paired with clinical and imaging data - provide an opportunity to identify specific active biologic processes that influence the natural history of the disease. Beyond the Aorta: Extra-Aortic Vascular Disease and Screening in Heritable and Systemic Arteriopathies.

New todayReviewJ Pers Med · Abdul Nabi H, Dreher L, Husain M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Heritable thoracic aortic disease (HTAD) comprises genetic disorders that predispose to thoracic aortic aneurysm and dissection. Several also affect arteries beyond the aorta. This narrative review examines the burden, distribution, mechanisms, and screening implications of extra-aortic vascular disease in vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (vEDS), Loeys-Dietz syndrome (LDS), Marfan syndrome (MFS), and nonsyndromic HTAD, alongside the related systemic arteriopathies spontaneous coronary artery dissection (SCAD) and fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD). Disease- and genotype-specific patterns emerge, although estimates differ with referral patterns, imaging coverage, and outcome definitions. In the Montalcino Aortic Consortium (MAC), COL3A1 carriers had the highest proportion of extra-aortic clinical arterial events, with prominent abdominopelvic and cervical involvement. LDS showed widespread arterial disease and head and neck involvement. MFS had fewer clinical arterial events, but imaging studies identified branch aneurysms, particularly in patients with advanced aortic disease. SCAD and FMD frequently involved additional vascular territories. Findings from broader nonsyndromic thoracic aortic disease cohorts should be distinguished from genetically confirmed HTAD. Imaging-detected aneurysm prevalence and clinical event proportions are not interchangeable. Guidelines and consensus support disease-specific vascular assessment, but anatomical coverage, surveillance intervals, and evidence strength vary. Comprehensive baseline imaging is supported in LDS, FMD, and SCAD; European guidance also recommends peripheral surveillance in vEDS and MFS. Additional assessment in nonsyndromic HTAD and cervical artery dissection depends on genotype and clinical features. We outline a practical framework integrating the presenting vascular lesion, molecular diagnosis, prior events, family history, and imaging-related burden, and discuss when an extra-aortic presentation should prompt aortic evaluation. Prospective studies are needed to establish whether increased detection and changes in management improve patient-important outcomes.

Sleep, stasis, and thromboprotection: what hibernating mammals can teach us about cardiovascular risk.

New todayReviewPlatelets · Zauli E, Caruso L, Zauli G et al. · Full text
Abstract

Prolonged immobility is one of the best-known triggers for venous blood clots, yet hibernating brown bears spend months lying still without ever developing them - and something similar seems to hold for many people with chronic paralysis. Thienel and colleagues recently offered a compelling explanation: platelets in hibernating bears sharply downregulate a protein called HSP47, dropping to roughly 55-fold below normal levels. HSP47 turns out to drive clot formation on two fronts at once, by boosting platelet activation and by prompting neutrophils to release the web-like structures that help clots take hold. Suppress it, and both arms of the process weaken together. That this pattern shows up across bears, immobilized patients, bed-rest volunteers, and other animal models suggests it isn't a quirk of bear physiology but something closer to an ancient survival mechanism. Here we consider why immobility's clotting risk seems to shift over time, look at an HSP47-blocking compound called Col003 that is already showing promise, and ask whether HSP47 might eventually double as a marker of clotting and inflammatory risk more broadly.

Abstract

MASLD is the most prevalent chronic liver disease and a significant cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality.
