Bifunctional Pt/CeO2 Nanozyme-Mediated Metabolic Regulation Promotes NAD+ Regeneration and ROS Clearance to Ameliorate Age-Related Osteoporosis.

ACS Appl Mater Interfaces · Zhang H, Yang C, Zhou Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Age-related osteoporosis is characterized by progressive loss of bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, driven by chronic inflammation, redox imbalance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and osteoblast senescence that uncouples bone formation from resorption. During aging, declining nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) availability further exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and stress responses, suggesting that restoring NAD+ redox homeostasis could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Here, we reported a bifunctional nanozyme-mediated metabolic regulation strategy to promote NAD+ regeneration and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) for the treatment of age-related osteoporosis. The bifunctional nanozyme (Pt/CeO2) was constructed by in situ growth of platinum nanoparticles on cerium oxide nanorods and was designed for ROS scavenging and NAD+ regeneration via NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) oxidation. In senescent osteoblasts, Pt/CeO2 reduced ROS, restored mitochondrial integrity, and alleviated senescence, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation. In vivo results confirmed that systemic administration of Pt/CeO2 preserved trabecular architecture and increased bone mineral density in naturally aged mice. These findings demonstrated that coupling ROS scavenging with the restoration of NAD+ redox homeostasis via Pt/CeO2 nanozyme provided a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract osteoblast senescence and slow the progression of age-related osteoporosis.

Dual-layer regulation of macrophage grancalcin links diabetic redox-metabolic imbalance to impaired vascularized bone regeneration.

Redox Biol · Jin S, Song Z, Fang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Effective bone regeneration requires the timely transition of immune cells from inflammatory defense to regenerative support. However, how diabetic redox-metabolic stress sustains maladaptive immune states and drives regenerative failure remains incompletely understood. Herein, using a mouse jawbone defect model combined with single-cell RNA sequencing, metabolic assays and human peri-extraction gingival specimens, we identified a senescence-associated macrophage state characterized by elevated grancalcin (GCA). Functionally, recombinant GCA impaired osteogenic and endothelial responses, whereas GCA neutralization partially reversed the inhibitory effects of diabetic macrophage-conditioned medium, supporting extracellular GCA as a functional mediator of regenerative dysfunction. Mechanistically, diabetic macrophages exhibited enhanced glycolytic activity, impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and a reduced NAD+/NADH ratio. This redox imbalance lowered SIRT6 abundance, increased H3K9ac/H3K56ac enrichment at the accessible Gca regulatory locus and sustained Gca transcription. Meanwhile, hyperglycemia-triggered Ca2+ signaling promoted GCA recruitment to membrane-proximal regions and increased both total extracellular and extracellular vesicle-associated GCA. Local delivery of quercetin using a β-cyclodextrin/GelMA hydrogel attenuated macrophage GCA dysregulation, reduced senescence-associated macrophage features and improved vascularized jawbone regeneration. Collectively, this work defines a dual-layer paradigm controlling extracellular GCA bioavailability, thereby linking diabetic redox-metabolic perturbation to defective bone regeneration and supporting local targeting of macrophage GCA for diabetic bone-healing therapy.

Neurovascular Unit Redox-Metabolic Communication in Poststroke Cognitive Impairment: Spatiotemporal Dysregulation, Cellular Crosstalk, and Precision Intervention.

ReviewEur J Neurosci · Zhang Q, Chen Y, Bai M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is a dynamic syndrome driven by focal brain injury, pre-existing cerebrovascular burden, metabolic comorbidities, and reduced brain resilience. Increasing evidence suggests that persistent disruption of redox-metabolic communication within the neurovascular unit (NVU), rather than neuronal loss alone, promotes the transition from acute ischemic injury to chronic cognitive decline. This review summarizes metabolic reprogramming in BMECs, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, and neurons, emphasizing the intercellular transfer of metabolites, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondria. We propose that PSCI develops through a spatiotemporally dysregulated continuum involving vascular injury, cell-specific metabolic remodeling, maladaptive signal propagation, impaired axonal and synaptic energy support, and progressive network disconnection. Ischemia-reperfusion triggers bioenergetic failure, NADH/NAD+ imbalance, mitochondrial electron leakage, endothelial dysfunction, and capillary no-reflow. Subsequently, blood-brain barrier disruption, immunometabolic activation, iron and lipid dysregulation, and defective organelle quality control sustain white matter and synaptic injury. Therapeutic strategies should therefore be stage-specific: restoring microcirculatory perfusion and physiological redox signaling acutely, promoting inflammation resolution and metabolic repair subacutely, and combining vascular-metabolic modulation with neurorehabilitation chronically. Future studies should integrate cell type-specific interventions, metabolic flux tracing, spatial multi-omics, and longitudinal cognitive assessments to establish causal donor-carrier-recipient-stage-outcome relationships.

Renal tubule-targeted biomimetic nanodrugs achieve precise AKI treatment by halting pro-inflammatory crosstalk between proximal tubular epithelial cells and macrophages.

J Nanobiotechnology · Yang P, Zhao L, Yang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a critical clinical disorder characterized by high incidence, high mortality, and numerous complications. However, current supportive therapies cannot reverse established injury, leading to poor outcomes in some patients, and thus, necessitating the development of active therapeutic strategies. Here, we analyzed a clinical single-nucleus RNA sequencing dataset and found that a pathological feedback loop between injured proximal tubular epithelial cells (PTECs) and macrophages drives AKI progression. To simultaneously target this maladaptive crosstalk, we developed N@CM2c, a biomimetic nanodrug composed of NAD⁺ and CCR2-enriched M2c macrophage membranes. The nanoparticle actively targets injured tubules via CCL2 chemotaxis. N@CM2c replenishes the NAD+ pool in PTECs and upregulates HAO2 expression to improve fatty acid metabolism, thereby promoting mitochondrial repair while attenuating the inflammatory cascade, which in turn reduces cytokine-mediated macrophage recruitment and pro-inflammatory induction. Concurrently, the CM2c membrane shell and NAD+ jointly promote polarization of macrophages towards a reparative phenotype. In AKI mice, this dual-targeting strategy synergistically terminated the inflammatory feedback loop, restored renal function, and reversed pathological damage. By integrating targeted delivery, metabolic reprogramming, and immunomodulation, N@CM2c presents a promising application to halt AKI progression and promote renal repair.

Novel Proteolyzed Aortic Extracellular Matrix Substrate Coating as a Model to Assess Aneurysmal Smooth Muscle Cell Behavior In Vitro.

New todayTissue Eng Part A · Krajewski T, Ramamurthi A · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAAs) are localized expansions of the abdominal aortic wall that grow slowly to potentially fatal rupture. AAAs result from irreversible loss of structural extracellular matrix (ECM) due to upregulated proteolytic activity. Surgery to treat AAAs is risky; therefore, new drug-based approaches are needed to slow aneurysm growth by reversing ECM pathophysiology. This necessitates the study of disease-relevant cells in vitro, in this case smooth muscle cells (SMCs); however, current in vitro models are limited due to SMC dedifferentiation on conventional plastic substrates, which cause a loss of disease-relevant features. This study generated a physiologically relevant culture model to assess matrix regenerative and reparative responses of AAA SMCs. We developed a surface coating of ECM from digested aortic wall tissue that mimics the AAA microenvironment and maintains the diseased phenotype of AAA SMCs in vitro. When cultured on ECM substrates, late passage SMCs (EaRASMCs) from a rat elastase injury AAA model retained spindle-shaped morphology, gene expression indicative of a synthetic phenotype, and increased chemoattractant expression. These behaviors are more evocative of subprimary aneurysmal cells compared with traditionally cultured SMCs, demonstrating the capability of our native ECM substrate to modulate and promote pathophysiologically relevant SMC phenotypes in vitro. This model system offers a promising platform for screening aneurysmal SMC responses to therapeutics similar to expected in vivo outcomes.

AMOTL2: A key regulator of mechanotransduction in development, vascular disease and cancer.

ReviewCells Dev · Fonseca P, Zhang Y, Holmgren L · Full text
Abstract

Mechanical forces play a fundamental role in regulating cell behavior during development, tissue homeostasis, and disease. The angiomotin family protein AmotL2 has emerged as a central integrator of mechanotransduction, linking cell-cell adhesion, cytoskeletal tension, and nuclear architecture to transcriptional control. Through its ability to connect cadherin-based junctions to radial actin filaments and the nuclear lamina, AmotL2 establishes a continuous mechanical axis that enables cells to sense and respond to extracellular forces. This function is essential for epithelial organization, vascular morphogenesis, and endothelial responses to shear stress, in part through regulation of Hippo pathway effectors such as YAP and TAZ. Dysregulation of AmotL2 contributes to pathological states, including abdominal aortic aneurysm, where reduced AmotL2 impairs endothelial mechanotransduction, and cancer, where induction of the p60AmotL2 isoform disrupts polarity, alters nuclear mechanics, and promotes invasive behavior. Emerging evidence further suggests that AmotL2 regulates transcription through direct effects on nuclear structure and chromatin accessibility, positioning it as a key regulator of force-dependent gene expression. In this review, we summarize the structural and mechanistic basis of AmotL2 function and discuss its roles in development, vascular biology, and cancer, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target in diseases driven by altered mechanotransduction.

Developing structure and strategies for analysis of circulating biomarkers to detect and monitor abdominal aortic aneurysms.

Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol · Lindsey ML, Shivam P, Nde PN et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a multifactorial disease characterized by progressive remodeling of the aortic wall. Current clinical management includes screening ultrasound for diagnosis, and routine aortic imaging to monitor growth in patients with small AAA and surgical exclusion of larger AAA. With the evolution of a diverse array of multianalyte assessments from peripheral blood, comprehensive assessments of circulating biomarkers in patients with AAA is possible. In the context of this dynamic disease process, circulating markers - paired with clinical and imaging data - provide an opportunity to identify specific active biologic processes that influence the natural history of the disease. We propose a disease-specific framework for understanding relationships between circulating markers and imaging within a novel three class structure. Class I associations correlate with aortic size and presence of AAA and may be related to the mechanisms of initial formation. Class II associations track with change in aneurysm MTD and may be related to mechanisms of AAA growth and rupture risk. Class II may be clinically relevant for the identification of targets for modification that could alter the natural history of established AAA. Class III associations relate to static or dynamic morphologic features such as mural thrombus or calcifications that have less well-defined associations with clinical outcomes. Identified and appropriately classified markers can provide specific avenues for novel therapeutic and diagnostic options to reduce morbidity and mortality in patients with AAA.

Aortic aneurysm (AA) is a life-threatening vascular disease for which no pharmacological therapy has been proven to effectively limit disease progression. Haemostasis in Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease.

New todayReviewLiver Int · Ferdinande K, Senzolo M, Campello E et al. · Full text
Abstract

MASLD is the most prevalent chronic liver disease and a significant cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality. Portal hypertension may develop before advanced fibrosis, driven by vascular and metabolic alterations that concurrently affect systemic haemostasis and promote intrahepatic interactions between endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and haemostatic pathways. Once cirrhosis is established, the rebalanced haemostatic state is largely independent of aetiology, with impaired fibrinolysis representing the principal MASLD-associated difference. In non-cirrhotic MASLD, increasing in vivo platelet activation, a progressive procoagulant imbalance between factor VIII and protein C, and reduced fibrinolysis, including increased plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 and prolonged clot lysis, have been described, although their attribution to MASLD is complicated by the concomitant metabolic factors. Clinically, MASLD is associated with increased risks of venous thromboembolism, portal vein thrombosis, and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, increasing with disease severity; however, prospective data linking specific haemostatic alterations to incident thrombosis remain lacking. Within the liver, platelets act as central hubs interacting with dysfunctional liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, hepatic stellate cells, Kupffer cells, and hepatocytes, orchestrating platelet-neutrophil crosstalk, neutrophil extracellular trap formation, and coagulation activation, thereby promoting immunothrombosis, hepatic stellate cell activation, microvascular remodelling, and fibrogenesis. Coagulation proteases may additionally contribute to MASLD progression through protease-activated receptor signalling in hepatocytes and hepatic stellate cells. These observations raise the hypothesis that antiplatelet and anticoagulant therapies could modify MASLD progression, although evidence remains predominantly preclinical and observational. This review synthesizes haemostatic balance and thrombotic complications in MASLD, the endothelial-inflammation-haemostasis axis in MASLD progression, and emerging perspectives.

Machine Learning Versus Traditional Methods for Predicting Venous Thromboembolism After Surgery: A Systematic Review and Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis.

ReviewANZ J Surg · Chen S, Dhaliwal S, Papas C et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Postoperative venous thromboembolism remains a major cause of preventable morbidity after surgery, and established risk scores may perform inconsistently across surgical populations. METHODS: MEDLINE, Embase and the Cochrane Library were searched for studies comparing machine learning with traditional risk models for postoperative venous thromboembolism prediction. Risk-of-bias was assessed with PROBAST + AI. The primary outcome was discrimination measured by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. Bayesian random-effects network meta-analysis estimated mean differences versus Caprini with 95% credible intervals. Best and worst-version analyses explored the impact of within-study multiplicity, with subgroup analyses by cancer surgery and procedure type. RESULTS: Eighteen studies including 177 656 validation patients were included. Meta-ensembles showed the largest improvement compared with Caprini in the best-version analysis (mean difference in AUROC, 0.232; 95% credible interval, 0.129-0.334), with a similar finding in the worst-version analysis (0.237; 0.123-0.351). Several other machine learning classes also had higher AUROC estimates than Caprini, whereas most traditional scores and surgeon judgement did not. Geneva showed lower performance than Caprini. Meta-ensembles ranked first overall, while Caprini and Geneva ranked 14th and last, respectively. Meta-ensembles and boosting models had higher AUROC than Caprini in both cancer surgery analyses; in general surgery, no model class showed higher performance than Caprini in the worst-version analysis. CONCLUSIONS: Machine learning models, particularly meta-ensembles, showed higher reported discrimination than traditional scores, but evidence remains limited by heterogeneity, inconsistent calibration reporting, sparse decision-analytic evaluation and infrequent external validation. Clinical adoption requires external validation and demonstration of real-world net benefit.

Transcatheter Thrombus Removal With the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System in Children With Congenital Heart Disease.

Case reportCatheter Cardiovasc Interv · Balachandran AN, Hill KD, Chamberlain RC et al. · Full text
Abstract

Thromboembolic complications in children with congenital heart disease (CHD) carry significant morbidity and mortality, particularly in patients with single ventricle physiology. Current management strategies, including systemic anticoagulation, thrombolytics, and surgical thrombectomy, each carry substantial risks in this population. Transcatheter aspiration thrombectomy represents an emerging alternative. We retrospectively reviewed all pediatric patients with CHD at our institution who underwent transcatheter aspiration thrombectomy using the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System between January 2024 and February 2025. Patient demographics, cardiac diagnosis, thrombus location, procedural details, and clinical outcomes were recorded. Five patients (age 5 months to 9 years; weight 6.3-25.9 kg) with diverse CHD diagnoses underwent six thrombectomy procedures. Thrombus locations included the Fontan circuit, inferior vena cava, pulmonary arteries, and internal jugular veins. Technical success with angiographic restoration of antegrade flow and no residual gradient across the segment of interest, was achieved in all cases (100%). Three patients were discharged home with confirmed vessel patency on follow-up imaging (echocardiography and/or vascular ultrasound at 4-6 months post intervention). Two patients, both on extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support prior to the procedure, subsequently died from causes unrelated to the thrombectomy intervention. In this limited case series, the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System demonstrated feasibility for transcatheter thrombus removal in pediatric patients across a spectrum of CHD. Given the limited number of available catheter systems in this population, this approach may be considered as a first-line or adjunct therapy in select patients when conventional medical management is insufficient or contraindicated. Further prospective studies are needed to define optimal patient selection, procedural techniques, and post-procedural management.

Variation in Inferior Vena Cava Filter Retrieval Across U.S. Veterans Health Administration Facilities.

J Vasc Interv Radiol · Trivedi P, Wells K, Puello Baron M et al. · Full text
Abstract

PURPOSE: Inferior vena cava (IVC) filters prevent pulmonary embolism (PE) in patients unable to receive anticoagulation but require prompt retrieval once protection is no longer needed. Despite national safety advisories and multidisciplinary guidelines, IVC filter retrieval remains low in the United States. How much variation underlies low retrieval across Veterans Affairs (VA) facilities has not been studied. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This retrospective longitudinal cohort study included Veterans who underwent IVC filter implantation at VA facilities between 2016 and 2023. Veterans who died within 90 days of implantation or lacked 1 year of follow-up were excluded. IVC filter implantation and retrieval were identified using procedure codes in the VA Corporate Data Warehouse. A multivariable Bayesian mixed-effects model estimated risk-adjusted retrieval rates across facilities. Logistic regression was performed to identify specific patient- and facility-level factors associated with non-retrieval. RESULTS: Among 3,670 Veterans (median age 69 years; 95.9% male), retrieval occurred in 10.5% within 90 days, 22.2% within 1 year, and 24.7% overall. Risk-adjusted 1-year retrieval varied widely across facilities: 0-60%. Older veteran age (> 75 years), facility academic affiliation, and Southern census region were associated with lower retrieval, whereas implantation at higher-complexity facilities and pre-COVID years were associated with higher retrieval. CONCLUSIONS: IVC filter retrieval rates among Veterans remain low but exceed those reported in non-VA Medicare cohorts. Substantial inter-facility variation highlights opportunities to standardize structured device surveillance, with clear responsibility for follow-up.

RESULTS.—: YOLO-PLT distinguished independent PLTs and aggregates. Non-aggregated samples showed a linear gradient (Pearson r = 0.863), whereas EDTA-PTCP samples displayed nonlinear tail aggregation. CONCLUSIONS.—: PLT-pro2.0 provides a rapid, objective, and accurate WSI-based correction strategy for EDTA-PTCP, particularly for samples difficult to correct using conventional methods.

Residual Pure Intralymphatic Breast Carcinoma After Neoadjuvant Therapy: Clinicopathologic Analysis and Comparative Outcomes Across Published Studies.

Arch Pathol Lab Med · Bahmad HF, Brachtel EF, Jorda M et al. · Full text
Abstract

CONTEXT.—: Residual pure intralymphatic carcinoma (PIC), defined as residual tumor confined exclusively to lymphovascular spaces without stromal invasion after neoadjuvant therapy (NAT), is a rare, residual disease pattern. No standardized staging, pathology reporting protocol, or consensus classification exists within current frameworks. OBJECTIVE.—: To characterize the clinicopathologic features, post-NAT staging, Residual Cancer Burden (RCB) classification, and outcomes of patients with PIC at our institution, and to perform a pooled analysis of all published cohorts. DESIGN.—: This is a retrospective cohort study of consecutive patients with breast cancer treated with NAT and surgery at a large academic medical center from January 2015 to September 2025. Identified PIC cases were systematically characterized and compared against 5 previously published cohorts. RESULTS.—: Of 2000 patients screened, 13 (0.65%) had PIC (9 of 13 [69%] grade 3; 7 of 13 [54%] human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 [HER2]-positive). All had ypT0 disease (yp denotes pathologic stage after neoadjuvant therapy) on American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging, while 8 of 13 node-negative patients (62%) may have an RCB score of RCB-I or higher when intralymphatic tumor dimensions are entered-a framework mismatch rather than true pathologic complete response (pCR). At median follow-up of 59.2 months, overall survival was 100% (13 of 13) and disease-free survival, was 92% (12 of 13). Pooled analysis (N = 60) showed mortality decline from 83% (5 of 6) in the earliest (pre-2018) cohort to 0% to 20% post-2018. CONCLUSIONS.—: Current staging frameworks may misclassify PIC as pCR, implying incomplete standardization with potential clinical consequences. We propose an AJCC modifier, ypT0(LVI+), for residual lymphovascular invasion in the absence of residual in situ carcinoma or nodal metastasis, plus a dedicated College of American Pathologists synoptic field to ensure appropriate management.
