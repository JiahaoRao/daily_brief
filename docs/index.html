Targeted metabolic reprogramming of CD8 T cells restores immune regulation in lupus.

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A · Oba S, Humbel MH, Lancia HH et al. · Full text
Abstract

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is characterized by pathogenic humoral autoimmunity, yet the mechanisms linking T cell dysfunction to aberrant B cell responses remain poorly defined. Here, we identify a CD8+ T cell-intrinsic metabolic defect that promotes humoral immune dysregulation in lupus. CD8+ T cells from patients with SLE exhibited elevated CD38 expression associated with reduced intracellular NAD+ levels, mitochondrial dysfunction, and impaired cytotoxic capacity. Pharmacologic inhibition of CD38 with the small-molecule inhibitor MK-0159 restored NAD+ metabolism and rescued cytotoxic function in human CD8+ T cells ex vivo. To selectively target this pathway in vivo, we developed anti-CD8 antibody-conjugated nanoparticles encapsulating MK-0159. In lupus-prone MRL/lpr mice, CD8-targeted delivery enhanced granzyme B expression, improved degranulation, reduced plasmablast responses and autoantibody production, immune complex deposition, and renal pathology. Importantly, targeted delivery restored CD8+ T cell function without broad immune perturbation. These findings identify CD38-driven NAD+ depletion as a reversible immunometabolic checkpoint limiting CD8+ T cell cytotoxic competence and support a model in which targeted restoration of cytotoxic fitness contributes to the control of humoral autoimmunity in SLE.

PMMA nanoplastics induce neurotoxicity in Caenorhabditis elegans associated with disruption of nicotinamide metabolism.

New todayEcotoxicol Environ Saf · Lei F, He X, Wang Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

The escalating contamination by poly(methyl methacrylate) nanoplastics (PMMA-NPs) raises urgent concerns for nervous system health, yet their neurotoxic mechanisms remain poorly understood. Here, we employed an integrated multi-omics approach in Caenorhabditis elegans combined with phenotypic assessments spanning high concentrations (0.1-10 mg/L) and low concentrations (250 ng/L and 25 ng/L) that fall within or near the lower range of concentrations reported or considered plausible for nanoplastic contamination, continuous multigenerational exposure (F0-F3), and targeted nicotinamide intervention to dissect PMMA-NP neurotoxicity. High-concentration exposure induced dose-dependently induced dopaminergic neuronal dysfunction, oxidative stress, mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt) activation, and behavioral deficits. Remarkably, low concentrations likewise caused significant dopaminergic neuronal dysfunction and oxidative stress, whereas mitochondrial UPRmt was not significantly activated at these concentrations. Untargeted metabolomics and transcriptomics revealed that nicotinate and nicotinamide metabolism, the central pathway for NAD⁺/NADP⁺ cofactor synthesis, was the most profoundly dysregulated pathway. Transcriptional upregulation of NADPH‑consuming cytochrome P450 detoxification, coupled with metabolic depletion of the NAD⁺ biosynthetic pathway, is consistent with a self‑defeating metabolic bottleneck that may drive neuronal bioenergetic crisis. Continuous multigenerational exposure to 1 mg/L, 250 ng/L, and 25 ng/L polymethyl methacrylate nanoparticles (PMMA‑NPs) induces persistent neurobehavioral toxicity accompanied by notable effect dissociation. Notably, 250 ng/L PMMA impaired cognition without sustained UPRmt activation, which implies separate regulatory pathways. Supplementation with nicotinamide (NA) effectively alleviated PMMA‑induced oxidative stress and motor deficits but failed to rescue cognitive impairment, supporting the conclusion that learning and memory damage is largely independent of oxidative stress. Our findings identify NAD⁺ metabolism disruption as a key mechanism contributing to PMMA‑NPs neurotoxicity, reveal a dissociation between motor and cognitive toxic pathways, and highlight the potential for persistent cognitive hazards across generations under continuous environmental exposure. Mechanistically, our conclusions regarding NAD⁺/NADH metabolic disruption are derived from multi-omics associations and pharmacological intervention rather than from direct measurement of NAD⁺/NADH flux.

Bifunctional Pt/CeO2 Nanozyme-Mediated Metabolic Regulation Promotes NAD+ Regeneration and ROS Clearance to Ameliorate Age-Related Osteoporosis.

ACS Appl Mater Interfaces · Zhang H, Yang C, Zhou Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Age-related osteoporosis is characterized by progressive loss of bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, driven by chronic inflammation, redox imbalance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and osteoblast senescence that uncouples bone formation from resorption. During aging, declining nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) availability further exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and stress responses, suggesting that restoring NAD+ redox homeostasis could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Here, we reported a bifunctional nanozyme-mediated metabolic regulation strategy to promote NAD+ regeneration and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) for the treatment of age-related osteoporosis. The bifunctional nanozyme (Pt/CeO2) was constructed by in situ growth of platinum nanoparticles on cerium oxide nanorods and was designed for ROS scavenging and NAD+ regeneration via NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) oxidation. In senescent osteoblasts, Pt/CeO2 reduced ROS, restored mitochondrial integrity, and alleviated senescence, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation. In vivo results confirmed that systemic administration of Pt/CeO2 preserved trabecular architecture and increased bone mineral density in naturally aged mice. These findings demonstrated that coupling ROS scavenging with the restoration of NAD+ redox homeostasis via Pt/CeO2 nanozyme provided a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract osteoblast senescence and slow the progression of age-related osteoporosis.

Abstract

Circulating damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) are associated with injury and adverse outcomes in sepsis, but their concentrations do not identify their cellular origins or rates of release. Abstract

Effective bone regeneration requires the timely transition of immune cells from inflammatory defense to regenerative support. Reactive Oxygen Species After Myocardial Infarction: From Pathophysiological Mechanisms to Prognostic and Therapeutic Implications.

New todayReviewAntioxidants (Basel) · Țocu L, Ștefănescu BI, Mihailov R et al. · Full text
Abstract

Myocardial infarction (MI) remains a major cause of death and heart failure despite reperfusion. Reopening an occluded coronary artery salvages viable myocardium but creates an ischemia-reperfusion environment in which reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS) can contribute to injury. Postinfarction redox responses continue through inflammation, repair, and remodeling. Their effects depend on species, concentration, source, compartment, timing, molecular targets, and local antioxidant capacity; controlled redox signaling is essential for normal vascular, metabolic, and immune function. This narrative review focuses primarily on type 1 MI, particularly reperfused ST-segment elevation MI. It examines mitochondrial and enzymatic oxidant sources, including nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidases, xanthine oxidoreductase, nitric oxide synthases, and myeloperoxidase, alongside secondary lipid- and nitrogen-derived products. Broad antioxidant strategies have not yielded consistent clinical benefit. More precise therapeutic approaches require source- and compartment-specific targeting, appropriate timing and patient selection, reliable measures of target engagement, and benefit beyond contemporary MI care.

ReviewEur J Neurosci · Zhang Q, Chen Y, Bai M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is a dynamic syndrome driven by focal brain injury, pre-existing cerebrovascular burden, metabolic comorbidities, and reduced brain resilience. Increasing evidence suggests that persistent disruption of redox-metabolic communication within the neurovascular unit (NVU), rather than neuronal loss alone, promotes the transition from acute ischemic injury to chronic cognitive decline. This review summarizes metabolic reprogramming in BMECs, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, and neurons, emphasizing the intercellular transfer of metabolites, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondria. Epigenetic remodeling in pulmonary arterial hypertension: metabolic regulation and therapeutic opportunities.

New todayReviewClin Epigenetics · Kucheriava M, Hadri L, Bisserier M · Full text
Abstract

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive pulmonary vasculopathy characterized by endothelial dysfunction, smooth muscle cell proliferation, vascular remodeling, and right ventricular pressure overload. Although contemporary therapies improve symptoms, hemodynamics, and clinical outcomes, they do not fully reverse the persistent pulmonary vascular cell phenotypes that sustain disease progression. Disease-associated vascular cell phenotypes can persist beyond the immediate signaling environment, indicating that mechanisms capable of stabilizing abnormal cell states contribute to PAH. These include regulatory reprogramming, sustained signaling, and the selection or expansion of abnormal cell populations. Epigenetic remodeling provides a framework for understanding this biology. DNA methylation, histone acetylation, histone methylation, chromatin accessibility, and acetyl-lysine reader pathways have all been implicated in PAH pathogenesis. Lactate is also emerging as a candidate interface between glycolytic remodeling and chromatin regulation through histone lactylation, and RNA N6-methyladenosine constitutes a parallel epitranscriptomic layer governed by the same pool of metabolites. This review focuses on PAH, with an emphasis on pulmonary artery endothelial and smooth muscle vascular cells. We first define the pulmonary vascular and transcriptional context in which persistent disease phenotypes develop and set out the evidence required to distinguish metabolic-epigenetic coupling from durable cellular memory. We summarize the core epigenetic mechanisms relevant to PAH and discuss the specific epigenetic abnormalities reported in pulmonary vascular cells. Finally, we examine how metabolic dysfunction shapes the epigenetic landscape, consider the therapeutic implications, and outline the priorities needed to move the field toward translational precision.

Long COVID, chronic fatigue syndrome, and chronic lyme disease as Post-acute Infection Syndromes (PAIS): a translational synthesis.

ReviewJ Transl Med · Sewell PE, Reddy R · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) comprise a heterogeneous group of chronic, multisystem conditions characterized by persistent or relapsing symptoms following resolution of an acute infectious illness, in the absence of ongoing pathogen replication detectable by standard diagnostics. Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), Long COVID, post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, and related entities are increasingly recognized as part of a shared post-infectious disease spectrum. The COVID-19 pandemic has markedly expanded the global burden of PAIS and intensified investigation into their biological underpinnings. Integrating molecular insights with pragmatic clinical frameworks may accelerate development of precision-oriented diagnostic and therapeutic strategies to address the growing global impact of post-infectious chronic illness.

Reintervention after endovascular infrarenal aortic aneurysm repair: Outcomes and implications for long-term success.

New todayReviewSemin Vasc Surg · Chen P, Zettervall SL · Full text
Abstract

Endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) has become the dominant strategy for the treatment of anatomically suitable infrarenal aortoiliac aneurysms, offering early survival benefits and decreased perioperative morbidity compared with open repair. However, long-term durability remains a defining limitation, as evidenced by landmark trials demonstrating attrition of the early survival advantage and significant rates of reintervention. Reintervention after EVAR is a predictable feature of the natural history of endovascular repair, with important clinical, prognostic, and economic implications. Approximately 20% to 30% of patients require reintervention within a decade of repair, with proximal and distal seal failure, type 2 and type 3 endoleaks, and graft infection representing mechanisms of late failure. Risk factors for reintervention include hostile neck anatomy, off-instructions-for-use repair, female sex, and failure of sac regression. Society guidelines provide treatment frameworks, although thresholds for intervention and surveillance recommendations continue to evolve. Improvements in device technology, refined patient selection, and operator experience have reduced reintervention rates in contemporary cohorts, although lifelong surveillance remains essential. Future directions include expanded application of fenestrated and branched endovascular repair for late failure.
