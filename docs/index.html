Prospects for adiponectin-based therapeutics in myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury.

ReviewPharmacol Ther · Bahit MC, Gibson CM, Lei Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Adiponectin is an adipocyte-derived hormone with broad cardiometabolic benefits, circulating at high levels in healthy individuals but reduced in obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Low adiponectin concentrations are strongly associated with coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI), and heart failure. MIRI remains a major unmet need in contemporary ST-segment-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) care. Although reperfusion is essential in limiting ischemic myocardial necrosis, it causes deleterious effects such as impaired carbohydrate oxidation, excessive fatty-acid oxidation, intracellular acidosis, calcium (Ca2+) overload, oxidative/nitrative stress, and mitochondrial fragmentation, that expand infarct size, worsen microvascular obstruction, and fuel adverse ventricular remodeling. Activation of the adiponectin-adiponectin receptor (AdipoR) axis directly counteracts these processes by stimulating adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-dependent metabolic rebalancing, restoring mitochondrial fusion-fission homeostasis, enhancing oxidative capacity, reducing inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS-) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase-mediated injury, improving endothelial nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability, and enhancing microvascular perfusion. In animal models, adiponectin or its mimetics consistently reduced infarct size, improved myocardial function, and attenuated apoptosis, inflammation, and no-reflow. A growing array of therapeutic strategies has emerged, each bringing its own pharmacologic nuances and implications for translation to the clinic. This review summarizes current strategies to enhance adiponectin action, highlights the progress in peptide and small-molecule mimetics, and discusses future directions for clinical translation in cardiometabolic diseases.

Smpd2 m6A modification contributes to hypoxic pulmonary hypertension via an alveolar epithelial cell senescence-associated phenotype.

New todayTissue Cell · Jiang J, Xu J, Wu T et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Pulmonary vascular remodelling induced by the abnormal proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) is a core pathological feature of hypoxic pulmonary hypertension (HPH), but its exact pathogenesis remains poorly understood. This study aimed to elucidate the functional role and intrinsic regulatory mechanisms of an alveolar epithelial cell (AEC) senescence-associated phenotype in the pathogenesis of HPH. METHODS: A 4-week hypoxia-induced HPH rat model and an in vitro hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype model were established. The effects of hypoxia on AECs were analysed by evaluating the senescence-associated phenotype and mitochondrial function. In addition, a Transwell coculture system and AEC-conditioned medium were used to evaluate the effects of hypoxia-exposed AECs with a senescence-associated phenotype on PASMC proliferation. The key signalling pathways associated with the hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype were identified through transcriptome sequencing and subsequent KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. The roles of the Smpd2/ceramide axis in the hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype, PASMC proliferation and HPH progression were explored via the administration of METTL3- and Smpd2-targeting siRNAs, AAV6-shSmpd2 for lung-directed Smpd2 silencing in vivo, the neutral sphingomyelinase (nSMase) inhibitor GW4869, the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant Mito-TEMPO (MT) and the NAD⁺ precursor β-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN). Finally, MeRIP-qPCR, RIP-qPCR, mRNA stability assays, and dual-luciferase assays were employed to elucidate the mechanisms of m6A RNA methylation in AECs with the senescence-associated phenotype. RESULTS: Chronic hypoxia induced typical manifestations of HPH in rats, accompanied by a more pronounced AEC senescence-associated phenotype. In cultured human AECs, hypoxia upregulated METTL3, which increased Smpd2 mRNA stability through m6A modification recognized by IGF2BP2. These changes were associated with ceramide accumulation and mitochondrial dysfunction and contributed to the AEC senescence-associated phenotype in vitro. AECs with this phenotype released soluble and/or vesicular paracrine signals that were associated with abnormal PASMC proliferation, contributing to vascular remodelling and right ventricular hypertrophy. In cultured human AECs, NMN pretreatment, Smpd2 knockdown, and METTL3 knockdown attenuated the hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype. In hypoxia-exposed rats, NMN supplementation, lung-directed Smpd2 silencing, and pharmacological nSMase inhibition with GW4869 attenuated the AEC senescence-associated phenotype and alleviated HPH progression. CONCLUSIONS: This study provides preclinical evidence that, in hypoxia-exposed rats and cultured human AECs/PASMCs, a hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype may contribute to PASMC proliferation and HPH development. Mechanistically, in cultured human AECs, hypoxia exposure increased METTL3-mediated m6A modification of Smpd2 mRNA, which increased its stability in an m6A- and IGF2BP2-dependent manner and upregulated Smpd2 expression. The resulting increase in ceramide accumulation was associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and the senescence-associated phenotype in AECs. In this preclinical setting, AECs with this phenotype may further contribute to PASMC proliferation and HPH development. The findings of this study provide preclinical data supporting a candidate mechanism in HPH pathogenesis and identify candidate targets for future validation in human PH and cell-specific in vivo models.

Integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing identify emodin as a candidate modulator of STAT3 signaling in abdominal aortic aneurysm.

Vascul Pharmacol · Liu Y, Xu Z, Deng L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease lacking effective pharmacological therapies. To identify potential therapeutic targets and candidate compounds, we performed integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing by combining Mendelian randomization (MR), summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR), and Bayesian colocalization. This framework prioritized multiple druggable candidates, among which STAT3 emerged as a key regulatory node. Subsequent functional studies demonstrated that emodin suppressed vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation, migration, phenotypic switching, and inflammatory responses. These effects were accompanied by reduced STAT3 phosphorylation and nuclear localization. Molecular docking suggested a potential binding mode of emodin within the STAT3 SH2 domain, while cellular thermal shift assays demonstrated thermal stabilization of STAT3 by emodin in intact cells, consistent with target engagement. Notably, emodin and the selective STAT3 inhibitor STAT3-IN-13 produced comparable effects, and co-treatment did not yield additive efficacy, suggesting overlapping mechanisms. In vivo, emodin attenuated aneurysm development and reduced vascular inflammation in experimental models. Collectively, these findings identify emodin as a candidate modulator of AAA progression acting through suppression of STAT3-associated signaling pathways, and demonstrate the potential of genetic evidence-guided drug repurposing strategies in complex vascular diseases.

Dickkopf-3 (DKK3) in cardiovascular diseases: from a stress-responsive biomarker to a potential therapeutic target.

New todayReviewFront Pharmacol · Fu X, Dai J, Wang X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Dickkopf-3 (DKK3) is a secreted glycoprotein that functions as a context-dependent modulator of the Wnt/β-catenin and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling pathways. Originally studied in development and cancer, DKK3 has recently emerged as a key stress-responsive factor. It is released by epithelial and other cell types under pathological strain and actively participates in fibrotic, inflammatory, and remodeling processes across various organ systems. In cardiovascular medicine, DKK3 is gaining recognition not only as a promising biomarker for renal and vascular injury-conditions closely intertwined with cardiovascular outcomes-but also as a direct mediator in the pathogenesis of specific cardiovascular conditions such as abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) and heart failure. This review synthesizes current knowledge on DKK3, beginning with its fundamental biology and established roles in renal, pulmonary, and dermal fibrosis, which provide crucial mechanistic parallels. We then focus on the evolving evidence of DKK3's involvement in cardiovascular diseases, critically evaluating its biomarker potential for risk stratification in conditions like diabetic kidney disease and its direct pathophysiological roles in vascular smooth muscle cell phenotype switching, myocardial remodeling, and aneurysm formation. We delve into specific molecular mechanisms, including interactions with the ATF6-TGFβ3-Smad2/3 axis and low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1 (LRP1), as revealed by preclinical models. Finally, we explore the therapeutic implications of targeting the DKK3 axis, discuss the challenges posed by its context-dependent dual roles, and outline future research directions necessary to translate these discoveries into clinical applications for cardiovascular patients.

Undiseased and diseased abdominal aortas contain environmentally relevant micronanoplastics.

New todayJVS Vasc Sci · Massie PL, Garcia MA, Gallego DF et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Plastics production has accelerated, with most products ultimately residing in the environment. Plastics degrade into micro- (<5 mm) and nano- (<1000 nm) plastic particles (MNPs) that can be ingested or inhaled by humans. In the literature, MNPs have been found in higher concentrations in diseased arteries, but the mechanisms remain unclear. Studies have found MNPs in tissue from ascending aortic dissections, but data on infrarenal aortic aneurysms, occlusive plaques, and cadaveric aortas have not been reported. METHODS: MNPs from aortic aneurysmal walls, aortic occlusive plaques, and fresh cadaveric aortas were measured using pyrolysis-gas chromatography/mass spectroscopy; the MNPs assessed included polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), polystyrene, acrylonitrile butadiene styrene, styrene-butadiene (SBR), poly(methyl methacrylate), polycarbonate, polyvinylchloride (PVC), polyurethane, polyethylene terephthalate, nylon 6, and nylon 66. Scanning electron microscopy/energy dispersive X ray spectroscopy was used to visualize MNPs and characterize the milieu of a single aortic plaque for hypothesis generation. RESULTS: Nine aneurysms (n = 9 male; mean age, 71 years), six occlusive plaques (n = 4 male; mean age, 67 years), and twelve cadaveric aortas (n = 4 male; mean age, 35 years) were collected. In non-atherosclerotic, cadaveric aortas, the most prevalent polymers were PE (33.7 μg/g tissue), PVC (33.5 μg/g), and PP (21.3 μg/g). In atherosclerotic cadaveric aortas, the most prevalent polymers were PE (99.5 μg/g), PP (66.7 μg/g), and PVC (58 μg/g). In aneurysms, the most prevalent polymers were PE (115.8 μg/g), SBR (114.7 μg/g), and PVC (67.3 μg/g). In plaques, the most prevalent polymers were PE (745.5 μg/g), PVC (47.5 μg/g), and SBR (40.3 μg/g). Numerous within-group differences between polymers were noted, often following a common theme of which was the most prevalent. Single plaque scanning electron microscopy/energy dispersive X ray spectroscopy revealed shard-like MNPs surrounded by calcium/phosphorus, suggesting that MNPs accumulate within plaque calcifications in occlusive disease. CONCLUSIONS: Detectable levels of MNPs were found in aortic atherosclerotic occlusive plaques, aortic aneurysms, and both normal and diseased cadaveric aortas. Nanoplastics were visualized within plaque calcifications in occlusive disease. Further study is needed to elucidate the mechanisms through which MNPs play a role in atherosclerotic and degenerative vascular disease development. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: This work examines the levels of micronanoplastics in human aortic aneurysms and aortoiliac atherosclerotic plaques and reports the levels in healthy and diseased cadaveric aortic tissues. No clear associations between age and micronanoplastic levels were demonstrated among patients. Of the polymers measured, polyethene, polyvinyl chloride, and polypropylene were the most common. These data not only add to the growing literature suggesting that micronanoplastic particles accumulate in atherosclerotic lesions but also provide valuable insights regarding the micronanoplastic content of healthy arteries. Future work should investigate the mechanistic role, if any, of micronanoplastics in the pathophysiology of vascular atherosclerotic disease.

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a severe cardiovascular disease involving various genetic and metabolic factors. Micronutrients play critical roles in cardiovascular health, yet their specific impacts on AAA remain unclear. This study aims to explore the effects of micronutrients calcium and zinc on AAA risk mediated by plasma proteins using the two-sample Mendelian Randomization (MR) approach. METHODS: Utilizing large-scale GWAS data from the UK Biobank; FinnGen databases and Decode database, we assessed the causal relationships between 16 micronutrients concentration and AAA risk and analyzed potential mechanisms mediated by plasma proteins. These results highlight the need for further investigation of micronutrients, plasma proteins, and AAA biology before clinical translation.

J Transl Med · Zhuo H, Ou C, Duan M et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) rupture remains the leading cause of AAA-related mortality, while current clinical management relies primarily on aneurysm diameter, which inadequately predicts rupture risk. Increasing evidence suggests that stromal cell heterogeneity critically shapes vascular remodeling; however, the specific fibroblast states associated with AAA rupture predisposition remain poorly defined. METHODS: We integrated cross-species single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets spanning the full spectrum of AAA progression to systematically characterize fibroblast heterogeneity and state transitions. Pseudotime trajectory analysis, transcriptional regulatory network inference, and ligand-receptor interaction modeling were applied to delineate fibroblast-associated microenvironmental programs. CONCLUSIONS: This study identifies a COL10A1+ fibroblast-associated transcriptional state that defines a rupture-prone vascular microenvironment in abdominal aortic aneurysm. Intravenous leiomyomatosis with marked edematous degeneration mimicking IVC thrombus: A case report.

Case reportRadiol Case Rep · Wada K, Yamada S, Takahata A et al. · Full text
Abstract

Intravenous leiomyomatosis (IVL) is a rare variant of leiomyoma characterized by proliferation of smooth muscle cells within uterine veins, with potential extension into the inferior vena cava (IVC), right atrium, or even pulmonary arteries. In advanced stages, IVL can lead to life-threatening complications, underscoring the need for accurate diagnosis and timely intervention. We report the case of a 40-year-old woman in whom an isoechoic structure within the IVC was incidentally detected on transabdominal ultrasonography and initially suspected to represent an IVC thrombus. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) demonstrated an enlarged posterior uterine wall with heterogeneous internal enhancement. A filling defect with subtle contrast enhancement within the dilated vessels extended continuously from the uterus through the pelvic venous plexus and right ovarian vein into the IVC. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed heterogeneous high-signal-intensity lesions in the uterine myometrium with extension into the pelvic venous plexuses, ovarian veins, and the IVC, findings suggestive of a neoplastic process rather than thrombosis. The patient underwent surgical excision of the intravenous mass in conjunction with hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy. Histopathological examination confirmed IVL with prominent edematous and degenerative changes. This case highlights the diagnostic difficulty of IVL with marked degeneration, which can closely mimic venous thrombosis, and emphasizes that a continuous pelvic-to-IVC extension, an associated uterine mass, and subtle contrast enhancement should strongly prompt the inclusion of IVL in the differential diagnosis.

Retroperitoneal Hematoma in Essential Thrombocythaemia: A Diagnostic Challenge.

Case reportClin Ter · Di Gennaro L, Foti F, Benedetti B et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Essential thrombocythaemia (ET) is a chronic myeloproliferative neoplasm characterized by sustained thrombocytosis and an increased risk of both thrombotic and haemorrhagic events. While thrombotic complications are more frequent, major bleeding remains a rare but potentially fatal occurrence, particularly when anticoagulant therapy is required. Retroperitoneal haematoma represents one of the most severe bleeding manifestations, often linked to anticoagulation or vascular fragility. CASE REPORT: We report the case of an 85-year-old woman with ET, obesity, and hypertension who underwent elective total right hip replacement. Postoperatively, she developed pulmonary micro-embolism and was treated with high-dose enoxaparin. Four days later, she suffered sudden haemodynamic collapse due to a massive retroperitoneal haematoma. Enoxaparin was discontinued and replaced with tranexamic acid infusion. Despite urgent surgical evacuation, multiple transfusions, and intensive care, her condition worsened with the development of femoral vein thrombosis, multidrug-resistant sepsis, worsening pulmonary embolism, and severe thrombocytopenia. She died 34 days after surgery. Autopsy confirmed a large organized haematoma compressing the right renal lodge and inferior vena cava, associated with femoral vein thrombosis. DISCUSSION: This case highlights the therapeutic and diagnostic challenges of ET, in which thrombotic and haemorrhagic risks may coexist and be further complicated by antithrombotic therapy. Despite individualized enoxaparin dosing based on body weight, the patient developed a major retroperitoneal haemorrhage followed by femoral vein thrombosis. Although disseminated intravascular coagulation and heparin-induced thrombocytopenia were both considered in the differential diagnosis, insufficient clinical and laboratory evidence prevented definitive diagnostic confirmation of either condition. CONCLUSIONS: Early and accurate diagnostic assessment is crucial in ET patients, where thrombotic and haemorrhagic risks coexist. In the absence of clear guidelines, clinical judgment prevails, underscoring the need to reassess physicians' criminal liability.

Endothelial indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 drives high-tryptophan diet-mediated thrombosis in cancer-bearing mice.

Blood Vessel Thromb Hemost · Jose A, Lotfollahzadeh S, Natrakul A et al. · Full text
Abstract

Cancer-associated venous thromboembolism is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with solid tumors and is driven, in part, by tumor-induced metabolic reprogramming. The tryptophan (Trp)-kynurenine (Kyn) pathway, regulated by indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), is frequently upregulated in cancer and generates bioactive metabolites with immunomodulatory and vascular effects. Although pharmacologic inhibition of IDO1 reduces thrombosis in tumor-bearing mice, the endothelial-specific contribution of IDO1 to cancer-associated thrombogenesis has not been genetically defined. Here, we used IDO1 global knockout and inducible IDO1 endothelial-specific knockout mice in a syngeneic MC38 tumor model combined with dietary Trp modulation to delineate the vascular role of IDO1. Global genetic deletion of IDO1 significantly reduced systemic Kyn accumulation and attenuated thrombus formation in the inferior vena cava of tumor-bearing mice.
