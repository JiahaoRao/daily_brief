Ischemic myocardium-targeted NAD+ nanotherapy mitigates myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury.

New todayJ Control Release · Xie X, Zhang X, Zhou Z et al. · Full text
Abstract

Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury remains a major cause of cardiomyocyte loss and adverse remodeling following acute myocardial infarction, yet effective therapeutic options remain limited. Although nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) plays a central role in mitochondrial function and redox homeostasis, its therapeutic application is limited by poor membrane permeability and inefficient restoration of intracellular NAD+ pools. Here, we developed a cardiac-targeted lipid-polymer hybrid nanoparticle (LN-DPC) that enables efficient intracellular delivery of NAD+ to ischemic myocardium. The nanoparticale integrates boronate-guanidine-mediated NAD+ encapsulation with an ischemic myocardium-homing peptide (CSTSMLKAC), achieving stable loading, controlled release, and preferential accumulation in injured cardiac tissue. In vitro, LN-DPC enhanced cellular uptake and restored intracellular NAD+ levels, leading to improved mitochondrial function, reduced reactive oxygen species generation, and alleviation of calcium overload under OGD/R conditions. In vivo, the nanoparticles preferentially accumulated in the injured myocardium, attenuated early inflammation, significantly reduced infarct size, improved cardiac function, and markedly limited chronic fibrosis. Mechanistically, NAD+ delivery restored SIRT3 expression, improved mitochondrial homeostasis, and suppressed HMGB1-RAGE signaling to mitigate secondary myocardial injury; pharmacological SIRT3 inhibition attenuated the protective effects of LN-DPC both in vitro and in vivo, supporting a functional contribution of SIRT3 to LN-DPC-mediated cardioprotection. Collectively, this work establishes targeted NAD+ delivery as an effective strategy to restore metabolic homeostasis and protect against ischemia-reperfusion injury, and provides a nanomedicine platform for metabolic intervention in cardiovascular diseases.

Packing-accelerated consecutive NIR excitation (PACE) enables high energy photocatalysis and antitumor immunity in vivo.

Sci Adv · Liu Y, Wang S, Hu W et al. · Full text
Abstract

Near-infrared (NIR) photocatalysis promises deep-tissue compatibilities but remains fundamentally constrained by two bottlenecks: insufficient photon energy and rapid charge recombination. Here, we report packing-accelerated consecutive excitation (PACE), a mechanism enabling low energy near-infrared (NIR) photons to drive high energy single electron transfer (SET) reactions. We design a self-assembling ruthenium complex, Ru-3, that forms densely packed nanoaggregates exhibiting aggregation-stabilized excited states, a pronounced excited state absorption band at ∼1315 nm, and nanosecond scale triplet lifetimes. Under dual NIR-I/IIa irradiation (808 nm/1310 nm), Ru-3 nanoparticles exhibit photophysical behavior consistent with consecutive photon harvesting and access to a higher-energy reactive state that supports efficient SET catalysis under aqueous conditions. This PACE-driven reactivity enables robust oxidation of mitochondrial NADH to NAD+, disrupting electron-transport homeostasis, inducing cGAS-STING dependent pyroptosis, and reprogramming tumor-associated macrophages toward an M1 phenotype. In multiple murine tumor models, PACE-mediated photocatalysis elicits strong antitumor immunity and leads to tumor regression with minimal systemic toxicity. This work establishes PACE as a new photochemical paradigm that overcomes the intrinsic energy and charge-separation limits of NIR photoredox chemistry, opening a route toward high-energy photocatalysis in complex biological environments.

Maternal exposure to microplastics and nanoplastics: Placental lipid metabolic dysregulation as a potential link to fetal growth restriction.

ReviewReprod Toxicol · Gong W, Luo X, Wang H et al. · Full text
Abstract

Microplastics and nanoplastics (MNPs) are pervasive environmental contaminants linked to reproductive and developmental health. Plastic particles or polymeric residues have been detected in human blood, placenta, meconium, breast milk, fetal appendages, and umbilical venous blood, indicating maternal-fetal interface exposure during pregnancy. Fetal growth restriction (FGR) is a placental syndrome in which the fetus fails to achieve its growth potential, and impaired placental vascular, transport, endocrine, immune, and metabolic functions contribute to its pathogenesis. Abstract

Abstract

Abstract

Novel applications of SIRT1 in Chronic Kidney Disease.

ReviewFront Med (Lausanne) · Han Y, Wang Q, Pan X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) has become a global public health issue, with progressive kidney fibrosis serving as a key pathological process leading to kidney failure, and current therapeutic approaches remain largely ineffective in reversing this progression. SIRT1, as an NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase, plays a central protective role in counteracting kidney fibrosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, and autophagic dysfunction by regulating multiple signaling pathways, and has emerged as a promising target in CKD treatment. This review systematically elaborates on the mechanisms regulating SIRT1 expression and activity in CKD, its protective roles in core pathological processes, and summarizes recent advances in targeting SIRT1 for CKD therapy. Functionally, SIRT1 antagonizes kidney fibrosis by deacetylating Smad3/Smad4 and inhibiting epithelial-mesenchymal transition; curbs inflammatory cascades through dual blockade of NF-κB transcriptional activity and NLRP3 inflammasome assembly; constructs a multi-layered antioxidant defense network by activating the Nrf2/ARE pathway, regulating FOXO transcription factors, and enhancing mitochondrial biogenesis; and restores autophagic flux to clear damaged organelles. In terms of therapeutic strategies, the synthetic SIRT1 agonist SRT2104 has completed Phase I-II clinical trials with favorable safety profiles, but its pharmacokinetic limitations and the disconnect from preclinical effects pose barriers to clinical translation; NAD⁺ precursor strategies offer theoretical advantages but lack clinical evidence in CKD populations. In summary, SIRT1 integrates metabolic sensing, transcriptional regulation, and post-translational modification functions, serving as a key molecular hub linking upstream pathological signals to downstream organ damage in CKD. Precise modulation of SIRT1 activity may offer a novel multi-target synergistic therapeutic strategy for CKD.

Mouse Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm Model Induced by Perivascular Sheath Microinjection of Chymotrypsin.

J Vis Exp · Shen L, Zeng J, Huang R et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening degenerative vascular disease for which no effective pharmacological therapy currently exists. Reliable and technically accessible animal models are essential for mechanistic and preclinical investigations. However, existing murine platforms are limited by technical complexity, prolonged operative time, or incomplete recapitulation of human AAA pathology. Here, this study presents a novel murine AAA model established by direct perivascular sheath microinjection of chymotrypsin. After midline laparotomy and identification of the infrarenal abdominal aorta, chymotrypsin working solution is injected directly into the perivascular sheath space under direct vision using a microscale needle. To determine the optimal enzyme concentration, a pilot dose-finding study was conducted using 0.125% (w/v; 1.25 mg/mL), 0.25% (w/v; 2.50 mg/mL), and 0.375% (w/v; 3.75 mg/mL) chymotrypsin (n = 5 per group), with PBS as negative control and topical PPE as positive control. Serial Doppler ultrasonography confirmed a 100% aneurysm induction rate at day 14 in chymotrypsin-treated mice, whereas no dilation occurred in PBS-injected controls. Quantitative morphometry demonstrated significant increases in aortic wall thickness and collagen volume fraction, and a corresponding reduction in elastic fiber content in chymotrypsin-treated animals compared with controls. This protocol thus provides a technically streamlined, highly reproducible, and translationally relevant murine AAA model. Because the procedure avoids aortic dissection, arteriotomy, or specialized microsurgical training, it is well suited to laboratories investigating aneurysm pathobiology or evaluating novel pharmacological interventions.
