Targeted metabolic reprogramming of CD8 T cells restores immune regulation in lupus.

Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A · Oba S, Humbel MH, Lancia HH et al. · Full text
Abstract

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is characterized by pathogenic humoral autoimmunity, yet the mechanisms linking T cell dysfunction to aberrant B cell responses remain poorly defined. Here, we identify a CD8+ T cell-intrinsic metabolic defect that promotes humoral immune dysregulation in lupus. CD8+ T cells from patients with SLE exhibited elevated CD38 expression associated with reduced intracellular NAD+ levels, mitochondrial dysfunction, and impaired cytotoxic capacity. Pharmacologic inhibition of CD38 with the small-molecule inhibitor MK-0159 restored NAD+ metabolism and rescued cytotoxic function in human CD8+ T cells ex vivo. To selectively target this pathway in vivo, we developed anti-CD8 antibody-conjugated nanoparticles encapsulating MK-0159. In lupus-prone MRL/lpr mice, CD8-targeted delivery enhanced granzyme B expression, improved degranulation, reduced plasmablast responses and autoantibody production, immune complex deposition, and renal pathology. Importantly, targeted delivery restored CD8+ T cell function without broad immune perturbation. These findings identify CD38-driven NAD+ depletion as a reversible immunometabolic checkpoint limiting CD8+ T cell cytotoxic competence and support a model in which targeted restoration of cytotoxic fitness contributes to the control of humoral autoimmunity in SLE.

Bifunctional Pt/CeO2 Nanozyme-Mediated Metabolic Regulation Promotes NAD+ Regeneration and ROS Clearance to Ameliorate Age-Related Osteoporosis.

ACS Appl Mater Interfaces · Zhang H, Yang C, Zhou Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Age-related osteoporosis is characterized by progressive loss of bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, driven by chronic inflammation, redox imbalance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and osteoblast senescence that uncouples bone formation from resorption. During aging, declining nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) availability further exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and stress responses, suggesting that restoring NAD+ redox homeostasis could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Here, we reported a bifunctional nanozyme-mediated metabolic regulation strategy to promote NAD+ regeneration and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) for the treatment of age-related osteoporosis. The bifunctional nanozyme (Pt/CeO2) was constructed by in situ growth of platinum nanoparticles on cerium oxide nanorods and was designed for ROS scavenging and NAD+ regeneration via NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) oxidation. In senescent osteoblasts, Pt/CeO2 reduced ROS, restored mitochondrial integrity, and alleviated senescence, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation. In vivo results confirmed that systemic administration of Pt/CeO2 preserved trabecular architecture and increased bone mineral density in naturally aged mice. These findings demonstrated that coupling ROS scavenging with the restoration of NAD+ redox homeostasis via Pt/CeO2 nanozyme provided a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract osteoblast senescence and slow the progression of age-related osteoporosis.

Dynamic Cellular Origins of Circulating DAMPs and Their Role in Thromboinflammation During Sepsis.

New todayReviewJ Clin Med · Iba T, Helms J, Okada H et al. · Full text
Abstract

Circulating damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) are associated with injury and adverse outcomes in sepsis, but their concentrations do not identify their cellular origins or rates of release. This narrative review compares nuclear cell-free DNA, mitochondrial DNA, HMGB1, extracellular histones, nucleosomes, ATP, heat shock proteins, extracellular RNA, and NAD+, with attention to source specificity, extracellular persistence, and assay limitations. Human methylation studies identify leukocyte and parenchymal contributions to nuclear cell-free DNA, while experimental models demonstrate that nonhematopoietic contributions can emerge alongside ongoing hematopoietic release. A DAMP concentration should therefore be interpreted together with sampling time, molecular form, and clinical context.

Dual-layer regulation of macrophage grancalcin links diabetic redox-metabolic imbalance to impaired vascularized bone regeneration.

Redox Biol · Jin S, Song Z, Fang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Effective bone regeneration requires the timely transition of immune cells from inflammatory defense to regenerative support. However, how diabetic redox-metabolic stress sustains maladaptive immune states and drives regenerative failure remains incompletely understood. Herein, using a mouse jawbone defect model combined with single-cell RNA sequencing, metabolic assays and human peri-extraction gingival specimens, we identified a senescence-associated macrophage state characterized by elevated grancalcin (GCA). Functionally, recombinant GCA impaired osteogenic and endothelial responses, whereas GCA neutralization partially reversed the inhibitory effects of diabetic macrophage-conditioned medium, supporting extracellular GCA as a functional mediator of regenerative dysfunction. Mechanistically, diabetic macrophages exhibited enhanced glycolytic activity, impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and a reduced NAD+/NADH ratio. Local delivery of quercetin using a β-cyclodextrin/GelMA hydrogel attenuated macrophage GCA dysregulation, reduced senescence-associated macrophage features and improved vascularized jawbone regeneration. Collectively, this work defines a dual-layer paradigm controlling extracellular GCA bioavailability, thereby linking diabetic redox-metabolic perturbation to defective bone regeneration and supporting local targeting of macrophage GCA for diabetic bone-healing therapy.

Reactive Oxygen Species After Myocardial Infarction: From Pathophysiological Mechanisms to Prognostic and Therapeutic Implications.

New todayReviewAntioxidants (Basel) · Țocu L, Ștefănescu BI, Mihailov R et al. · Full text
Abstract

Myocardial infarction (MI) remains a major cause of death and heart failure despite reperfusion. Reopening an occluded coronary artery salvages viable myocardium but creates an ischemia-reperfusion environment in which reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reactive nitrogen species (RNS) can contribute to injury. Postinfarction redox responses continue through inflammation, repair, and remodeling. Their effects depend on species, concentration, source, compartment, timing, molecular targets, and local antioxidant capacity; controlled redox signaling is essential for normal vascular, metabolic, and immune function. This narrative review focuses primarily on type 1 MI, particularly reperfused ST-segment elevation MI. It examines mitochondrial and enzymatic oxidant sources, including nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidases, xanthine oxidoreductase, nitric oxide synthases, and myeloperoxidase, alongside secondary lipid- and nitrogen-derived products. ReviewEur J Neurosci · Zhang Q, Chen Y, Bai M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is a dynamic syndrome driven by focal brain injury, pre-existing cerebrovascular burden, metabolic comorbidities, and reduced brain resilience. Increasing evidence suggests that persistent disruption of redox-metabolic communication within the neurovascular unit (NVU), rather than neuronal loss alone, promotes the transition from acute ischemic injury to chronic cognitive decline. This review summarizes metabolic reprogramming in BMECs, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, and neurons, emphasizing the intercellular transfer of metabolites, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondria. We propose that PSCI develops through a spatiotemporally dysregulated continuum involving vascular injury, cell-specific metabolic remodeling, maladaptive signal propagation, impaired axonal and synaptic energy support, and progressive network disconnection. Epigenetic remodeling in pulmonary arterial hypertension: metabolic regulation and therapeutic opportunities.

New todayReviewClin Epigenetics · Kucheriava M, Hadri L, Bisserier M · Full text
Abstract

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive pulmonary vasculopathy characterized by endothelial dysfunction, smooth muscle cell proliferation, vascular remodeling, and right ventricular pressure overload. Although contemporary therapies improve symptoms, hemodynamics, and clinical outcomes, they do not fully reverse the persistent pulmonary vascular cell phenotypes that sustain disease progression. Disease-associated vascular cell phenotypes can persist beyond the immediate signaling environment, indicating that mechanisms capable of stabilizing abnormal cell states contribute to PAH. These include regulatory reprogramming, sustained signaling, and the selection or expansion of abnormal cell populations. Epigenetic remodeling provides a framework for understanding this biology. DNA methylation, histone acetylation, histone methylation, chromatin accessibility, and acetyl-lysine reader pathways have all been implicated in PAH pathogenesis. PAH is characterized by profound metabolic reprogramming, including aerobic glycolysis, impaired mitochondrial oxidative metabolism, altered redox homeostasis, glutamine dependence, and changes in lipid and acetyl-CoA metabolism. These two fields should not be considered separately. Many metabolites disrupted in PAH directly regulate chromatin-modifying enzymes. Acetyl-CoA fuels histone acetylation, NAD+ controls sirtuin-dependent deacetylation, S-adenosylmethionine supports DNA and histone methylation, and α-ketoglutarate and oxygen are required for dioxygenases that mediate DNA and histone demethylation, respectively. Lactate is also emerging as a candidate interface between glycolytic remodeling and chromatin regulation through histone lactylation, and RNA N6-methyladenosine constitutes a parallel epitranscriptomic layer governed by the same pool of metabolites. This review focuses on PAH, with an emphasis on pulmonary artery endothelial and smooth muscle vascular cells. We first define the pulmonary vascular and transcriptional context in which persistent disease phenotypes develop and set out the evidence required to distinguish metabolic-epigenetic coupling from durable cellular memory. We summarize the core epigenetic mechanisms relevant to PAH and discuss the specific epigenetic abnormalities reported in pulmonary vascular cells. Finally, we examine how metabolic dysfunction shapes the epigenetic landscape, consider the therapeutic implications, and outline the priorities needed to move the field toward translational precision.

Abstract

Long COVID, chronic fatigue syndrome, and chronic lyme disease as Post-acute Infection Syndromes (PAIS): a translational synthesis.

ReviewJ Transl Med · Sewell PE, Reddy R · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) comprise a heterogeneous group of chronic, multisystem conditions characterized by persistent or relapsing symptoms following resolution of an acute infectious illness, in the absence of ongoing pathogen replication detectable by standard diagnostics. Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), Long COVID, post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, and related entities are increasingly recognized as part of a shared post-infectious disease spectrum. The COVID-19 pandemic has markedly expanded the global burden of PAIS and intensified investigation into their biological underpinnings. Integrating molecular insights with pragmatic clinical frameworks may accelerate development of precision-oriented diagnostic and therapeutic strategies to address the growing global impact of post-infectious chronic illness.

Abstract

This necessitates the study of disease-relevant cells in vitro, in this case smooth muscle cells (SMCs); however, current in vitro models are limited due to SMC dedifferentiation on conventional plastic substrates, which cause a loss of disease-relevant features. Abstract

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) contribute to vascular injury, but their involvement in aneurysm progression and their potential as therapeutic targets remain incompletely understood.
