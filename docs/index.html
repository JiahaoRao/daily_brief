Eragrostis (Poaceae): a case of mismatch between C4 biochemical subtype and Kranz anatomy traits.

New todayProtoplasma · Voznesenskaya EV, Britski DA, Borisenko TA et al. · Full text
Abstract

Kranz anatomy traits have traditionally been used for the identification of C4 biochemical subtypes in C4 grasses. However, distinct mismatches have been reported within the genus Eragrostis. We investigated photosynthetic traits in 37 Eragrostis species, 16 of which were examined for the first time using light microscopy (LM) and 31 via transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Western blot analysis confirmed that all studied species belong to the NAD-ME biochemical subtype, regardless of whether they exhibit centripetal (cp.), centrifugal (cf.), or intermediate bundle sheath (BS) chloroplast positions. Despite this "PEP-CK-like" or intermediate Kranz anatomy, chloroplast ultrastructure, specifically the dimorphism in grana development, consistently matched the classical NAD-ME subtype. Mitochondria positioning strictly follows chloroplast location across all variants while their size and ultrastructure corresponded to NAD-ME biochemistry. Even a minimal spatial displacement of chloroplastic Rubisco and mitochondrial decarboxylases in BS cells from their optimal arrangement near the vascular tissues leads to BS cell wall suberization. Consequently, suberin lamella deposition in BS cell walls, which prevents CO2 leakage, is not rigidly determined by the C4 biochemical subtype. Macroecological analysis demonstrated a strong correlation between specific Kranz anatomy traits, such as BS chloroplast positioning (cp. versus non-cp.), and annual precipitation within the species habitats. Accordingly, species with cp. positioning are confined to drier habitats, suggesting that the geographic distribution of these grasses is primarily driven by specific Kranz anatomy modifications rather than their uniform NAD-ME biochemistry. This decoupling highlights a high evolutionary plasticity of Kranz anatomy in response to environmental gradients.

Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1-targeted redox remodeling by a mesoporous medium-entropy alloy nanozyme promotes ferroptosis and cuproptosis for tumor therapy.

J Colloid Interface Sci · Wang C, Chen C, Zhu J et al. · Full text
Abstract

Tumor cells exhibit robust redox adaptive capacity, limiting the effectiveness of ROS amplification alone in inducing persistent and irreversible oxidative damage. Accordingly, simultaneously enhancing oxidative stress and disrupting antioxidant defenses represents an effective strategy for improving catalytic cancer therapy. Here, we developed hyaluronic acid-functionalized NLG-919-loaded mesoporous PtPdRhCu medium-entropy alloy colloidal nanoparticles (PPRCNH), an interface-engineered platform integrating porous architecture, multimetallic electronic coupling, surface functionalization, and metabolic regulation. The mesoporous framework exposes abundant low-coordination catalytic sites, while interactions among Pt, Pd, Rh, and Cu promote H2O2 activation and sustained ROS production. HA functionalization facilitates the uptake of PPRCNH by CD44-overexpressing osteosarcoma cells, whereas NLG-919 inhibits indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1)-dependent tryptophan-to-kynurenine conversion, consequently impairing G6PD-mediated nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) regeneration. The acidic tumor microenvironment further accelerates Cu release and perturbs mitochondrial lipoylated protein homeostasis. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that PPRCNH enhanced ROS generation and suppressed antioxidant compensation dependent on glutathione (GSH) and NADPH, resulting in mitochondrial dysfunction and the simultaneous induction of ferroptosis and cuproptosis. In the osteosarcoma model, PPRCNH achieved a tumor growth inhibition rate of approximately 77.2%. This study establishes a catalytic-metabolic regulatory paradigm by integrating mesoporous medium-entropy alloy interfaces with metabolic checkpoint modulation to achieve sustained oxidative stress amplification.

From NAD+ elevation to clinical benefit: Tissue-specific regulation and translational barriers in aging and age-related disease.

ReviewPharmacol Res · Pei Z, Zhong M, Jiang Q et al. · Full text
Abstract

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) decline has been implicated in aging and age-related disease, and precursor supplementation with nicotinamide riboside (NR) or nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) has shown efficacy in preclinical models. Human trials, however, have produced inconsistent functional outcomes, and the sources of this translational gap remain poorly defined. We combined a bibliometric analysis of 3271 aging-focused NAD+ publications (2004-2025) with a critical synthesis of mechanistic and clinical evidence to localize where and why preclinical findings fail to translate. The bibliometric analysis revealed three structural features of the literature: (i) original research articles dominated the corpus, whereas the document-type classification cannot distinguish preclinical experiments from human observational or interventional studies; (ii) research is siloed across disease domains with limited cross-tissue comparison; and (iii) intervention-oriented keywords (NMN, NR, supplementation, drug delivery) have outpaced empirical clinical validation. Mechanistic synthesis showed that these structural gaps correspond to biological heterogeneity: NAD+ biosynthesis, consumption, redox balance, and subcellular compartmentalization are tissue- and compartment-specific, such that systemic precursor elevation does not uniformly restore functional NAD+ in disease-relevant tissues. Four subcellular pools-nuclear, cytosolic, mitochondrial, and axonal-each possess distinct rate-limiting bottlenecks, and hepatic metabolic reserve introduces an overlooked source of inter-individual variability. Across neurodegeneration, metabolic disease, cardiovascular aging, and sarcopenia, NAD+ dysregulation follows disease-specific mechanisms rather than a universal decline phenotype. We propose a precision-medicine framework that shifts the core question from whether NAD+ can be elevated to whether a defined intervention corrects a specific NAD+ defect in the appropriate tissue and subcellular compartment. This framework provides a structured basis for designing next-generation NAD+-targeted trials with tissue-specific biomarkers and function-oriented endpoints.

Abstract

Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is one of the major causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide, still lacking an etiologic therapy. Aberrant activation of TGF-β and dysregulation of mechanotransduction signalling in vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) play a key role in the pathology. Abstract

J Mol Cell Cardiol · Guo J, Lareyre F, Chinetti G et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIM: The molecular mechanisms underlying abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) progression remain incompletely understood and may vary across anatomically distinct regions of the diseased aorta. We hypothesised that the aneurysmal wall and aneurysm neck exhibit distinct molecular profiles, reflecting regional heterogeneity in AAA tissue. To this end, we characterized and compared targeted proteomic and transcriptomic profiles of these two anatomically distinct regions to identify molecular alterations associated with AAA pathology. METHODS: Paired aneurysmal wall and aneurysm neck tissues were obtained from 18 patients with AAA. Inflammatory proteins were quantified using the Olink Target 48 cytokine panel, with 45 proteins retained for analysis after quality-control filtering. CONCLUSIONS: Aneurysm wall and neck tissues showed largely similar inflammatory molecular profiles, with only a limited number of regional differences identified either on the protein or transcript level. CCL19 being the only concordant signal in both proteomic and transcriptomic analysis.

bioRxiv · Spin JM, Mulorz J, Shen WJ et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Smoking and family history are major risk factors for abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). Smoking and nicotine are modulators of DNA methylation, capable of causing heritable epigenetic alterations. Nicotine infusion and e-cigarette (e-cig) vaping augment murine AAA. We investigated the effects of murine maternal e-cig vaping on offspring experimental AAA, and impacts on transgenerational DNA methylation and accessibility. METHODS: We performed perinatal e-cig nicotine vaping in two strains of adult female mice (ApoE-/- and C57BL/6J). Mice were mated to control males, and treated through gestation. A parallel group were instead sacrificed after treatment, for DNA analysis. Offspring were aged to adulthood, and AAAs were then induced by elastase (C57BL/6J) or angiotensin-II infusion (ApoE-/-), and ultrasound-tracked for 4 weeks. In a second cohort, parental and offspring aortic tissue and blood DNA underwent RRBS and ATAC-seq analysis respectively. Offspring abdominal aortic anatomy was examined, and stiffness was assessed with ex vivo arterial myography. RESULTS: Maternal e-cig exposure increased offspring AAA growth in both models and genders. AAA incidence was increased in ApoE-/- males, and observed effects were overall more pronounced in males. Maternal vape exposure altered aortic DNA methylation and blood DNA accessibility in mothers and offspring, with differential sites in numerous genes identified, including AAA-related genes and pathways. Pathways were enriched for transcription factors, inflammation/immune-related genes, and angiogenesis genes, with similar changes identified in exposed mothers. Male offspring displayed more differential genes. Significant changes were located in genes known to harbor AAA risk loci in humans, and overlapped with published epigenetic alterations in human smokers. Differences also occurred in offspring aortic structure and stiffness. CONCLUSIONS: Maternal e-cig nicotine exposure augments AAA growth in multiple models across generations. These effects are accompanied by broad epigenetic DNA reprogramming of key AAA modulator genes and pathways.

Abstract

Aortic dissection (AD) is an emergent medical condition with a hidden onset and sudden rupture, which usually results in a high mortality rate. N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant modification in eukaryotic RNA molecules and plays a critical role in regulating RNA metabolism. SPOCK2-mediated phenotype switch of vascular smooth muscle cells contributes to aortic aneurysm in Kawasaki disease.

Front Immunol · Xu X, Wei Y, Wang M et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Coronary artery aneurysm is a major cardiovascular complication of Kawasaki disease (KD). The phenotype switch of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) plays a crucial role in pathological vascular wall remodeling in KD; however, the molecular mechanisms driving this process remain incompletely understood. Although the SPARC protein family has been implicated in various vascular diseases, the role of specific SPARC family members in mediating VSMC phenotypic transition during KD-associated vasculitis remains unknown. Here, we investigated the function and mechanistic role of SPOCK2, a SPARC family member, in vascular wall homeostasis under KD-induced inflammatory stress. METHODS: SPOCK2 transcript expression was evaluated in public transcriptomic datasets and clinical KD cohorts. To model KD vasculitis in vivo, mice were challenged with Candida albicans water-soluble fraction (CAWS). Adeno-associated virus (AAV)2/9-mediated gene transfer was employed for targeted in vivo overexpression of SPOCK2 in mouse aortas. VSMC phenotypic transition, vascular remodeling, extracellular matrix (ECM) integrity, and local cytokine production were assessed using immunofluorescence, RNA sequencing, quantitative RT-PCR, and co-immunoprecipitation assays to delineate SPOCK2-MMP2 structural and functional interactions. RESULTS: Transcriptional profiling of clinical KD cohorts revealed that SPOCK2 is consistently downregulated in the acute phase of the disease. In murine KD vasculitis models, SPOCK2 expression was notably depleted in both aortic root and abdominal aorta, correlating with severe vascular inflammation and aneurysm formation. Targeted in vivo overexpression of SPOCK2 via AAV2/9 effectively restored the contractile VSMC phenotype, thereby attenuating vasculitis and preventing abdominal aortic aneurysm formation. Mechanistically, SPOCK2 acts as a specific endogenous inhibitor of MMP2. Through physical interaction, SPOCK2 binds to MMP2, hindering its enzymatic activation. In the inflammatory microenvironment of KD, the pathological loss of SPOCK2 leads to unchecked MMP2 activity, promoting uncontrolled extracellular matrix degradation and pathogenic contractile-to-synthetic transition of VSMCs. CONCLUSION: Our findings establish SPOCK2 as a novel protective factor in vascular wall homeostasis and a potential candidate for preventive strategies targeting vascular remodeling and systemic vasculitis in Kawasaki disease.

Treatment with an anti-interleukin-1β antibody does not impact aortic aneurysm progression in a rupture-prone rat model.

New todayJVS Vasc Sci · Soltan O, Elizondo-Benedetto S, Wahidi R et al. · Full text
Abstract

UNLABELLED: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening inflammatory vascular disease with no effective medical therapies to mitigate its progression and rupture. Although interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) plays a central role in AAA pathobiology, the specific impact of its inhibition on progression and rupture remains unclear. This study evaluated whether selective antibody-mediated IL-1β inhibition can reduce aneurysm progression in a rupture-prone AAA model in rats. Infrarenal AAAs were induced in male and female Sprague-Dawley rats (N = 30). The 14-day cohort included n = 5/sex/treatment group, whereas the day 6 inflammatory analyses included male rats only (n = 5/treatment group). AAAs were induced using intraluminal porcine pancreatic elastase alongside oral β-aminopropionitrile administration to promote AAA rupture. After 3 days, rats received a neutralizing anti-IL-1β antibody or a control immunoglobulin G twice weekly. Aneurysm growth, rupture incidence, and histopathology were assessed over 14 days. Results demonstrated that treatment with anti-IL-1β antibody did not significantly impact aneurysm progression, rupture rates, collagen content, or elastin degradation in either sex. Although upstream inflammatory mediators remained unchanged, in male rats assessed at day 6, treatment with the neutralizing anti-IL-1β antibody was associated with significantly reduced IL-10 and interferon-γ levels within aneurysmal tissue, alongside a trend toward reduced CD206 expression. These findings suggest that IL-1β plays a multifaceted role in maintaining counter-regulatory immune responses in established AAA, indicating that treatment with anti-IL-1β antibody did not demonstrate measurable structural benefit under the experimental conditions examined. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening condition with no effective medical treatments to prevent its progression and rupture. Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) is essential for AAA formation, as evidenced by the significant attenuation of aneurysm formation in IL-1β receptor knockout models. However, our study demonstrated that treatment with a neutralizing anti-IL-1β antibody did not significantly alter AAA growth or rupture rates in a rupture-prone rat model. These results indicate that anti-IL-1β antibody treatment initiated after aneurysm induction may have limited efficacy in this short-term rupture-prone model, but these findings do not exclude a potential effect of IL-1β inhibition during earlier stages of aneurysm formation. However, as pharmacodynamic target engagement was not directly quantified, incomplete IL-1β neutralization cannot be excluded.

The gut microbiome and vascular disease - A systematic review of mechanistic insights and implications for vascular surgery.

ReviewJVS Vasc Sci · Castro E Silva M, Silva E, Nunes C et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Observational studies repeatedly linked gut microbial features and metabolites to vascular disease, but it is unclear which associations are reproducible, which remain significant after confounder adjustment, and how they relate to experimental evidence. This review aims to determine which taxa and metabolites are consistently associated across independent human studies; whether the population, phenotype, method, and sample type explain divergent findings; and where the human data converge with, diverge from, or remain untested with the animal and interventional literature. METHODS: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library were searched for human studies of the gut microbiome composition or gut-derived metabolites reporting atherosclerotic, aneurysmal, peripheral arterial, calcific, or arterial stiffness outcomes from database inception to December 31, 2025. Study screening and duplicate article extraction were conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. The study design was classified explicitly, the covariates in each primary analysis were extracted, and the risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale or the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist. The findings were then compared with the animal and human interventional literature identified through targeted searching. RESULTS: All the 29 included reports were observational (19 cross-sectional, 3 case-control, and 7 cohort) studies, and none was interventional. The reproducible findings were the enrichment of Proteobacteria-affiliated genera (5 reports) and of oral-origin organisms (3 reports), and the depletion of butyrate producers in carotid disease. The carnitine pathway intermediates gamma-butyrobetaine and trimethyllysine were independently associated with cardiovascular death and major adverse limb events (hazard ratio, 1.93; 95% confidence interval, 1.35-2.76) in two independent cohorts. Trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) was outcome-specific; TMAO levels were associated with 5-year mortality in peripheral arterial disease (adjusted hazard ratio, 2.06; 95% confidence interval, 1.36-3.11) and with ankle-brachial-index-defined disease in diabetes (odds ratio, 1.051), but TMAO levels were lower in large-artery stroke than in controls and were unrelated to carotid or limb outcomes once renal function was modelled. In addition, 16 of 29 reports fitted a multivariable model, and only three included renal function; the compositional findings were almost exclusively unadjusted. The risk of bias was good in 11 reports, moderate in 16, and low in 2. Differential methylation analysis demonstrated hypomethylation of neural crest-related genes in SNT. Copy number variation was significantly higher in the Ewing sarcoma methylation cluster, with recurrent chromosome 8 gains, whereas tumors in the SNT cluster were largely copy number neutral. Despite clustering with SNT by DNA methylation profiling, two of the "Difficult to classify" cases showed at least focal high-grade cytology and had distant metastases, including one dying of disease. Pooled survival analysis of published and current cases demonstrated superior outcome of SNT when compared to all Ewing sarcomas, but no significant differences in overall survival comparing SNT to superficial Ewing sarcoma. Our findings support a cautious diagnostic approach, reserving the diagnosis of SNT for tumors with classic morphologic and immunophenotypic features, while regarding cytologically high-grade or ambiguous lesions as representing Ewing sarcoma for clinical purposes, until the precise nosology of these exceptionally rare tumors is better understood.

Spatial Clonal Discordance in Early Mycosis Fungoides Revealed by Compartment-specific TRBC1 Immunohistochemistry.

Am J Surg Pathol · Huang C, Shao X, Zang J et al. · Full text
Abstract

Diagnosis of early mycosis fungoides (MF) is hindered by overlap with benign dermatoses and by the variable sensitivity of conventional polymerase chain reaction-based molecular clonality assays in paucicellular specimens. We demonstrate that T-cell receptor β-chain constant region 1 (TRBC1) immunohistochemistry (IHC) provides a spatially resolved assessment of T-cell clonality that may aid the evaluation of early MF. In early MF, we identified a characteristic "spatial clonal discordance," where monotypic TRBC1 expression is confined to intraepidermal T cells while dermal infiltrates remain polytypic. This compartment-specific pattern reflects neoplastic epidermotropism and may help explain discordant clonality results obtained by bulk molecular assays in paucicellular lesions. Conversely, tumor-stage MF and Sézary syndrome exhibited spatially concordant, pan-compartmental restriction. Digital image analysis showed high concordance between single-stain IHC and multiplex immunofluorescence, establishing TRBC1+ thresholds of ≤25% or ≥75% for defining clonality. Although this study is limited by its retrospective, single-center design and relatively small cohort, the findings establish TRBC1 IHC as a powerful, spatially informative tool. It effectively overcomes the "dilution effect" of reactive cells in early MF, serving as a high-sensitivity adjunct to conventional molecular testing for accurate, compartment-specific clonal evaluation.
