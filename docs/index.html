Prospects for adiponectin-based therapeutics in myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury.

New todayReviewPharmacol Ther · Bahit MC, Gibson CM, Lei Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Adiponectin is an adipocyte-derived hormone with broad cardiometabolic benefits, circulating at high levels in healthy individuals but reduced in obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Low adiponectin concentrations are strongly associated with coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI), and heart failure. MIRI remains a major unmet need in contemporary ST-segment-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) care. Although reperfusion is essential in limiting ischemic myocardial necrosis, it causes deleterious effects such as impaired carbohydrate oxidation, excessive fatty-acid oxidation, intracellular acidosis, calcium (Ca2+) overload, oxidative/nitrative stress, and mitochondrial fragmentation, that expand infarct size, worsen microvascular obstruction, and fuel adverse ventricular remodeling. Activation of the adiponectin-adiponectin receptor (AdipoR) axis directly counteracts these processes by stimulating adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-dependent metabolic rebalancing, restoring mitochondrial fusion-fission homeostasis, enhancing oxidative capacity, reducing inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS-) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase-mediated injury, improving endothelial nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability, and enhancing microvascular perfusion. In animal models, adiponectin or its mimetics consistently reduced infarct size, improved myocardial function, and attenuated apoptosis, inflammation, and no-reflow. A growing array of therapeutic strategies has emerged, each bringing its own pharmacologic nuances and implications for translation to the clinic. This review summarizes current strategies to enhance adiponectin action, highlights the progress in peptide and small-molecule mimetics, and discusses future directions for clinical translation in cardiometabolic diseases.

Mitochondria-targeted drug delivery systems for aging-related diseases: Mechanisms, strategies, and advances.

ReviewJ Control Release · Zhang F, Ma H, Zhou S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Mitochondrial dysfunction, characterized by increased oxidative stress, impaired energy metabolism, disrupted mitochondrial dynamics, abnormal quality control, and activation of apoptosis, represents a critical pathological basis for a wide range of aging-related diseases (ARDs), including neurodegenerative diseases, cardiovascular disorders, sarcopenia, and osteoarthritis. Mitochondria-targeted drug delivery systems increase the effective concentration of bioactive molecules within these dysfunctional organelles, offering novel strategies for the precise modulation of mitochondrial function. This review summarizes structural and functional changes in mitochondria during aging and their associations with major ARDs. We comprehensively review key advances in the design principles, mechanisms of action, and applications of passive targeting, stimuli-responsive systems, active targeting, and biomimetic drug delivery platforms. In addition, emerging interventions such as NAD+ supplementation, mitochondrial gene delivery, EV-mediated mitochondrial delivery and mitochondrial transplantation are discussed. Despite the demonstrated potential of these systems to restore mitochondrial homeostasis and tissue functionality in various disease models, their clinical translation remains constrained by obstacles including unstable targeting efficacy under pathological conditions, insufficient long-term safety of materials, manufacturing complexity, and a lack of standardized evaluation criteria. Future efforts should focus on integrating disease heterogeneity, advanced materials, and multi-omics technologies to advance mitochondria-targeted therapies toward standardization, individualization, and diagnostic-therapeutic integration.

Heart-brain co-ageing: Phenotypic heterogeneity, molecular mechanisms and translational interventions.

ReviewAgeing Res Rev · Huang H, Zhang W, Hou C et al. · Full text
Abstract

Population ageing has increased the burden of cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline and frailty in older adults. These conditions are often studied separately, yet increasing evidence suggests that the ageing heart, vasculature and brain are biologically interconnected. Using a hybrid design that combines a systematic literature search with narrative synthesis, this review summarizes current knowledge on heart-brain co-ageing, with emphasis on phenotypic heterogeneity, molecular mechanisms and translational interventions. Heart-brain co-ageing is characterized by partially coupled but non-synchronous ageing trajectories of cardiac, vascular and brain function. Major clinical phenotypes include vascular-dominant ageing, cardiac-output-related brain vulnerability, arrhythmia-associated thromboinflammatory injury, frailty-cognitive decline and metabolic-inflammatory co-ageing. These phenotypes differ in risk profiles, dominant mechanisms and potential biomarkers. Candidate shared mechanisms include cellular senescence, chronic low-grade inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, endothelial impairment, blood-brain barrier disruption, epigenetic remodelling, telomere attrition, non-coding ribonucleic acid regulation, Klotho signalling, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide metabolism and microbiome-derived metabolic signalling. Advances in multimodal imaging, circulating biomarkers, biological ageing clocks and multi-omics profiling provide new opportunities for risk stratification, although clinical standardization remains limited. Heart-brain co-ageing should not be regarded as the simple coexistence of cardiovascular and neurological diseases, but as a dynamic multi-organ ageing process shaped by shared biological drivers, interorgan interactions and organ-specific vulnerability, and we propose it as a testable conceptual framework with explicit operational criteria that distinguish coupled from parallel organ ageing. Future research should integrate longitudinal phenotyping, mechanism-based biomarkers and stratified clinical trials to develop precision interventions that preserve both cardiovascular and brain health in older adults.

Metabolic and Immunometabolic Neuroprotection in Glaucoma: Mitochondrial Dysfunction, Neuroinflammation, and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies.

ReviewMolecules · Martucci A, Anselmi C, Romano E et al. · Full text
Abstract

Glaucoma is increasingly recognized as a multifactorial neurodegenerative optic neuropathy in which intraocular pressure (IOP) is one of several stress amplifiers rather than a sufficient etiological explanation. Vascular dysregulation, mitochondrial and metabolic vulnerability, oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, glial dysregulation, and persistent neuroimmune activation can converge on retinal ganglion cell (RGC) and optic nerve injury. This broader framework is particularly relevant to normal-tension glaucoma (NTG), which represents a large proportion of primary open-angle glaucoma in Asian populations, and to patients who continue to progress despite IOP being maintained within the target range. Consequently, mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolic impairment, oxidative stress, and the associated neuroinflammatory response are not merely downstream adjunctive targets: they are core disease mechanisms and potential treatment targets in pressure-independent and pressure-resistant glaucoma. Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), nicotinamide, pyruvate, and citicoline are emerging metabolic and neuroprotective agents whose primary actions involve mitochondrial bioenergetics, redox homeostasis, membrane integrity, and cellular stress responses, with potential secondary effects on neuroinflammatory pathways. Chronic activation of microglia and astrocytes promotes the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species, and apoptotic mediators, leading to RGC degeneration. CoQ10 may counteract these mechanisms through antioxidant activity and stabilization of mitochondrial function; nicotinamide, a precursor of NAD+, may enhance mitochondrial resilience and suppress inflammatory cascades associated with energy failure; pyruvate supports aerobic metabolism and may limit oxidative, inflammatory, and excitotoxic injury; and citicoline supports membrane homeostasis, phospholipid synthesis, neuronal repair, and visual-pathway function. Combined targeting of these interconnected pathways may enhance RGC resilience independently of, and in addition to, IOP reduction. However, current clinical evidence remains insufficient to establish sustained neuroprotection or disease-modifying efficacy. This review examines the interplay among these mechanisms in glaucomatous neurodegeneration and critically discusses the experimental and clinical evidence supporting metabolic and immunometabolic neuroprotective strategies as potential adjuncts to conventional intraocular pressure-lowering therapy.

Integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing identify emodin as a candidate modulator of STAT3 signaling in abdominal aortic aneurysm.

New todayVascul Pharmacol · Liu Y, Xu Z, Deng L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease lacking effective pharmacological therapies. To identify potential therapeutic targets and candidate compounds, we performed integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing by combining Mendelian randomization (MR), summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR), and Bayesian colocalization. This framework prioritized multiple druggable candidates, among which STAT3 emerged as a key regulatory node. Subsequent functional studies demonstrated that emodin suppressed vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation, migration, phenotypic switching, and inflammatory responses. These effects were accompanied by reduced STAT3 phosphorylation and nuclear localization. In vivo, emodin attenuated aneurysm development and reduced vascular inflammation in experimental models. Collectively, these findings identify emodin as a candidate modulator of AAA progression acting through suppression of STAT3-associated signaling pathways, and demonstrate the potential of genetic evidence-guided drug repurposing strategies in complex vascular diseases.

The emerging molecular landscape of abdominal aortic aneurysms: non-coding RNAs as diagnostic and therapeutic targets.

ReviewMol Biol Rep · Skórka P, Brelik J, Kiełbowski K et al. · Full text
Abstract

An abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a progressive, often asymptomatic condition whose rupture is associated with a high mortality rate. Ultrasonography and computed tomography are established diagnostic methods, while molecular biomarkers may provide additional information about disease activity. This review discusses the potential role of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) as diagnostic and therapeutic tools in AAAs. These molecules, including microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs), are strongly involved in the regulation of gene expression. Through binding to their target mRNAs, miRNAs primarily suppress translation or promote mRNA degradation. Some lncRNAs and circRNAs act as miRNA sponges, affecting their availability. Here, these mechanisms are discussed by pathological function, distinguishing human tissue and circulating studies from animal, cellular and computational findings. Particular attention is given to miR-21, H19, GAS5, circCdyl and miR-26b-3p, which have functional experimental evidence or human biomarker data beyond expression profiling. Abnormal expression can be detected in peripheral blood or aortic tissue, but these findings do not necessarily predict aneurysm growth or rupture. Integration of ncRNA profiling with imaging and clinical parameters may improve the assessment of AAAs. However, prospective validation and further studies of delivery and safety are needed before these molecules can be used in clinical practice.

Glycocalicin is contained inside the thrombus.

Platelets · Debaene C, Six KR, Vertongen S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Glycoprotein Ibα (GPIbα) is a platelet-specific receptor that is subjected to proteolytic shedding by ADAM17, releasing its ectodomain glycocalicin. GPIbα proteolysis is induced by strong platelet activation in a process too slow to be crucial in primary hemostasis. The spatiotemporal fate of glycocalicin was assessed during thrombosis using human and murine samples, integrating in vitro approaches and an inferior vena cava (IVC) ligation model. We found that the glycocalicin concentration inside human thrombi was 4-fold higher than in initial plasma (4.0 ± 0.8 µg/mL vs 0.9 ± 0.0 µg/mL, respectively). This was confirmed in vitro starting from human platelet-rich plasma or murine whole blood, with 6-fold and 3-fold increases, respectively. In contrast, HN ES arose in the skull base and sinonasal tract. ALES showed basaloid, epithelioid, or round cell morphology, with occasional palisading and rare squamous differentiation. Site-specific differences observed in our study may aid in diagnosis; however, accurate recognition remains difficult, and a high index of suspicion at specific anatomical sites may be important for diagnosis and management.

Genomic and Transcriptomic Characterization of Angiosarcomas: Focus on Cardiac Origin.

Mod Pathol · Lu T, Liu Y, Yuan M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Cardiac angiosarcoma is a rare and aggressive malignancy with limited therapeutic options, and its molecular features remain poorly defined. This study sought to characterize the genomic and transcriptomic landscape of cardiac angiosarcoma and to explore molecular features associated with clinical outcomes, chemotherapy response, and potential therapeutic vulnerabilities. We retrospectively analyzed 35 patients with cardiac angiosarcoma who underwent whole-exome sequencing (n = 11) and/or whole-transcriptome sequencing (n = 33). An additional 35 patients with angiosarcoma from noncardiac or unknown primary sites were included for comparative genomic analyses. Cardiac angiosarcoma frequently harbored alterations in KDR (55%), PLCG1 (45%), and CCND1 (36%), with recurrent amplifications at 8p23.1, 8p23.2, and 4q12 and recurrent deletions at 17q21.31, 15q13.2, 8p23.1, and 2q11.2. Transcriptomic analyses identified differential expression patterns associated with relapse, mortality, and chemotherapy response. Tumors from the low-relapse-risk group were enriched for axon guidance, whereas those from the high-relapse-risk group were enriched for cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. Approximately one-third of patients exhibited high infiltration of most immune cell populations. Potentially actionable genomic alterations were identified in 71.7% of angiosarcomas. Compared with noncardiac angiosarcomas, cardiac tumors showed higher frequencies of KDR, CCND1, MCL1, and PLCG1 alterations and unique enrichment of the apelin signaling and vascular smooth muscle contraction pathways. Genomic and transcriptomic profiling of cardiac angiosarcoma revealed a distinct molecular landscape with candidate prognostic and therapeutic markers, providing a foundation for future precision oncology strategies in this rare disease.

Dermal Nerve Sheath Myxomas Harbor Recurrent DPYSL2::CDX1 Fusions and Expand the Spectrum of CDX1-Rearranged Soft Tissue Tumors.

Mod Pathol · Dehner CA, Warmke LM, Jasmine SW et al. · Full text
Abstract

Dermal nerve sheath myxoma (DNSM) is a rare benign mesenchymal tumor with presumed nerve sheath differentiation with distinctive morphologic and immunophenotypic features; however, its molecular pathogenesis remains poorly understood. We investigated a series of nine morphologically classic DNSMs and performed molecular testing to identify recurrent genetic alterations. The cohort included five female and four male patients (median age: 56 years; range: 23-75 years). Tumors arose predominantly on the extremities and presented as multinodular dermal-based myxoid neoplasms composed of uniform spindled to epithelioid cells arranged in reticular cords within abundant myxoid stroma. Vacuolated "signet ring-like" cells were identified in all tumors. Immunohistochemically, all tested tumors showed diffuse S100, SOX10 and GFAP expression, and a subset focally expressed EMA, keratin AE1/AE3, and GLUT1. RNA-based sequencing identified an identical in-frame DPYSL2::CDX1 fusion in 7 of 9 cases. Fluorescence in situ hybridization testing using CDX1 and DPYSL2 break apart probes as well as DPYSL2/CDX1 dual-fusion probe confirmed rearrangement in 6/7 tested tumors. We herein demonstrate that most DNSM are characterized by recurrent DPYSL2::CDX1 fusions, representing the first recurrent molecular alteration identified in this tumor type. These findings further support the classification of DNSM as a distinct neoplastic entity and highlight the potential utility of molecular testing as a diagnostic adjunct in morphologically challenging cases. Further studies are warranted to clarify the biologic role of CDX1-related fusions and their relationship to other fusion-driven soft tissue neoplasms, particularly those with CDX1/CDX2 fusions.
