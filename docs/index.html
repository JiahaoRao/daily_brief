Abstract

The intrinsic proteostasis of HSPs/P-gp remains formidable barriers in the multidrug resistance (MDR) and thermotolerance. Mitochondria-targeted drug delivery systems for aging-related diseases: Mechanisms, strategies, and advances.

New todayReviewJ Control Release · Zhang F, Ma H, Zhou S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Mitochondrial dysfunction, characterized by increased oxidative stress, impaired energy metabolism, disrupted mitochondrial dynamics, abnormal quality control, and activation of apoptosis, represents a critical pathological basis for a wide range of aging-related diseases (ARDs), including neurodegenerative diseases, cardiovascular disorders, sarcopenia, and osteoarthritis. Mitochondria-targeted drug delivery systems increase the effective concentration of bioactive molecules within these dysfunctional organelles, offering novel strategies for the precise modulation of mitochondrial function. This review summarizes structural and functional changes in mitochondria during aging and their associations with major ARDs. We comprehensively review key advances in the design principles, mechanisms of action, and applications of passive targeting, stimuli-responsive systems, active targeting, and biomimetic drug delivery platforms. In addition, emerging interventions such as NAD+ supplementation, mitochondrial gene delivery, EV-mediated mitochondrial delivery and mitochondrial transplantation are discussed. Despite the demonstrated potential of these systems to restore mitochondrial homeostasis and tissue functionality in various disease models, their clinical translation remains constrained by obstacles including unstable targeting efficacy under pathological conditions, insufficient long-term safety of materials, manufacturing complexity, and a lack of standardized evaluation criteria. Future efforts should focus on integrating disease heterogeneity, advanced materials, and multi-omics technologies to advance mitochondria-targeted therapies toward standardization, individualization, and diagnostic-therapeutic integration.

Heart-brain co-ageing: Phenotypic heterogeneity, molecular mechanisms and translational interventions.

ReviewAgeing Res Rev · Huang H, Zhang W, Hou C et al. · Full text
Abstract

Population ageing has increased the burden of cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline and frailty in older adults. These conditions are often studied separately, yet increasing evidence suggests that the ageing heart, vasculature and brain are biologically interconnected. Using a hybrid design that combines a systematic literature search with narrative synthesis, this review summarizes current knowledge on heart-brain co-ageing, with emphasis on phenotypic heterogeneity, molecular mechanisms and translational interventions. Heart-brain co-ageing is characterized by partially coupled but non-synchronous ageing trajectories of cardiac, vascular and brain function. Major clinical phenotypes include vascular-dominant ageing, cardiac-output-related brain vulnerability, arrhythmia-associated thromboinflammatory injury, frailty-cognitive decline and metabolic-inflammatory co-ageing. These phenotypes differ in risk profiles, dominant mechanisms and potential biomarkers. Candidate shared mechanisms include cellular senescence, chronic low-grade inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, endothelial impairment, blood-brain barrier disruption, epigenetic remodelling, telomere attrition, non-coding ribonucleic acid regulation, Klotho signalling, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide metabolism and microbiome-derived metabolic signalling. Advances in multimodal imaging, circulating biomarkers, biological ageing clocks and multi-omics profiling provide new opportunities for risk stratification, although clinical standardization remains limited. Heart-brain co-ageing should not be regarded as the simple coexistence of cardiovascular and neurological diseases, but as a dynamic multi-organ ageing process shaped by shared biological drivers, interorgan interactions and organ-specific vulnerability, and we propose it as a testable conceptual framework with explicit operational criteria that distinguish coupled from parallel organ ageing. Future research should integrate longitudinal phenotyping, mechanism-based biomarkers and stratified clinical trials to develop precision interventions that preserve both cardiovascular and brain health in older adults.

Ischemic myocardium-targeted NAD+ nanotherapy mitigates myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury.

J Control Release · Xie X, Zhang X, Zhou Z et al. · Full text
Abstract

Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury remains a major cause of cardiomyocyte loss and adverse remodeling following acute myocardial infarction, yet effective therapeutic options remain limited. Although nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) plays a central role in mitochondrial function and redox homeostasis, its therapeutic application is limited by poor membrane permeability and inefficient restoration of intracellular NAD+ pools. Here, we developed a cardiac-targeted lipid-polymer hybrid nanoparticle (LN-DPC) that enables efficient intracellular delivery of NAD+ to ischemic myocardium. The nanoparticale integrates boronate-guanidine-mediated NAD+ encapsulation with an ischemic myocardium-homing peptide (CSTSMLKAC), achieving stable loading, controlled release, and preferential accumulation in injured cardiac tissue. In vitro, LN-DPC enhanced cellular uptake and restored intracellular NAD+ levels, leading to improved mitochondrial function, reduced reactive oxygen species generation, and alleviation of calcium overload under OGD/R conditions. In vivo, the nanoparticles preferentially accumulated in the injured myocardium, attenuated early inflammation, significantly reduced infarct size, improved cardiac function, and markedly limited chronic fibrosis. Mechanistically, NAD+ delivery restored SIRT3 expression, improved mitochondrial homeostasis, and suppressed HMGB1-RAGE signaling to mitigate secondary myocardial injury; pharmacological SIRT3 inhibition attenuated the protective effects of LN-DPC both in vitro and in vivo, supporting a functional contribution of SIRT3 to LN-DPC-mediated cardioprotection. Collectively, this work establishes targeted NAD+ delivery as an effective strategy to restore metabolic homeostasis and protect against ischemia-reperfusion injury, and provides a nanomedicine platform for metabolic intervention in cardiovascular diseases.

Metabolic and Immunometabolic Neuroprotection in Glaucoma: Mitochondrial Dysfunction, Neuroinflammation, and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies.

New todayReviewMolecules · Martucci A, Anselmi C, Romano E et al. · Full text
Abstract

Glaucoma is increasingly recognized as a multifactorial neurodegenerative optic neuropathy in which intraocular pressure (IOP) is one of several stress amplifiers rather than a sufficient etiological explanation. Vascular dysregulation, mitochondrial and metabolic vulnerability, oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, glial dysregulation, and persistent neuroimmune activation can converge on retinal ganglion cell (RGC) and optic nerve injury. This broader framework is particularly relevant to normal-tension glaucoma (NTG), which represents a large proportion of primary open-angle glaucoma in Asian populations, and to patients who continue to progress despite IOP being maintained within the target range. Consequently, mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolic impairment, oxidative stress, and the associated neuroinflammatory response are not merely downstream adjunctive targets: they are core disease mechanisms and potential treatment targets in pressure-independent and pressure-resistant glaucoma. Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), nicotinamide, pyruvate, and citicoline are emerging metabolic and neuroprotective agents whose primary actions involve mitochondrial bioenergetics, redox homeostasis, membrane integrity, and cellular stress responses, with potential secondary effects on neuroinflammatory pathways. Chronic activation of microglia and astrocytes promotes the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species, and apoptotic mediators, leading to RGC degeneration. CoQ10 may counteract these mechanisms through antioxidant activity and stabilization of mitochondrial function; nicotinamide, a precursor of NAD+, may enhance mitochondrial resilience and suppress inflammatory cascades associated with energy failure; pyruvate supports aerobic metabolism and may limit oxidative, inflammatory, and excitotoxic injury; and citicoline supports membrane homeostasis, phospholipid synthesis, neuronal repair, and visual-pathway function. Combined targeting of these interconnected pathways may enhance RGC resilience independently of, and in addition to, IOP reduction. However, current clinical evidence remains insufficient to establish sustained neuroprotection or disease-modifying efficacy. This review examines the interplay among these mechanisms in glaucomatous neurodegeneration and critically discusses the experimental and clinical evidence supporting metabolic and immunometabolic neuroprotective strategies as potential adjuncts to conventional intraocular pressure-lowering therapy.

The emerging molecular landscape of abdominal aortic aneurysms: non-coding RNAs as diagnostic and therapeutic targets.

New todayReviewMol Biol Rep · Skórka P, Brelik J, Kiełbowski K et al. · Full text
Abstract

An abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a progressive, often asymptomatic condition whose rupture is associated with a high mortality rate. Ultrasonography and computed tomography are established diagnostic methods, while molecular biomarkers may provide additional information about disease activity. This review discusses the potential role of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) as diagnostic and therapeutic tools in AAAs. These molecules, including microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs), are strongly involved in the regulation of gene expression. Through binding to their target mRNAs, miRNAs primarily suppress translation or promote mRNA degradation. Some lncRNAs and circRNAs act as miRNA sponges, affecting their availability. Here, these mechanisms are discussed by pathological function, distinguishing human tissue and circulating studies from animal, cellular and computational findings. Particular attention is given to miR-21, H19, GAS5, circCdyl and miR-26b-3p, which have functional experimental evidence or human biomarker data beyond expression profiling. Abnormal expression can be detected in peripheral blood or aortic tissue, but these findings do not necessarily predict aneurysm growth or rupture. Integration of ncRNA profiling with imaging and clinical parameters may improve the assessment of AAAs. However, prospective validation and further studies of delivery and safety are needed before these molecules can be used in clinical practice.

NDUFAF2 promotes abdominal aortic aneurysm formation through mitochondrial dysfunction-induced apoptosis.

iScience · He H, Peng J, Chen H et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease closely associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis. Here, we found that NDUFAF2 (NADH dehydrogenase (ubiquinone) 1 alpha subcomplex assembly factor 2) was significantly upregulated in cultured cells and murine AAA tissues. Knockdown of NDUFAF2 in vitro attenuated Ang II-induced mitochondrial fragmentation, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and apoptosis in vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), while improving mitochondrial membrane potential and oxygen consumption rate. Abstract

Isolation and cultivation of primary aortic smooth muscle cells (SMCs) provide a valuable approach for investigating cellular mechanisms underlying vascular diseases, including aortic aneurysms. Mouse Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm Model Induced by Perivascular Sheath Microinjection of Chymotrypsin.

J Vis Exp · Shen L, Zeng J, Huang R et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening degenerative vascular disease for which no effective pharmacological therapy currently exists. Reliable and technically accessible animal models are essential for mechanistic and preclinical investigations. However, existing murine platforms are limited by technical complexity, prolonged operative time, or incomplete recapitulation of human AAA pathology. Here, this study presents a novel murine AAA model established by direct perivascular sheath microinjection of chymotrypsin. After midline laparotomy and identification of the infrarenal abdominal aorta, chymotrypsin working solution is injected directly into the perivascular sheath space under direct vision using a microscale needle. To determine the optimal enzyme concentration, a pilot dose-finding study was conducted using 0.125% (w/v; 1.25 mg/mL), 0.25% (w/v; 2.50 mg/mL), and 0.375% (w/v; 3.75 mg/mL) chymotrypsin (n = 5 per group), with PBS as negative control and topical PPE as positive control. Based on aneurysm incidence, aortic diameter progression, and mortality, 0.25% chymotrypsin was selected for the main study. The chymotrypsin group was directly compared with the PPE positive control group to benchmark the pathological features of this novel model. Serial Doppler ultrasonography confirmed a 100% aneurysm induction rate at day 14 in chymotrypsin-treated mice, whereas no dilation occurred in PBS-injected controls. Histological analyses revealed pronounced medial thickening, elastic fiber fragmentation, and collagen deposition, together with markedly elevated immunohistochemical expression of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-2 and MMP-9 in aortic sections. Quantitative morphometry demonstrated significant increases in aortic wall thickness and collagen volume fraction, and a corresponding reduction in elastic fiber content in chymotrypsin-treated animals compared with controls. This protocol thus provides a technically streamlined, highly reproducible, and translationally relevant murine AAA model. Because the procedure avoids aortic dissection, arteriotomy, or specialized microsurgical training, it is well suited to laboratories investigating aneurysm pathobiology or evaluating novel pharmacological interventions.

Geriatr Gerontol Int · Song Z, Son BK, Oura M et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Sarcopenia is widely recognized that affects multiple organs, including the cardiovascular system. Clinical studies have consistently shown associations between sarcopenia and vascular aging phenotypes such as vascular calcification, atherosclerosis, and abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAA). However, the molecular mechanisms by which muscle deterioration contributes to vascular degeneration remain poorly understood. METHODS: To elucidate muscle-vascular crosstalk, we established a murine model combining inflammation-associated muscle atrophy induced by hindlimb immobilization with CaPO₄-induced AAA. Abstract

Ossifying renal tumor of infancy (ORTI) is an exceedingly rare pediatric renal neoplasm with an inherently benign clinical course. Although histomorphologically well-described, its underlying molecular pathogenesis remains largely unknown, often posing diagnostic pitfalls when distinguishing it from malignant mimics such as Wilms tumor and cellular congenital mesoblastic nephroma. Here, we systematically evaluated the clinicopathologic, immunohistochemical, and genomic spectrum of 4 pediatric ORTI cases from male infants aged 0.5 to 10 months (median: 2.5 mo), all presenting with gross hematuria. Histologically, all tumors exhibited a characteristic biphasic architecture composed of an osteoid/osteoblast-rich component intermixed with a bland spindle cell component, accompanied by variable calcification, localized blastema-like foci, and a lobulated growth pattern. By immunohistochemistry, all cases (4/4, 100%) demonstrated strong, diffuse nuclear β-catenin accumulation in both components, alongside diffuse nuclear positivity for WT1 and SATB2, and focal cyclin D1 expression. The Ki-67 proliferation index revealed a distinct gradient, being consistently higher in the spindle cell component (5% to 30%) than in the osteoid areas (2% to 15%). All tumors were negative for pan-TRK, ALK, CD34, S100, and PAX8. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) (WGS/WES) identified a recurrent, somatic activating hotspot mutation in exon 3 of CTNNB1 (c.A121G, p.T41A) in 100% of cases (4/4), perfectly correlating with nuclear β-catenin localization. Secondary alterations in Wnt pathway regulators (APC, AXIN1/AXIN2) and BMPR1A were also detected, while FISH confirmed the absence of ETV6::NTRK3 gene rearrangements. All patients achieved long-term disease-free survival following complete surgical resection (follow-up up to 10 y). In summary, ORTI is a distinct, CTNNB1-driven pediatric renal neoplasm defined by recurrent exon 3 hotspot mutations and aberrant nuclear β-catenin expression. Recognition of these pathognomonic molecular and immunophenotypic hallmarks establishes robust diagnostic criteria to definitively distinguish ORTI from its malignant pediatric renal mimics.

MAP2K1-mutated melanocytic tumors: correlation of histological features with their molecular functional class based on a study of 114 cases.

Mod Pathol · Gadeyne L, Rossi SM, Mansour B et al. · Full text
Abstract

MAP2K1: mutated melanocytic tumors are relatively recently described and show diverse morphological features and complex genetic aberrations which require further characterization. In this study we describe the clinical, histomorphological, immunohistochemical and genetic characteristics of a cohort of 114 MAP2K1-mutated melanocytic neoplasms, including benign (n = 55), intermediate (n = 34) and malignant (n = 25) tumors, collected from different institutions, harboring Class I (RAF-dependent) (n = 16), Class II (RAF-regulated) (n = 59) and/or Class III (RAF-independent) (n = 44) MAP2K1 mutations. Five lesions exhibited dual MAP2K1 mutations, all involving 2 different functional classes. Benign melanocytic lesions with Class II/III MAP2K1 mutations show reproducible morphologic patterns, compatible with the assumption that the MAP2K1 variant is the molecular driver mutation in these lesions. These melanocytic lesions fall into a morphological spectrum which include either mainly dermal-based lesions with combined (nevoid to spitzoid) cytonuclear characteristics and associated desmoplastic stroma (22%), or mainly junctionally-located lesions with spitzoid Clark (SPARK) morphology (18%), as well as lesions with overlapping features (6%). A minority of lesions show a dysplastic nevus (4%) or desmoplastic Spitz nevus morphology (3%), that could represent the ends of this morphologic spectrum. Chromosome 15 amplifications and dual MAP2K1 mutations were demonstrated in a subset of lesions with the combined desmoplastic morphology. Furthermore, we describe MAP2K1-driven melanocytic lesions with secondary well-recognized pathogenic mutations, including WNT-activated melanocytomas (12%), but also a new, genetically and morphologically heterogenous group of melanocytomas, most often harboring Class II MAP2K1 mutations in conjunction with RAF-activating co-mutations (11%). In MAP2K1-driven melanoma, we demonstrate the presence of secondary mutations in classical progression genes, such as TERT promoter and CDKN2A, and a plexiform dermal, superficial spreading or desmoplastic melanoma morphology. Lastly, lesions harboring only Class I MAP2K1 mutations are mostly melanoma, commonly have RAF-activating co-mutations and show a heterogeneous morphological phenotype.

Adamantinoma-like Ewing Sarcoma: An Underrecognized Mimicker in the Head and Neck With Divergent Clinicopathologic Features from Ewing Sarcoma.

Mod Pathol · Amin SE, Learned KO, Ijaz K et al. · Full text
Abstract

Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma (ALES) is a cytokeratin-positive member of the Ewing sarcoma (ES) family with a predilection for the head and neck (HN). Its rarity and overlapping immunophenotype with carcinomas contribute to frequent misclassification, while its biologic behavior and response to standard ES therapy remain incompletely defined. We conducted a 25-year retrospective review to characterize the clinicopathologic spectrum of ALES and compare HN ALES with non-HN ALES and HN ES, incorporating histologic, immunophenotypic, molecular, and clinical outcome data. Twenty-three cases were identified; ALES (n=20) and HN ES (n=3). ALES occurred in older patients (median age 49 vs. 29 years) and demonstrated a marked HN predilection (17/20), with a substantial proportion involving glandular sites. In contrast, HN ES arose in the skull base and sinonasal tract. ALES showed basaloid, epithelioid, or round cell morphology, with occasional palisading and rare squamous differentiation. Diffuse cytokeratin expression was present in all ALES, with frequent p40/p63 positivity, whereas ES showed only focal cytokeratin staining. Nodal metastases occurred in 9/20 ALES cases. EWSR1 rearrangement was identified in all ALES and ES cases, with FLI1 fusion identified in 18/20 ALES and 1/3 ES cases. With a mean follow-up of 60.6 months, outcomes varied by site: salivary gland ALES showed a more favorable course (4/5 in remission), whereas thyroid (6/8) and non-HN ALES (3/3) more often progressed. HN ES demonstrated poor outcomes. ALES is a distinct entity that frequently mimics carcinoma and extends beyond classic morphologic patterns. Site-specific differences observed in our study may aid in diagnosis; however, accurate recognition remains difficult, and a high index of suspicion at specific anatomical sites may be important for diagnosis and management.

Genomic and Transcriptomic Characterization of Angiosarcomas: Focus on Cardiac Origin.

Mod Pathol · Lu T, Liu Y, Yuan M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Cardiac angiosarcoma is a rare and aggressive malignancy with limited therapeutic options, and its molecular features remain poorly defined. This study sought to characterize the genomic and transcriptomic landscape of cardiac angiosarcoma and to explore molecular features associated with clinical outcomes, chemotherapy response, and potential therapeutic vulnerabilities. We retrospectively analyzed 35 patients with cardiac angiosarcoma who underwent whole-exome sequencing (n = 11) and/or whole-transcriptome sequencing (n = 33). An additional 35 patients with angiosarcoma from noncardiac or unknown primary sites were included for comparative genomic analyses. Cardiac angiosarcoma frequently harbored alterations in KDR (55%), PLCG1 (45%), and CCND1 (36%), with recurrent amplifications at 8p23.1, 8p23.2, and 4q12 and recurrent deletions at 17q21.31, 15q13.2, 8p23.1, and 2q11.2. Transcriptomic analyses identified differential expression patterns associated with relapse, mortality, and chemotherapy response. Tumors from the low-relapse-risk group were enriched for axon guidance, whereas those from the high-relapse-risk group were enriched for cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. Approximately one-third of patients exhibited high infiltration of most immune cell populations. Potentially actionable genomic alterations were identified in 71.7% of angiosarcomas. Compared with noncardiac angiosarcomas, cardiac tumors showed higher frequencies of KDR, CCND1, MCL1, and PLCG1 alterations and unique enrichment of the apelin signaling and vascular smooth muscle contraction pathways. Genomic and transcriptomic profiling of cardiac angiosarcoma revealed a distinct molecular landscape with candidate prognostic and therapeutic markers, providing a foundation for future precision oncology strategies in this rare disease.

Dermal Nerve Sheath Myxomas Harbor Recurrent DPYSL2::CDX1 Fusions and Expand the Spectrum of CDX1-Rearranged Soft Tissue Tumors.

Mod Pathol · Dehner CA, Warmke LM, Jasmine SW et al. · Full text
Abstract

Dermal nerve sheath myxoma (DNSM) is a rare benign mesenchymal tumor with presumed nerve sheath differentiation with distinctive morphologic and immunophenotypic features; however, its molecular pathogenesis remains poorly understood. We investigated a series of nine morphologically classic DNSMs and performed molecular testing to identify recurrent genetic alterations. The cohort included five female and four male patients (median age: 56 years; range: 23-75 years). Tumors arose predominantly on the extremities and presented as multinodular dermal-based myxoid neoplasms composed of uniform spindled to epithelioid cells arranged in reticular cords within abundant myxoid stroma. Vacuolated "signet ring-like" cells were identified in all tumors. Immunohistochemically, all tested tumors showed diffuse S100, SOX10 and GFAP expression, and a subset focally expressed EMA, keratin AE1/AE3, and GLUT1. RNA-based sequencing identified an identical in-frame DPYSL2::CDX1 fusion in 7 of 9 cases. Fluorescence in situ hybridization testing using CDX1 and DPYSL2 break apart probes as well as DPYSL2/CDX1 dual-fusion probe confirmed rearrangement in 6/7 tested tumors. We herein demonstrate that most DNSM are characterized by recurrent DPYSL2::CDX1 fusions, representing the first recurrent molecular alteration identified in this tumor type. These findings further support the classification of DNSM as a distinct neoplastic entity and highlight the potential utility of molecular testing as a diagnostic adjunct in morphologically challenging cases. Further studies are warranted to clarify the biologic role of CDX1-related fusions and their relationship to other fusion-driven soft tissue neoplasms, particularly those with CDX1/CDX2 fusions.

Musculoskeletal Histopathology of Klippel-Trénaunay Syndrome: A Clinicopathologic Study of 21 Cases With Long-Term Orthopedic Follow-Up.

Am J Surg Pathol · Sharma AE, Odintsov I, Hung YP et al. · Full text
Abstract

Klippel-Trénaunay syndrome (KTS) is a rare congenital vascular-overgrowth disorder within the broader spectrum of PIK3CA-related overgrowth syndromes. Although clinical, radiologic, and orthopedic manifestations are well described, the histologic spectrum of KTS-associated pathology remains under-characterized. Clinicopathologic findings from 21 patients with clinically established KTS were reviewed, including 18 with longitudinal clinical follow-up and 3 consultation cases of rare malignant mesenchymal neoplasms. Cases were identified from institutional pathology and clinical databases, and available hematoxylin and eosin-stained sections and ancillary studies were reviewed. The cohort included 11 males and 10 females. Among the 18 clinically followed patients, 12 had limb length discrepancy or asymmetric limb overgrowth, 9 had documented deep vascular malformations, 4 had intra-articular/synovial malformations, and 10 underwent initial orthopedic evaluation or intervention before age 18. Orthopedic morbidity clustered into mechanical overgrowth-related, vascular malformation-related, and soft tissue/wound-related complications. Histologic findings included superficial and/or deep slow-flow vascular malformations, ulceration and granulation tissue, hemosiderin-laden reactive synovium, and destructive hemorrhagic arthropathy. Intra-articular disease recapitulated a hemophilic arthropathy-like pattern, with hemosiderotic synovitis, papillary synovial hyperplasia, and progressive joint destruction with loss of articular cartilage, exposed subchondral bone destruction and remodeling, all attributable to long-standing intra-articular hemorrhage. Three consultation cases showed malignant mesenchymal neoplasia, including 2 angiosarcomas. Recognition of these patterns is important for pathologists evaluating specimens from patients with known or suspected KTS, particularly when findings mimic hemophilic arthropathy, diffuse-type tenosynovial giant cell tumor, nonsyndromic vascular malformation, and/or end-stage destructive arthropathy.

Recurrent PDGFRB-Mutations in the Epithelioid Variant of Fibrous Papules.

Am J Surg Pathol · Kervarrec T, Alsugair Z, Macagno N et al. · Full text
Abstract

Fibrous papule (FP) is a common benign dermal mesenchymal lesion presenting as a solitary, dome-shaped papule mostly located on the nose. The morphologic spectrum is broad, and a number of variants have been described, including hypercellular, clear cell, pigmented, pleomorphic, granular, inflammatory, and epithelioid. The latter is very rare. In the current study, we present a comprehensive morphologic, immunohistochemical, and molecular characterization of 16 additional cases of epithelioid fibrous papule (EFP). The cohort included 7 females and 9 males, with a median age at diagnosis of 42 years (range: 7 to 83 y). Anatomic distribution showed predilection for the head and neck region (n=14). Microscopically, all lesions presented as a dome-shaped, well-circumscribed dermal proliferation composed of epithelioid and spindle-shaped cells arranged in nests or individual units and embedded in an abundant eosinophilic stroma. Neoplastic cells displayed uniform morphology with abundant pale cytoplasm and medium-sized nuclei. Focal immunoreactivity for CD68 and CD163 was detected in 7 and 5 cases, respectively. One case each exhibited an in-frame deletion, p.R561_R565del and p.W566_S574delinsC, respectively. In summary, epithelioid fibrous papule is characterised by distinct morphologic features and harbours recurrent, activating PDGFRB mutations.

Vulvovaginal Epithelioid Mesenchymal Neoplasm With "Deciduoid" Morphology: Report of a Novel Entity Frequently Associated With Inactivating Germline BAP1 Variants.

Am J Surg Pathol · Rókusz A, Attygalle AD, Melegh Z et al. · Full text
Abstract

A wide variety of mesenchymal lesions arise in the vulvovaginal region, some relatively site-specific. We report 4 cases of a novel vulvovaginal mesenchymal lesion, which we term vulvovaginal epithelioid mesenchymal neoplasm with "deciduoid" morphology, reflecting the predominant morphologic feature of epithelioid cells with a granular glassy "deciduoid" appearance. The tumors arose in women aged 34 to 48 years and involved the vagina (2 cases) or the vulval Bartholin's gland region (2 cases). The size ranged from 25 to 55 mm and they were solid and well-circumscribed. The tumors were composed of epithelioid and lesser numbers of spindle-shaped cells with abundant granular glassy eosinophilic cytoplasm, imparting a "deciduoid" morphology; there was prominent vascularity and a mild lymphoid infiltrate. Immunohistochemistry showed consistent diffuse estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, CD63, and vimentin positivity with no other consistent immunophenotype. Two of the tumors showed immunohistochemical BAP1 loss and exhibited inactivating germline BAP1 variants (BAP1 tumor predisposition syndrome). On molecular testing, all tumors showed copy number loss of genes in 14q and/or 17q regions. Alterations of genes involved in the MAPK pathway were also present in all cases (including NF1 loss, a likely pathogenic variant in FLT3, and variants of unknown significance in FLT3, BMPR1A, and RUNX1). One of the cases harbored a novel FBXL20::MSI2 fusion. On follow-up (8 mo to 15 y), none of the tumors recurred. In reporting this novel neoplasm, we describe in detail the morphologic, immunohistochemical, and molecular features, discuss the differential diagnosis and histogenesis, and establish this novel entity as part of the BAP1 tumor predisposition syndrome.
