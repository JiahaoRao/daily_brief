Colloids Surf B Biointerfaces · Mao X, Wang W, Zhu X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is characterized by metabolic plasticity and redox dysregulation, yet therapeutic outcomes remain constrained by adaptive resistance. Herein, a Pt/Ti₃C₂ MXene-based multifunctional nanozyme (TP@MH) was developed to enhance mild-temperature photothermal therapy (mPTT) through combined catalytic/redox regulation and metabolic intervention. Platinum nanoparticles were in situ anchored onto Ti₃C₂ nanosheets to construct a catalytic heterostructure, followed by mitoxantrone (MTO) loading and hyaluronic acid (HA)-mediated CD44-targeting. The obtained TP@MH exhibited peroxidase and GSH-depleting capability, promoting ROS generation and perturbing tumor redox homeostasis. Upon near-infrared irradiation, TP@MH nanozyme not only modulated the apparent POD-like kinetics under the tested conditions but also perturbed intracellular NAD⁺/NADH metabolism and reduced ATP availability, which was associated with decreased HSP70 expression and potentially weakened cellular thermal adaptation. MTO provided an additional chemotherapeutic component, while TP@MH treatment induced mitochondrial dysfunction, enhanced lipid peroxidation, reduced glutathione peroxidase 4 expression, and promoted apoptosis and ferroptosis. Both in vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated that TP@MH achieved efficient antitumor efficacy through the integrated effects of mild photothermal activation, redox regulation, metabolic intervention, and chemotherapy. Collectively, this work provides a multifunctional nanozyme strategy for integrating catalytic, metabolic, photothermal, and chemotherapeutic interventions in RCC therapy.

Aging Dis · Ali U, Tan R, Ding S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Immune aging is characterized by inflammaging, progressive immune decline, and compromised tissue surveillance. Although researchers have recognized an association between PARP hyperactivation and immune aging (emerging and context-dependent), a unifying framework for how PARP members beyond PARP1 orchestrate immune dysfunction via NAD+ metabolism is lacking. We propose a context-dependent PARP-CD38-NAD+-inflammaging hierarchy. PARP1 is the most abundant nuclear PARP, responsible for 85-90% of nuclear PARP activity and dominant NAD+ consumption under acute DNA damage; CD38, the principal NAD+ glycohydrolase, is upregulated in inflamed aged tissues; NAD+ is compartmentalized (cytosolic, mitochondrial) with distinct regulators (NAMPT, NMNAT1/2/3, SLC25A51). Recent human data challenge the universal NAD+ decline hypothesis, showing that an 85% muscle NAD+ reduction does not impair function, and whole-blood NAD+ is age-invariant. Thus, NAD+ decline is tissue- and compartment-specific; blood NAD+ is not a reliable biomarker. Beyond PARP1, PARP2 (cardiomyocyte aging in mouse), PARP3 (intestinal aging), PARP16 (vascular aging), and PARP9/14 (Alzheimer's models) act as tissue-specific regulators, but their roles in human immune aging remain unquantified. PARP inhibitors and NAD+ precursors show promise but face risks: genomic instability, hematopoietic toxicity, senescence promotion, and inconsistent efficacy. Unresolved questions, whether PBMC PARP activity predicts immune aging, sex differences, and whether partial inhibition reduces NAD+ consumption without compromising DNA repair. By rethinking PARPs as context-dependent integrators, we provide a framework for immunogerontology.

Abstract

Repetitive mild traumatic brain injury (rmTBI) may arise when concussive or subconcussive impacts recur before physiological recovery is complete. Testosterone replacement therapy and aneurysm biology: current evidence and clinical implications.

ReviewIndian J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg · de Oliveira Manduca Palmiero H, Gadelha Figueiredo E · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) is increasingly used to treat male hypogonadism, but its role in aneurysm biology remains unclear. Because aneurysm formation is driven by inflammation, extracellular matrix degradation, and vascular remodeling, testosterone may influence disease progression through androgen receptor-mediated pathways. METHODS: A narrative literature review was conducted using PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Google Scholar (August-September 2025). Studies examining testosterone, TRT, vascular inflammation, and aneurysm-related outcomes, including experimental, preclinical, and clinical studies, were included. EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS: Available evidence is mostly mechanistic and preclinical. Animal studies indicate vascular bed-specific effects: physiological androgen signaling seems protective in abdominal aortic aneurysm models by reducing macrophage infiltration and inflammatory cytokine signaling, whereas exogenous or supraphysiologic testosterone may exacerbate oxidative stress and vascular injury in intracranial aneurysm models. Human data remain indirect, with no prospective studies specifically assessing aneurysm formation, growth, or rupture in TRT users. Large randomized trials and meta-analyses indicate cardiovascular safety comparable to placebo in appropriately selected patients, though higher rates of atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism, and erythrocytosis have been reported. CONCLUSION: Testosterone has context-, dose-, and vascular bed-specific effects on aneurysm biology. Until clinical studies on aneurysm outcomes are available, TRT should be individualized, dosed to meet physiological needs, and closely monitored for cardiovascular and vascular health. No evidence-based recommendation can currently be made to initiate, withhold, or modify TRT solely on the basis of aneurysm status. Multidisciplinary discussion and continued standard aneurysm surveillance are advisable for hypogonadal patients with known aneurysmal disease.

New todayEur J Vasc Endovasc Surg · Prendes CF, Booth M, Young L et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: The aim of this study was to characterise contemporary deep venous interventions (DVIs) performed within the English National Health Service (NHS), describing procedural mix, patient heterogeneity, anatomic distribution, and short and midterm outcomes. METHODS: National data on DVIs performed between 1 June 2020 and 31 May 2023 were extracted from the National Consultant Information Programme. Percutaneous transluminal venous procedures (PTVs) and venous thrombolysis were identified using pre-defined procedural and diagnostic code hierarchies. All cases underwent structured patient level validation to ensure concordance between indication, treated venous anatomy, and procedural intent. The primary outcomes were ninety day and one year mortality. Secondary outcomes included procedural volume variation. Re-interventions were recorded descriptively. RESULTS: In total, 2 339 DVIs were performed, of which 1 617 PTVs and 604 venous thrombolysis procedures were validated. Patient cohorts were heterogeneous, most commonly chronic kidney disease (CKD) (41.8%), chronic venous insufficiency (CVI) (16.0%), and venous compression syndromes (14.0%). Thrombolysis was most frequently performed for lower extremity venous thrombosis and was associated with low short and midterm mortality in this group. CONCLUSION: This service level analysis of DVIs in England demonstrates distinct clinical populations with marked differences in anatomy, indication, and outcomes that vary substantially by indication and comorbidity. Mortality was lowest in elective primary lower limb venous disease and highest in patients with advanced systemic illness such as malignancy and renal disease. Future work should focus on indication specific venous registries and risk adjusted outcomes to aid benchmarking, service level organisation, and patient care.

New todayReviewCancer Invest · Chang L, Sun Y, Wang Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) develops in a microbe-rich, injury-prone mucosa in which coagulation, inflammation, and tissue repair are closely coupled. Tumor and host cells can activate tissue factor-thrombin signaling, platelets, fibrin turnover, extracellular vesicles, and neutrophil extracellular traps, thereby reshaping myeloid-cell recruitment, cytotoxic immunity, angiogenesis, and invasion. Abstract

Fujita Med J · Sato Y, Nishio E, Oikawa S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Pregnancy-associated venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a major cause of maternal morbidity and mortality. Pregnancies achieved by in vitro fertilization carry an increased risk of VTE, particularly during early gestation. A 33-year-old woman developed deep vein thrombosis during late pregnancy in her first spontaneous conception despite no identifiable thrombophilia. During a subsequent pregnancy achieved by natural cycle frozen embryo transfer, which was selected to avoid exogenous estrogen exposure and potentially reduce the thrombotic risk compared with hormone replacement cycles, she developed recurrent VTE at 8 weeks of gestation. Inflammation and thrombosis: from pathophysiology to future treatments.

Intern Emerg Med · Santagata D, Giovenali L, Guerrieri E et al. · Full text
Abstract

Inflammation and coagulation are tightly interconnected host-defense systems that, when dysregulated, promote pathological thrombosis. This narrative review examines mechanisms, clinical settings, and therapeutic implications of thromboinflammation (TI) across venous, arterial, and microvascular thrombosis, with particular attention to the differences in clinical translation among vascular territories. Endothelial activation, tissue factor expression, platelet-leukocyte interactions, neutrophil extracellular traps, complement activation, inflammasome signalling, and impaired fibrinolysis converge to generate a procoagulant phenotype. ReviewCEN Case Rep · Sutanto R, Koesbandono , Masui M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Renal vein thrombosis (RVT) is a rare and underrecognized complication of acute pyelonephritis, with nephrotic syndrome and malignancy remaining the predominant causes of RVT. Because its clinical features overlap with those of the underlying infection, the diagnosis is often delayed until thrombus propagation or embolization occurs. A previously healthy 29-year-old man presented with a two-week history of left flank pain and fever. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated acute left pyelonephritis with extensive renal vein thrombosis extending into the suprarenal inferior vena cava. Human papillomavirus (HPV)-associated non-salivary adenocarcinoma of the oropharynx is exceptionally rare and poorly defined. We report a multi-institutional series of 12 cases from 6 tertiary centers. Histologic, immunohistochemical, HPV testing, molecular, and follow-up data were reviewed. Among 11 patients with available sex data, all were men; the median age was 62 years (range: 46 to 70 y). The cohort comprised 9 primary oropharyngeal adenocarcinomas and 3 metastatic adenocarcinomas, including a right neck metastasis of presumed oropharyngeal origin and 2 distant metastases in patients with previously documented HPV-associated oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma. Primary sites included the palatine tonsil or tonsil-palate region (n=7), base of tongue (n=1), and oropharynx, not otherwise specified (n=1). Among cases with available T-category, 5 of 6 were T3/T4. All tumors showed high-grade adenocarcinoma morphology without a recognizable squamous component, with infiltrative glandular, ductal, or cribriform architecture. Diffuse p16 expression was present in all cases, and high-risk HPV was confirmed by polymerase chain reaction or in situ hybridization. p40 was negative in the tumor cell population in cases 1 to 6, 8, 9, 11, and 12, whereas case 7 showed p40 and CK5/6 expression confined to abluminal cells. Molecular profiling in 2 cases identified alterations or variants involving PIK3CA and PIK3R1. Follow-up in 6 patients with primary tumors showed distant metastases in 3 and disease-specific death in 1. HPV-associated nonsalivary adenocarcinoma of the oropharynx is a diagnostically challenging tumor that expands the morphologic spectrum of HPV-related oropharyngeal carcinoma. Recognition requires exclusion of salivary-type and metastatic adenocarcinoma and confirmation of HPV association.

Recurrent Activating PDGFRB Mutations in Fibroblastic Connective Tissue Nevus: Reappraisal of Its Relationship to Dermatomyofibroma.

Am J Surg Pathol · Odintsov I, Wang C, Hanna J · Full text
Abstract

Fibroblastic connective tissue nevus is an uncommon benign cutaneous lesion first described in 2012. It is characterized by an irregularly disposed intradermal spindle cell proliferation and is sometimes associated with papillomatous epidermal hyperplasia and/or admixed adipose tissue. Fibroblastic connective tissue nevus shows some morphologic overlap with a second benign cutaneous lesion known as dermatomyofibroma, and some authors have speculated that the 2 lesions might represent a morphologic spectrum of a single entity. Recurrent activating mutations in the cell-surface receptor tyrosine kinase PDGFRB have recently been identified in dermatomyofibroma. Here, we identified similar recurrent activating PDGFRB mutations in fibroblastic connective tissue nevus, supporting the notion that the 2 lesions are indeed closely related. Direct comparison of the 2 lesions suggests that the most reliable distinguishing characteristics are the lesional architecture and immunoprofile. Whereas fibroblastic connective tissue nevus typically shows an irregular or haphazard architecture, dermatomyofibroma typically shows a horizontal band-like arrangement relative to the overlying epidermis. Immunohistochemically, fibroblastic connective tissue nevus shows frequent expression of CD34 with relative lack of SMA, while dermatomyofibroma shows frequent SMA expression with much less frequent CD34. These results provide insight into the relationship between fibroblastic connective tissue nevus and dermatomyofibroma, and place fibroblastic connective tissue nevus in the broader context of dermal spindle cell neoplasms characterized by activation of the PDGFB pathway.
