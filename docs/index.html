Bifunctional Pt/CeO2 Nanozyme-Mediated Metabolic Regulation Promotes NAD+ Regeneration and ROS Clearance to Ameliorate Age-Related Osteoporosis.

ACS Appl Mater Interfaces · Zhang H, Yang C, Zhou Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Age-related osteoporosis is characterized by progressive loss of bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, driven by chronic inflammation, redox imbalance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and osteoblast senescence that uncouples bone formation from resorption. During aging, declining nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) availability further exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and stress responses, suggesting that restoring NAD+ redox homeostasis could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Here, we reported a bifunctional nanozyme-mediated metabolic regulation strategy to promote NAD+ regeneration and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) for the treatment of age-related osteoporosis. The bifunctional nanozyme (Pt/CeO2) was constructed by in situ growth of platinum nanoparticles on cerium oxide nanorods and was designed for ROS scavenging and NAD+ regeneration via NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) oxidation. In senescent osteoblasts, Pt/CeO2 reduced ROS, restored mitochondrial integrity, and alleviated senescence, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation. In vivo results confirmed that systemic administration of Pt/CeO2 preserved trabecular architecture and increased bone mineral density in naturally aged mice. These findings demonstrated that coupling ROS scavenging with the restoration of NAD+ redox homeostasis via Pt/CeO2 nanozyme provided a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract osteoblast senescence and slow the progression of age-related osteoporosis.

Dual-layer regulation of macrophage grancalcin links diabetic redox-metabolic imbalance to impaired vascularized bone regeneration.

Redox Biol · Jin S, Song Z, Fang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Effective bone regeneration requires the timely transition of immune cells from inflammatory defense to regenerative support. However, how diabetic redox-metabolic stress sustains maladaptive immune states and drives regenerative failure remains incompletely understood. Herein, using a mouse jawbone defect model combined with single-cell RNA sequencing, metabolic assays and human peri-extraction gingival specimens, we identified a senescence-associated macrophage state characterized by elevated grancalcin (GCA). Functionally, recombinant GCA impaired osteogenic and endothelial responses, whereas GCA neutralization partially reversed the inhibitory effects of diabetic macrophage-conditioned medium, supporting extracellular GCA as a functional mediator of regenerative dysfunction. Mechanistically, diabetic macrophages exhibited enhanced glycolytic activity, impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and a reduced NAD+/NADH ratio. This redox imbalance lowered SIRT6 abundance, increased H3K9ac/H3K56ac enrichment at the accessible Gca regulatory locus and sustained Gca transcription. Meanwhile, hyperglycemia-triggered Ca2+ signaling promoted GCA recruitment to membrane-proximal regions and increased both total extracellular and extracellular vesicle-associated GCA. Local delivery of quercetin using a β-cyclodextrin/GelMA hydrogel attenuated macrophage GCA dysregulation, reduced senescence-associated macrophage features and improved vascularized jawbone regeneration. Collectively, this work defines a dual-layer paradigm controlling extracellular GCA bioavailability, thereby linking diabetic redox-metabolic perturbation to defective bone regeneration and supporting local targeting of macrophage GCA for diabetic bone-healing therapy.

Neurovascular Unit Redox-Metabolic Communication in Poststroke Cognitive Impairment: Spatiotemporal Dysregulation, Cellular Crosstalk, and Precision Intervention.

ReviewEur J Neurosci · Zhang Q, Chen Y, Bai M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is a dynamic syndrome driven by focal brain injury, pre-existing cerebrovascular burden, metabolic comorbidities, and reduced brain resilience. Increasing evidence suggests that persistent disruption of redox-metabolic communication within the neurovascular unit (NVU), rather than neuronal loss alone, promotes the transition from acute ischemic injury to chronic cognitive decline. This review summarizes metabolic reprogramming in BMECs, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, and neurons, emphasizing the intercellular transfer of metabolites, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondria. We propose that PSCI develops through a spatiotemporally dysregulated continuum involving vascular injury, cell-specific metabolic remodeling, maladaptive signal propagation, impaired axonal and synaptic energy support, and progressive network disconnection. Ischemia-reperfusion triggers bioenergetic failure, NADH/NAD+ imbalance, mitochondrial electron leakage, endothelial dysfunction, and capillary no-reflow. Subsequently, blood-brain barrier disruption, immunometabolic activation, iron and lipid dysregulation, and defective organelle quality control sustain white matter and synaptic injury. Therapeutic strategies should therefore be stage-specific: restoring microcirculatory perfusion and physiological redox signaling acutely, promoting inflammation resolution and metabolic repair subacutely, and combining vascular-metabolic modulation with neurorehabilitation chronically. Future studies should integrate cell type-specific interventions, metabolic flux tracing, spatial multi-omics, and longitudinal cognitive assessments to establish causal donor-carrier-recipient-stage-outcome relationships.

Renal tubule-targeted biomimetic nanodrugs achieve precise AKI treatment by halting pro-inflammatory crosstalk between proximal tubular epithelial cells and macrophages.

J Nanobiotechnology · Yang P, Zhao L, Yang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a critical clinical disorder characterized by high incidence, high mortality, and numerous complications. However, current supportive therapies cannot reverse established injury, leading to poor outcomes in some patients, and thus, necessitating the development of active therapeutic strategies. Here, we analyzed a clinical single-nucleus RNA sequencing dataset and found that a pathological feedback loop between injured proximal tubular epithelial cells (PTECs) and macrophages drives AKI progression. To simultaneously target this maladaptive crosstalk, we developed N@CM2c, a biomimetic nanodrug composed of NAD⁺ and CCR2-enriched M2c macrophage membranes. By integrating targeted delivery, metabolic reprogramming, and immunomodulation, N@CM2c presents a promising application to halt AKI progression and promote renal repair.

Long COVID, chronic fatigue syndrome, and chronic lyme disease as Post-acute Infection Syndromes (PAIS): a translational synthesis.

New todayReviewJ Transl Med · Sewell PE, Reddy R · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Post-acute infection syndromes (PAIS) comprise a heterogeneous group of chronic, multisystem conditions characterized by persistent or relapsing symptoms following resolution of an acute infectious illness, in the absence of ongoing pathogen replication detectable by standard diagnostics. Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), Long COVID, post-treatment Lyme disease syndrome, and related entities are increasingly recognized as part of a shared post-infectious disease spectrum. The COVID-19 pandemic has markedly expanded the global burden of PAIS and intensified investigation into their biological underpinnings. Recent genome-wide association studies support heritable susceptibility involving immune regulation, mitochondrial maintenance, and neuroinflammatory pathways. Diagnostic paradigms remain primarily clinical, though emerging multi-omic and cellular biomarkers show translational promise. Tiered diagnostic and evaluation strategies are discussed to guide clinicians while minimizing harm, particularly post-exertional malaise. CONCLUSIONS: PAIS represent biologically grounded, systems-level disorders with shared mechanisms and heterogeneous clinical expression. Integrating molecular insights with pragmatic clinical frameworks may accelerate development of precision-oriented diagnostic and therapeutic strategies to address the growing global impact of post-infectious chronic illness.

Novel Proteolyzed Aortic Extracellular Matrix Substrate Coating as a Model to Assess Aneurysmal Smooth Muscle Cell Behavior In Vitro.

New todayTissue Eng Part A · Krajewski T, Ramamurthi A · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAAs) are localized expansions of the abdominal aortic wall that grow slowly to potentially fatal rupture. AAAs result from irreversible loss of structural extracellular matrix (ECM) due to upregulated proteolytic activity. Surgery to treat AAAs is risky; therefore, new drug-based approaches are needed to slow aneurysm growth by reversing ECM pathophysiology. This necessitates the study of disease-relevant cells in vitro, in this case smooth muscle cells (SMCs); however, current in vitro models are limited due to SMC dedifferentiation on conventional plastic substrates, which cause a loss of disease-relevant features. This study generated a physiologically relevant culture model to assess matrix regenerative and reparative responses of AAA SMCs. This model system offers a promising platform for screening aneurysmal SMC responses to therapeutics similar to expected in vivo outcomes.

AMOTL2: A key regulator of mechanotransduction in development, vascular disease and cancer.

ReviewCells Dev · Fonseca P, Zhang Y, Holmgren L · Full text
Abstract

Mechanical forces play a fundamental role in regulating cell behavior during development, tissue homeostasis, and disease. The angiomotin family protein AmotL2 has emerged as a central integrator of mechanotransduction, linking cell-cell adhesion, cytoskeletal tension, and nuclear architecture to transcriptional control. Through its ability to connect cadherin-based junctions to radial actin filaments and the nuclear lamina, AmotL2 establishes a continuous mechanical axis that enables cells to sense and respond to extracellular forces. This function is essential for epithelial organization, vascular morphogenesis, and endothelial responses to shear stress, in part through regulation of Hippo pathway effectors such as YAP and TAZ. Dysregulation of AmotL2 contributes to pathological states, including abdominal aortic aneurysm, where reduced AmotL2 impairs endothelial mechanotransduction, and cancer, where induction of the p60AmotL2 isoform disrupts polarity, alters nuclear mechanics, and promotes invasive behavior. Emerging evidence further suggests that AmotL2 regulates transcription through direct effects on nuclear structure and chromatin accessibility, positioning it as a key regulator of force-dependent gene expression. In this review, we summarize the structural and mechanistic basis of AmotL2 function and discuss its roles in development, vascular biology, and cancer, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target in diseases driven by altered mechanotransduction.

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a fatal vascular disease lacking effective pharmacological treatments. METHODS: Integrated bioinformatics analysis was performed on the GSE51227 dataset to identify MRDEGs, followed by protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis to screen hub genes. Developing structure and strategies for analysis of circulating biomarkers to detect and monitor abdominal aortic aneurysms.

New todayAm J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol · Lindsey ML, Shivam P, Nde PN et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a multifactorial disease characterized by progressive remodeling of the aortic wall. Current clinical management includes screening ultrasound for diagnosis, and routine aortic imaging to monitor growth in patients with small AAA and surgical exclusion of larger AAA. With the evolution of a diverse array of multianalyte assessments from peripheral blood, comprehensive assessments of circulating biomarkers in patients with AAA is possible. In the context of this dynamic disease process, circulating markers - paired with clinical and imaging data - provide an opportunity to identify specific active biologic processes that influence the natural history of the disease. We propose a disease-specific framework for understanding relationships between circulating markers and imaging within a novel three class structure. Class I associations correlate with aortic size and presence of AAA and may be related to the mechanisms of initial formation. Class II associations track with change in aneurysm MTD and may be related to mechanisms of AAA growth and rupture risk. Class II may be clinically relevant for the identification of targets for modification that could alter the natural history of established AAA. Class III associations relate to static or dynamic morphologic features such as mural thrombus or calcifications that have less well-defined associations with clinical outcomes. Identified and appropriately classified markers can provide specific avenues for novel therapeutic and diagnostic options to reduce morbidity and mortality in patients with AAA.

Sleep, stasis, and thromboprotection: what hibernating mammals can teach us about cardiovascular risk.

New todayReviewPlatelets · Zauli E, Caruso L, Zauli G et al. · Full text
Abstract

Prolonged immobility is one of the best-known triggers for venous blood clots, yet hibernating brown bears spend months lying still without ever developing them - and something similar seems to hold for many people with chronic paralysis. Thienel and colleagues recently offered a compelling explanation: platelets in hibernating bears sharply downregulate a protein called HSP47, dropping to roughly 55-fold below normal levels. HSP47 turns out to drive clot formation on two fronts at once, by boosting platelet activation and by prompting neutrophils to release the web-like structures that help clots take hold. Suppress it, and both arms of the process weaken together. That this pattern shows up across bears, immobilized patients, bed-rest volunteers, and other animal models suggests it isn't a quirk of bear physiology but something closer to an ancient survival mechanism. Here we consider why immobility's clotting risk seems to shift over time, look at an HSP47-blocking compound called Col003 that is already showing promise, and ask whether HSP47 might eventually double as a marker of clotting and inflammatory risk more broadly.

Haemostasis in Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease.

New todayReviewLiver Int · Ferdinande K, Senzolo M, Campello E et al. · Full text
Abstract

MASLD is the most prevalent chronic liver disease and a significant cause of liver-related morbidity and mortality. Portal hypertension may develop before advanced fibrosis, driven by vascular and metabolic alterations that concurrently affect systemic haemostasis and promote intrahepatic interactions between endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and haemostatic pathways. Once cirrhosis is established, the rebalanced haemostatic state is largely independent of aetiology, with impaired fibrinolysis representing the principal MASLD-associated difference. In non-cirrhotic MASLD, increasing in vivo platelet activation, a progressive procoagulant imbalance between factor VIII and protein C, and reduced fibrinolysis, including increased plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 and prolonged clot lysis, have been described, although their attribution to MASLD is complicated by the concomitant metabolic factors. Clinically, MASLD is associated with increased risks of venous thromboembolism, portal vein thrombosis, and atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, increasing with disease severity; however, prospective data linking specific haemostatic alterations to incident thrombosis remain lacking. Within the liver, platelets act as central hubs interacting with dysfunctional liver sinusoidal endothelial cells, hepatic stellate cells, Kupffer cells, and hepatocytes, orchestrating platelet-neutrophil crosstalk, neutrophil extracellular trap formation, and coagulation activation, thereby promoting immunothrombosis, hepatic stellate cell activation, microvascular remodelling, and fibrogenesis. Coagulation proteases may additionally contribute to MASLD progression through protease-activated receptor signalling in hepatocytes and hepatic stellate cells. These observations raise the hypothesis that antiplatelet and anticoagulant therapies could modify MASLD progression, although evidence remains predominantly preclinical and observational. This review synthesizes haemostatic balance and thrombotic complications in MASLD, the endothelial-inflammation-haemostasis axis in MASLD progression, and emerging perspectives.

A pseudo-ischemic tumor ecosystem couples malignant stress and inflammatory myeloid remodeling across adenocarcinomas.

New todayTransl Oncol · Lu H, Abudukeremu D, Qi X et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Cancer-associated thrombosis and tumor immune remodeling may converge through hypoxia, myeloid inflammation, neutrophil extracellular traps, platelet engagement, and endothelial dysfunction. We investigated whether they define a conserved, transcriptomically measurable tumor state. METHODS: We integrated a lung adenocarcinoma extracellular-vesicle RNA dataset with three venous thromboembolism cohorts to derive five immunothrombotic modules. Single-cell RNA-sequencing data from lung (LUAD), pancreatic (PAAD), and gastric adenocarcinoma (STAD) were analyzed. We defined the pseudo-ischemic tumor ecosystem (PTES) as a transcriptomically defined molecular state reflecting coordinated malignant-cell stress and inflammatory-myeloid activity, rather than direct evidence of tissue ischemia or a diagnosis of cancer-associated thrombosis or venous thromboembolism. A tissue-resolved Core PTES, calculated as the mean of standardized malignant-stress and inflammatory-myeloid programs, was evaluated in 37 single-cell samples and 1107 TCGA tumors. Associations with immune states, Hallmark pathways, inferred cell-cell communication, and overall survival were assessed. The primary survival analysis used continuous Core PTES per one-standard-deviation increase, with high-versus-low PTES analyzed as a sensitivity analysis. RESULTS: Direct analysis showed the strongest effects for malignant stress (Hedges' g = 1.116), NET-like activity (g = 1.477), and endothelial activation (g = 1.288). Single-cell analysis retained 143,101 cells and localized malignant-stress and inflammatory-myeloid programs to their respective compartments. In TCGA, high Core PTES most strongly separated the inflammatory program (median difference = 1.134; rank-biserial r = 0.735). Cross-cancer GSEA identified recurrent proliferative, inflammatory, metabolic, and hypoxic programs, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, TNF-NF-κB, IL6-JAK-STAT3, glycolysis, and hypoxia. Inferred SPP1-CD44 and SPP1-ITGAV/ITGB1 interactions were detected across all three cancers. In multivariable models adjusted for age, sex, and stage, each one-standard-deviation increase in Core PTES was associated with poorer overall survival in LUAD (HR = 1.205, 95% CI 1.045-1.389) and PAAD (HR = 1.458, 95% CI 1.138-1.868), but not STAD (HR = 0.912, 95% CI 0.755-1.103); the cancer-stratified pooled HR was 1.168 (95% CI 1.060-1.288).
