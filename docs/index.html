New todayColloids Surf B Biointerfaces · Mao X, Wang W, Zhu X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is characterized by metabolic plasticity and redox dysregulation, yet therapeutic outcomes remain constrained by adaptive resistance. Herein, a Pt/Ti₃C₂ MXene-based multifunctional nanozyme (TP@MH) was developed to enhance mild-temperature photothermal therapy (mPTT) through combined catalytic/redox regulation and metabolic intervention. Platinum nanoparticles were in situ anchored onto Ti₃C₂ nanosheets to construct a catalytic heterostructure, followed by mitoxantrone (MTO) loading and hyaluronic acid (HA)-mediated CD44-targeting. The obtained TP@MH exhibited peroxidase and GSH-depleting capability, promoting ROS generation and perturbing tumor redox homeostasis. Upon near-infrared irradiation, TP@MH nanozyme not only modulated the apparent POD-like kinetics under the tested conditions but also perturbed intracellular NAD⁺/NADH metabolism and reduced ATP availability, which was associated with decreased HSP70 expression and potentially weakened cellular thermal adaptation. MTO provided an additional chemotherapeutic component, while TP@MH treatment induced mitochondrial dysfunction, enhanced lipid peroxidation, reduced glutathione peroxidase 4 expression, and promoted apoptosis and ferroptosis. Both in vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated that TP@MH achieved efficient antitumor efficacy through the integrated effects of mild photothermal activation, redox regulation, metabolic intervention, and chemotherapy. Collectively, this work provides a multifunctional nanozyme strategy for integrating catalytic, metabolic, photothermal, and chemotherapeutic interventions in RCC therapy.

PARP-CD38-Driven NAD+ Loss in Immune Aging: An Emerging Mechanistic Framework.

Aging Dis · Ali U, Tan R, Ding S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Immune aging is characterized by inflammaging, progressive immune decline, and compromised tissue surveillance. Although researchers have recognized an association between PARP hyperactivation and immune aging (emerging and context-dependent), a unifying framework for how PARP members beyond PARP1 orchestrate immune dysfunction via NAD+ metabolism is lacking. We propose a context-dependent PARP-CD38-NAD+-inflammaging hierarchy. PARP1 is the most abundant nuclear PARP, responsible for 85-90% of nuclear PARP activity and dominant NAD+ consumption under acute DNA damage; CD38, the principal NAD+ glycohydrolase, is upregulated in inflamed aged tissues; NAD+ is compartmentalized (cytosolic, mitochondrial) with distinct regulators (NAMPT, NMNAT1/2/3, SLC25A51). Thus, NAD+ decline is tissue- and compartment-specific; blood NAD+ is not a reliable biomarker. Beyond PARP1, PARP2 (cardiomyocyte aging in mouse), PARP3 (intestinal aging), PARP16 (vascular aging), and PARP9/14 (Alzheimer's models) act as tissue-specific regulators, but their roles in human immune aging remain unquantified. PARP inhibitors and NAD+ precursors show promise but face risks: genomic instability, hematopoietic toxicity, senescence promotion, and inconsistent efficacy. Unresolved questions, whether PBMC PARP activity predicts immune aging, sex differences, and whether partial inhibition reduces NAD+ consumption without compromising DNA repair. By rethinking PARPs as context-dependent integrators, we provide a framework for immunogerontology.

Abstract

Repetitive mild traumatic brain injury (rmTBI) may arise when concussive or subconcussive impacts recur before physiological recovery is complete. Testosterone replacement therapy and aneurysm biology: current evidence and clinical implications.

ReviewIndian J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg · de Oliveira Manduca Palmiero H, Gadelha Figueiredo E · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) is increasingly used to treat male hypogonadism, but its role in aneurysm biology remains unclear. Because aneurysm formation is driven by inflammation, extracellular matrix degradation, and vascular remodeling, testosterone may influence disease progression through androgen receptor-mediated pathways. METHODS: A narrative literature review was conducted using PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Google Scholar (August-September 2025). Studies examining testosterone, TRT, vascular inflammation, and aneurysm-related outcomes, including experimental, preclinical, and clinical studies, were included. EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS: Available evidence is mostly mechanistic and preclinical. Animal studies indicate vascular bed-specific effects: physiological androgen signaling seems protective in abdominal aortic aneurysm models by reducing macrophage infiltration and inflammatory cytokine signaling, whereas exogenous or supraphysiologic testosterone may exacerbate oxidative stress and vascular injury in intracranial aneurysm models. CONCLUSION: Testosterone has context-, dose-, and vascular bed-specific effects on aneurysm biology. Until clinical studies on aneurysm outcomes are available, TRT should be individualized, dosed to meet physiological needs, and closely monitored for cardiovascular and vascular health. No evidence-based recommendation can currently be made to initiate, withhold, or modify TRT solely on the basis of aneurysm status. Multidisciplinary discussion and continued standard aneurysm surveillance are advisable for hypogonadal patients with known aneurysmal disease.

Abstract

AIMS: Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) regulates plasmin-mediated proteolysis, thereby influencing vascular stability and tissue remodeling. Angiotensin II (AngII) induces an increase in PAI-1 during the development of ascending thoracic aortic aneurysm (ATAA). The initial purpose of this study was to determine whether deletion of PAI-1 influenced development of ATAA. Subsequently, this study aimed to define the early pathological events preceding cardiac fibrosis in PAI-1 deficiency and the structural domain responsible for its protective effect. METHODS AND RESULTS: AngII was infused for 4 weeks in whole-body PAI-1 deficient (PAI-1-/-) mice and their wild-type littermates (PAI-1+/+) to examine the role of PAI-1 in ATAA. Mutational studies implicate dysregulated plasmin generation as an initiator of cardiac injury and fibrosis.

The Coagulation-Immune Axis in Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma: Mechanisms, Prognostic Significance, and Therapeutic Opportunities.

New todayReviewCancer Invest · Chang L, Sun Y, Wang Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) develops in a microbe-rich, injury-prone mucosa in which coagulation, inflammation, and tissue repair are closely coupled. Tumor and host cells can activate tissue factor-thrombin signaling, platelets, fibrin turnover, extracellular vesicles, and neutrophil extracellular traps, thereby reshaping myeloid-cell recruitment, cytotoxic immunity, angiogenesis, and invasion. However, local procoagulant biology does not necessarily predict systemic venous thromboembolism. We therefore organize the evidence using a compartmentalized immunothrombosis model that distinguishes intratumoral coagulation-immune remodeling, systemic thrombosis, and treatment-dependent perioperative events. Direct OSCC studies support several components of this network, but much of the evidence linking coagulation to immune escape and treatment response remains preclinical or extrapolated from other tumors. Circulating D-dimer, fibrinogen, platelet indices, and inflammatory ratios show prognostic associations, yet their incremental value beyond established clinicopathologic factors has not been established. Anticoagulant, antiplatelet, protease-activated receptor, podoplanin-CLEC-2, fibrinolytic, and NET-directed strategies are biologically plausible but should not be used as OSCC treatment without a standard indication or clinical trial. Future studies should pair longitudinal blood measurements with spatial tumor profiling and match interventions to a verified mechanism, anatomical compartment, and safe treatment window.

Thromboembolic Complications of Orthopedic Surgery.

Abstract

Orthopedic surgery is a major risk factor for thromboembolic events such as deep venous thrombosis and pulmonary embolism following a variety of surgeries such as total hip and knee replacements, fracture fixations, spinal surgeries, orthopedic traumas, and arthroscopic surgeries. Fujita Med J · Sato Y, Nishio E, Oikawa S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Pregnancy-associated venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a major cause of maternal morbidity and mortality. Pregnancies achieved by in vitro fertilization carry an increased risk of VTE, particularly during early gestation. A 33-year-old woman developed deep vein thrombosis during late pregnancy in her first spontaneous conception despite no identifiable thrombophilia. During a subsequent pregnancy achieved by natural cycle frozen embryo transfer, which was selected to avoid exogenous estrogen exposure and potentially reduce the thrombotic risk compared with hormone replacement cycles, she developed recurrent VTE at 8 weeks of gestation. Inflammation and thrombosis: from pathophysiology to future treatments.

Intern Emerg Med · Santagata D, Giovenali L, Guerrieri E et al. · Full text
Abstract

Inflammation and coagulation are tightly interconnected host-defense systems that, when dysregulated, promote pathological thrombosis. This narrative review examines mechanisms, clinical settings, and therapeutic implications of thromboinflammation (TI) across venous, arterial, and microvascular thrombosis, with particular attention to the differences in clinical translation among vascular territories. Endothelial activation, tissue factor expression, platelet-leukocyte interactions, neutrophil extracellular traps, complement activation, inflammasome signalling, and impaired fibrinolysis converge to generate a procoagulant phenotype. Abstract

Because its clinical features overlap with those of the underlying infection, the diagnosis is often delayed until thrombus propagation or embolization occurs. Abstract

Fibroblastic connective tissue nevus is an uncommon benign cutaneous lesion first described in 2012. Abstract

TFEB‑altered renal cell carcinomas are rare neoplasms, frequently mimicking other tumors. Although FISH remains the diagnostic gold standard, its limited availability has prompted the evaluation of light microscopy‑based ancillary markers. This study compares the performance of immunohistochemistry and RNA‑ISH in detecting TFEB‑altered renal cell carcinomas and distinguishing them from other renal neoplasms.
