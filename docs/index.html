Biomaterials Targeting Mitochondrial NAD+ Metabolism for Tissue Regeneration.

ReviewAdv Healthc Mater · Ma X, Li M, Ren T et al. · Full text
Abstract

Mitochondrial nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) has emerged as a pivotal regulator in tissue regeneration, central to cellular energy metabolism and redox homeostasis. It regulates key mitochondrial processes-including energy production, reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging, and cell death-primarily through the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TAC), oxidative phosphorylation, and sirtuin deacetylase activity. This coordinated regulation fosters a favorable metabolic and immune microenvironment conducive to tissue repair. Strategies to increase mitochondrial NAD+ levels, such as delivery, precursor supplementation (e.g., NR or NMN), catalytic conversion, and inhibition of NAD+-consuming enzymes, have demonstrated significant efficacy in promoting regeneration across diverse tissues, including bone, cartilage, tooth, skin, myocardium, muscle, nerve, and vascular. There is no review that systematically addresses the latest progress in the advances of biomaterials targeting mitochondrial NAD+ metabolism for tissue regeneration. This review fills the gap by outlining the central role of biomaterials targeting mitochondrial NAD+ in tissue regeneration. We introduce the biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of mitochondrial NAD+, highlight the intervention strategies to boost NAD+ levels, and summarize the applications across diverse regenerative contexts. Finally, we discuss prevailing challenges and future prospects, providing new theoretical and interventional insights for regenerative medicine.

Sirtuin-1 in Hypertension: Linking Oxidative Stress and Mitochondrial Dysfunction to Vascular Aging and Therapeutic Potential.

ReviewAntioxidants (Basel) · Budziak S, Kloza M, Baranowski P et al. · Full text
Abstract

Hypertension is a multifactorial cardiovascular disorder characterized by oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, mitochondrial impairment, and accelerated vascular aging, all of which contribute to hypertension-mediated target organ damage. Among the molecular pathways involved in these processes, sirtuin-1 (SIRT1), a nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+)-dependent deacetylase, has emerged as a potential regulator of vascular and metabolic homeostasis. This review summarizes current evidence on the role of SIRT1 in the pathophysiology of hypertension, with particular emphasis on its interactions with oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, endothelial integrity, inflammation, fibrosis, and cellular senescence. Experimental studies suggest that SIRT1 modulation can improve endothelial function and mitochondrial homeostasis, attenuate oxidative stress, inflammation, and vascular remodeling, and limit organ damage in several models. Effects on blood pressure are less consistent and appear to depend on the model and intervention used. Although clinical evidence remains limited, altered SIRT1 expression or activity has been reported in association with hypertension and preeclampsia, while preliminary studies suggest that interventions proposed to activate SIRT1 may improve vascular function and inflammatory status. However, changes in SIRT1 expression might not necessarily reflect changes in its enzymatic activity, and the effects of pharmacological interventions cannot always be attributed specifically to SIRT1. Overall, SIRT1 represents a potential therapeutic target whose value as a biomarker remains uncertain; however, well-designed clinical studies are required to establish its prognostic value and determine whether SIRT1 modulation can improve long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

Smpd2 m6A modification contributes to hypoxic pulmonary hypertension via an alveolar epithelial cell senescence-associated phenotype.

Tissue Cell · Jiang J, Xu J, Wu T et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Pulmonary vascular remodelling induced by the abnormal proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) is a core pathological feature of hypoxic pulmonary hypertension (HPH), but its exact pathogenesis remains poorly understood. This study aimed to elucidate the functional role and intrinsic regulatory mechanisms of an alveolar epithelial cell (AEC) senescence-associated phenotype in the pathogenesis of HPH. METHODS: A 4-week hypoxia-induced HPH rat model and an in vitro hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype model were established. The effects of hypoxia on AECs were analysed by evaluating the senescence-associated phenotype and mitochondrial function. In addition, a Transwell coculture system and AEC-conditioned medium were used to evaluate the effects of hypoxia-exposed AECs with a senescence-associated phenotype on PASMC proliferation. The key signalling pathways associated with the hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype were identified through transcriptome sequencing and subsequent KEGG pathway enrichment analysis. The roles of the Smpd2/ceramide axis in the hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype, PASMC proliferation and HPH progression were explored via the administration of METTL3- and Smpd2-targeting siRNAs, AAV6-shSmpd2 for lung-directed Smpd2 silencing in vivo, the neutral sphingomyelinase (nSMase) inhibitor GW4869, the mitochondria-targeted antioxidant Mito-TEMPO (MT) and the NAD⁺ precursor β-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN). Finally, MeRIP-qPCR, RIP-qPCR, mRNA stability assays, and dual-luciferase assays were employed to elucidate the mechanisms of m6A RNA methylation in AECs with the senescence-associated phenotype. RESULTS: Chronic hypoxia induced typical manifestations of HPH in rats, accompanied by a more pronounced AEC senescence-associated phenotype. In cultured human AECs, hypoxia upregulated METTL3, which increased Smpd2 mRNA stability through m6A modification recognized by IGF2BP2. These changes were associated with ceramide accumulation and mitochondrial dysfunction and contributed to the AEC senescence-associated phenotype in vitro. AECs with this phenotype released soluble and/or vesicular paracrine signals that were associated with abnormal PASMC proliferation, contributing to vascular remodelling and right ventricular hypertrophy. In cultured human AECs, NMN pretreatment, Smpd2 knockdown, and METTL3 knockdown attenuated the hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype. In hypoxia-exposed rats, NMN supplementation, lung-directed Smpd2 silencing, and pharmacological nSMase inhibition with GW4869 attenuated the AEC senescence-associated phenotype and alleviated HPH progression. CONCLUSIONS: This study provides preclinical evidence that, in hypoxia-exposed rats and cultured human AECs/PASMCs, a hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype may contribute to PASMC proliferation and HPH development. Mechanistically, in cultured human AECs, hypoxia exposure increased METTL3-mediated m6A modification of Smpd2 mRNA, which increased its stability in an m6A- and IGF2BP2-dependent manner and upregulated Smpd2 expression. The resulting increase in ceramide accumulation was associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and the senescence-associated phenotype in AECs. In this preclinical setting, AECs with this phenotype may further contribute to PASMC proliferation and HPH development. The findings of this study provide preclinical data supporting a candidate mechanism in HPH pathogenesis and identify candidate targets for future validation in human PH and cell-specific in vivo models.

MOTS-c attenuates placental vascular endothelial cell apoptosis in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy by inhibiting K33-linked deubiquitination of BACH1.

Cell Commun Signal · Li S, Ai L, Ge L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is a gestational liver disorder characterized by impaired placental vascular architecture, leading to placental dysfunction and fetal growth restriction. Our prior work demonstrated that the transcription repressor BACH1 modulates SLC25A51 expression to regulate placental injury under ICP conditions. MOTS-c, a mitochondria-originated peptide, participates in vascular homeostasis, yet its biological functions within the placental vasculature during ICP have not been fully characterized. Clinical analyses revealed significantly reduced MOTS-c protein levels in both maternal serum and placental tissues from ICP patients. In a cholic acid-induced murine model of ICP, MOTS-c administration promoted placental angiogenesis and inhibited apoptosis of placental vascular endothelial cells. These protective effects were recapitulated in vitro, where MOTS-c attenuated oxidative stress and apoptosis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) exposed to taurocholic acid. Mechanistically, biotinylated MOTS-c pull-down coupled with mass spectrometry identified BACH1 as a direct binding partner; subsequent functional validation demonstrated that MOTS-c binds competitively to the D560 residue of BACH1, thereby displacing the deubiquitinase USP7 and promoting K33-linked polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of BACH1. Consequently, MOTS-c-mediated BACH1 depletion relieves transcriptional repression of SLC25A51, enhancing mitochondrial NAD+ import, biogenesis, and redox resilience. Importantly, BACH1 overexpression abolished MOTS-c-induced protection in HUVECs, confirming the functional dependency of MOTS-c on this axis. In vivo, MOTS-c treatment in CA-induced ICP mice significantly improved key offspring developmental parameters-including birth weight, placental efficiency, and neonatal survival. Collectively, these findings establish MOTS-c as a placenta-targeted endogenous regulator that ameliorates ICP-associated placental vascular pathology and adverse perinatal outcomes via modulation of the BACH1/SLC25A51 signaling axis.

Testosterone replacement therapy and aneurysm biology: current evidence and clinical implications.

New todayReviewIndian J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg · de Oliveira Manduca Palmiero H, Gadelha Figueiredo E · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT) is increasingly used to treat male hypogonadism, but its role in aneurysm biology remains unclear. Because aneurysm formation is driven by inflammation, extracellular matrix degradation, and vascular remodeling, testosterone may influence disease progression through androgen receptor-mediated pathways. METHODS: A narrative literature review was conducted using PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Google Scholar (August-September 2025). Studies examining testosterone, TRT, vascular inflammation, and aneurysm-related outcomes, including experimental, preclinical, and clinical studies, were included. EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS: Available evidence is mostly mechanistic and preclinical. Animal studies indicate vascular bed-specific effects: physiological androgen signaling seems protective in abdominal aortic aneurysm models by reducing macrophage infiltration and inflammatory cytokine signaling, whereas exogenous or supraphysiologic testosterone may exacerbate oxidative stress and vascular injury in intracranial aneurysm models. Human data remain indirect, with no prospective studies specifically assessing aneurysm formation, growth, or rupture in TRT users. Large randomized trials and meta-analyses indicate cardiovascular safety comparable to placebo in appropriately selected patients, though higher rates of atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism, and erythrocytosis have been reported. CONCLUSION: Testosterone has context-, dose-, and vascular bed-specific effects on aneurysm biology. Until clinical studies on aneurysm outcomes are available, TRT should be individualized, dosed to meet physiological needs, and closely monitored for cardiovascular and vascular health. No evidence-based recommendation can currently be made to initiate, withhold, or modify TRT solely on the basis of aneurysm status. Multidisciplinary discussion and continued standard aneurysm surveillance are advisable for hypogonadal patients with known aneurysmal disease.

cGAS-STING activation promotes abdominal aortic aneurysm formation in mice and humans: Therapeutic potential of inhibition.

Biochem Pharmacol · Du P, Ma X, Zhu F et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease lacking effective pharmacotherapy. The cyclic GMP-AMP synthase-stimulator of interferon genes (cGAS-STING) innate immune pathway has been linked to inflammatory conditions, yet its role in AAA remains unclear. Here, we combined single-cell transcriptomics, mouse models, pharmacological inhibition, and human tissue validation to investigate the role of this pathway in AAA. In murine single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, cGAS-STING activation was predominantly observed in AAA endothelial cells (ECs). Human scRNA-seq and scissor-based classification of stable versus ruptured AAAs showed that cGAS and STING1 were significantly upregulated in ECs from ruptured AAA, implicating pathway activation in rupture risk. Bioinformatic analyses suggested that endothelial cGAS-STING activation correlates with barrier dysfunction and oxidative stress, and that dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) deficiency may link DNA damage to pathway activation. In Angiotensin II (Ang II) -induced murine AAA, cGAS, p-STING, and p-TBK1 were markedly elevated. Pharmacological blockade with RU.521 (cGAS inhibitor) or STING-IN-2 (STING antagonist) reduced AAA incidence, aortic dilation, and structural damage, while improving endothelial mitochondrial morphology. In human AAA tissues, immunohistochemistry (IHC) confirmed increased cGAS, STING, and TBK1 expression. Moreover, RU.521 or STING-IN-2 treatment of patient-derived 3D AAA organoids significantly downregulated cGAS, p-STING, and p-TBK1, confirming target engagement. Collectively, our findings indicate that the cGAS-STING pathway is activated in murine and human AAA, and that targeting this axis may offer a promising therapeutic approach to limit AAA progression.

Cardiac hemorrhage precedes hypertension-induced fibrosis in plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 deficient mice.

Cardiovasc Res · Pettey AC, Ito S, Franklin MK et al. · Full text
Abstract

AIMS: Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) regulates plasmin-mediated proteolysis, thereby influencing vascular stability and tissue remodeling. Angiotensin II (AngII) induces an increase in PAI-1 during the development of ascending thoracic aortic aneurysm (ATAA). The initial purpose of this study was to determine whether deletion of PAI-1 influenced development of ATAA. Subsequently, this study aimed to define the early pathological events preceding cardiac fibrosis in PAI-1 deficiency and the structural domain responsible for its protective effect. METHODS AND RESULTS: AngII was infused for 4 weeks in whole-body PAI-1 deficient (PAI-1-/-) mice and their wild-type littermates (PAI-1+/+) to examine the role of PAI-1 in ATAA. PAI-1 deficiency did not alter AngII-induced aortopathy but revealed a striking cardiac phenotype characterized by replacement fibrosis predominantly within the epicardium and posterior septum. Ferric iron, indicative of prior hemorrhage, was coincident with fibrosis. Similar phenotypes were observed in PAI-1-/- mice infused with norepinephrine for 4 weeks. To define the pathological events preceding cardiac fibrosis, either AngII or norepinephrine was infused for 1 week in PAI-1+/+ or -/- mice. Both infusions induced extensive epicardial hemorrhage and posterior septal fibrosis in PAI-1-/- mice. To explore the initiation of cardiac pathology, AngII was infused for approximately 1 day. PAI-1-/- mice developed diffuse hemorrhage and cardiomyocyte injury localized to the posterior septum, pathologic changes that preceded overt fibrosis. Finally, to determine the protective domain of PAI-1, saline or AngII was administered to mice harboring loss-of-function point mutations in the protease inhibitory (PAI-1Ala/Ala) or somatomedin B-binding domains (PAI-1AK/AK). Compared to saline infusion, 1 week of AngII induced hemorrhage and heterogeneous fibrosis in PAI-1Ala/Ala, but not PAI-1AK/AK mice. CONCLUSIONS: These findings support that, under hemodynamic stress, PAI-1 deficiency promotes early cardiac hemorrhage and cardiomyocyte injury that lead to fibrosis. Mutational studies implicate dysregulated plasmin generation as an initiator of cardiac injury and fibrosis.

Periodontitis Beyond ASCVD: Emerging Cardiovascular Phenotypes in Perio-Medicine.

ReviewClin Oral Investig · Sansavini M, Pieretto G, Samra M et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Periodontitis is increasingly recognized as a chronic inflammatory disease with systemic implications beyond the oral cavity. Within perio-medicine, its association with cardiovascular disease has been extensively investigated, particularly in relation to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). However, the field is now expanding toward a broader and more phenotype-specific cardiovascular framework. OBJECTIVE: This narrative review critically examines how the periodontitis-cardiovascular field has evolved from a predominantly ASCVD-centered model toward a broader spectrum of cardiovascular phenotypes, with particular emphasis on differences in evidence maturity, contemporary mechanistic concepts, and unresolved translational questions. METHODS: A structured literature search was conducted in PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library. Evidence from mechanistic, observational, and interventional studies was qualitatively synthesized according to its contribution to established clinical associations, supportive mechanistic evidence, emerging cardiovascular phenotypes, and unresolved questions regarding causality and clinical translation. RESULTS: The association between periodontitis and coronary atherosclerotic disease represents the most mature component of the literature, with substantial observational and meta-analytic support, while ischemic cerebrovascular disease is also supported by consistent but more heterogeneous evidence. Beyond ASCVD, atrial fibrillation and heart failure currently represent the most developed emerging cardiovascular phenotypes, whereas evidence concerning abdominal aortic aneurysm and venous thromboembolism remains more preliminary and hypothesis-generating. Contemporary mechanistic models extend beyond bacteremia and systemic inflammation to include endothelial dysfunction, thrombo-inflammatory signaling, trained immunity, immune-cell trafficking, functional dysregulation of the oral microbiome, and shared biological vulnerabilities. However, these pathways differ markedly in cardiovascular-specific evidentiary maturity and should not be interpreted as equivalent evidence of human causality. Periodontal therapy can improve inflammatory biomarkers, endothelial function, selected metabolic parameters, and blood pressure in specific populations, but a persistent translational gap remains between surrogate-marker improvement and demonstrated reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events. Periodontitis may also contribute to chronic systemic inflammatory burden in susceptible individuals, although its role within cardiovascular residual inflammatory risk remains an emerging hypothesis. CONCLUSIONS: The periodontal-cardiovascular field is evolving from a predominantly ASCVD-centered model toward a phenotype-specific continuum of evidence maturity. Periodontitis is best interpreted as a potential modifier of cardiovascular vulnerability rather than as an established causal determinant. Future research should prioritize adequately powered cardiovascular-outcome trials, standardized periodontal phenotyping, and prospective validation of emerging phenotypes and mechanistic frameworks before periodontal information can be considered clinically actionable for cardiovascular prevention.

Sca1⁺ stem cells contribute to β-Aminopropionitrile-exacerbated aortic aneurysm progression in marfan syndrome.

Heart Vessels · Gao Q, Cao X, Wang AA et al. · Full text
Abstract

Aortic aneurysm and dissection (AAD) is the major cause of mortality in patients with Marfan syndrome (MFS), with no currently available effective therapies. FBN1 mutations disrupt extracellular matrix homeostasis through impaired microfibril function, leading to aberrant matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) activity and compensatory lysyl oxidase (LOX) upregulation. Although the β-Aminopropionitrile (BAPN) induced mouse model is widely used to study aortic aneurysms, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain unclear. In this study, we show that BAPN treatment exacerbates aortic aneurysm progression in MFS mice by increasing collagen production, promoting MMP expression and reducing LOX expression in Sca1+ stem cells, promoting Sca1+ stem cells differentiation towards synthetic vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) and inhibiting their differentiation into contractile VSMCs and activating the Smad2/3 signaling pathway through increasing TβR1 (TGF-β receptor 1) level and Smad2/3 phosphorylation. The Diabetes Paradox in Aortic Aneurysms: An Unexpected Inverse Association.

ReviewBiomedicines · Orchi P, Saltarocchi S, Spunticchia F et al. · Full text
Abstract

Aortic aneurysms are associated with substantial morbidity and mortalitbecause of the risk of progressive dilation, rupture, and related acute aortic complications. Although diabetes mellitus (DM) is a major cardiovascular risk factor, epidemiological studies have consistently reported an inverse association between DM and the prevalence and progression of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). More limited evidence suggests an inverse association between diabetes and thoracic aortic aneurysm. Accordingly, this review focuses primarily on AAA; where available, evidence concerning TAA and aortic dissection is discussed separately. This review examines the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying the inverse association between DM and aortic aneurysm development. Key mechanisms include the effects of hyperglycemia on extracellular matrix remodeling, particularly through modulation of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP-2 and MMP-9) activity, increased formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs), and enhanced collagen cross-linking. Experimental and translational evidence suggests that diabetes may influence inflammatory cell states and mural neoangiogenesis, although these effects appear to be context- and model-dependent. Antidiabetic therapies, including metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, thiazolidinediones, sulfonylureas, and SGLT2 inhibitors, may also contribute to the observed association through glucose-lowering and/or pleiotropic vascular effects.
