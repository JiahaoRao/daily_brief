Bifunctional Pt/CeO2 Nanozyme-Mediated Metabolic Regulation Promotes NAD+ Regeneration and ROS Clearance to Ameliorate Age-Related Osteoporosis.

ACS Appl Mater Interfaces · Zhang H, Yang C, Zhou Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Age-related osteoporosis is characterized by progressive loss of bone mass and deterioration of trabecular microarchitecture, driven by chronic inflammation, redox imbalance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and osteoblast senescence that uncouples bone formation from resorption. During aging, declining nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) availability further exacerbates mitochondrial dysfunction and stress responses, suggesting that restoring NAD+ redox homeostasis could be a viable therapeutic strategy. Here, we reported a bifunctional nanozyme-mediated metabolic regulation strategy to promote NAD+ regeneration and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS) for the treatment of age-related osteoporosis. The bifunctional nanozyme (Pt/CeO2) was constructed by in situ growth of platinum nanoparticles on cerium oxide nanorods and was designed for ROS scavenging and NAD+ regeneration via NADH (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) oxidation. In senescent osteoblasts, Pt/CeO2 reduced ROS, restored mitochondrial integrity, and alleviated senescence, thereby promoting osteogenic differentiation. In vivo results confirmed that systemic administration of Pt/CeO2 preserved trabecular architecture and increased bone mineral density in naturally aged mice. These findings demonstrated that coupling ROS scavenging with the restoration of NAD+ redox homeostasis via Pt/CeO2 nanozyme provided a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract osteoblast senescence and slow the progression of age-related osteoporosis.

Dual-layer regulation of macrophage grancalcin links diabetic redox-metabolic imbalance to impaired vascularized bone regeneration.

Redox Biol · Jin S, Song Z, Fang L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Effective bone regeneration requires the timely transition of immune cells from inflammatory defense to regenerative support. However, how diabetic redox-metabolic stress sustains maladaptive immune states and drives regenerative failure remains incompletely understood. Herein, using a mouse jawbone defect model combined with single-cell RNA sequencing, metabolic assays and human peri-extraction gingival specimens, we identified a senescence-associated macrophage state characterized by elevated grancalcin (GCA). Functionally, recombinant GCA impaired osteogenic and endothelial responses, whereas GCA neutralization partially reversed the inhibitory effects of diabetic macrophage-conditioned medium, supporting extracellular GCA as a functional mediator of regenerative dysfunction. Mechanistically, diabetic macrophages exhibited enhanced glycolytic activity, impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and a reduced NAD+/NADH ratio. This redox imbalance lowered SIRT6 abundance, increased H3K9ac/H3K56ac enrichment at the accessible Gca regulatory locus and sustained Gca transcription. Meanwhile, hyperglycemia-triggered Ca2+ signaling promoted GCA recruitment to membrane-proximal regions and increased both total extracellular and extracellular vesicle-associated GCA. Local delivery of quercetin using a β-cyclodextrin/GelMA hydrogel attenuated macrophage GCA dysregulation, reduced senescence-associated macrophage features and improved vascularized jawbone regeneration. Collectively, this work defines a dual-layer paradigm controlling extracellular GCA bioavailability, thereby linking diabetic redox-metabolic perturbation to defective bone regeneration and supporting local targeting of macrophage GCA for diabetic bone-healing therapy.

Brain renin-angiotensin system: Pathophysiological mechanisms and therapeutic targets in ischemic stroke.

Neural Regen Res · Xie A, Li W, Chen B et al. · Full text
Abstract

This review aims to delineate the context-dependent, opposing actions of the angiotensin II type 1 receptor, angiotensin II type 2 receptor, and angiotensin-converting enzyme 2/ angiotensin-(1-7)/Mas receptor axis in ischemic stroke and to discuss their translational potential for neuroprotective therapy. Although recanalization cannot guarantee favorable functional outcomes for all patients with ischemic stroke, adjunctive neuroprotective targets remain limited. Dysregulation of the brain renin-angiotensin system is increasingly recognized as a potentially modifiable pathway in post-stroke brain injury. Cerebral ischemia shifts the physiological receptor balance toward angiotensin II type 1 receptor predominance, promoting vasoconstriction, blood-brain barrier disruption, neuroinflammation, excitotoxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis. Conversely, the angiotensin II type 2 receptor and Mas receptor may counter these detrimental processes through temporally distinct mechanisms. The angiotensin II type 2 receptor may provide early neuroprotection through endothelial nitric oxide synthase-mediated vasodilation, suppression of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase, and inhibition of nuclear factor kappa-B signaling. By comparison, Mas receptor activation may facilitate delayed brain repair through mitochondrial stabilization, nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2-mediated antioxidant responses, and preservation of blood-brain barrier integrity. This temporal framework, characterized by acute injury associated with angiotensin II type 1 receptor signaling, early protection associated with angiotensin II type 2 receptor signaling, and long-term recovery potentially supported by Mas receptor signaling, provides a rationale for time-sensitive therapeutic strategies. Preclinical evidence supports the neuroprotective effects of angiotensin II type 1 receptor blockers. Angiotensin II type 2 receptor agonists, such as compound 21, and glycosylated angiotensin-(1-7) peptide derivatives are promising approaches for engaging endogenous protective pathways.

Neurovascular Unit Redox-Metabolic Communication in Poststroke Cognitive Impairment: Spatiotemporal Dysregulation, Cellular Crosstalk, and Precision Intervention.

New todayReviewEur J Neurosci · Zhang Q, Chen Y, Bai M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is a dynamic syndrome driven by focal brain injury, pre-existing cerebrovascular burden, metabolic comorbidities, and reduced brain resilience. Increasing evidence suggests that persistent disruption of redox-metabolic communication within the neurovascular unit (NVU), rather than neuronal loss alone, promotes the transition from acute ischemic injury to chronic cognitive decline. This review summarizes metabolic reprogramming in BMECs, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, and neurons, emphasizing the intercellular transfer of metabolites, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, oxidized lipids, extracellular vesicles, and mitochondria. We propose that PSCI develops through a spatiotemporally dysregulated continuum involving vascular injury, cell-specific metabolic remodeling, maladaptive signal propagation, impaired axonal and synaptic energy support, and progressive network disconnection. Therapeutic strategies should therefore be stage-specific: restoring microcirculatory perfusion and physiological redox signaling acutely, promoting inflammation resolution and metabolic repair subacutely, and combining vascular-metabolic modulation with neurorehabilitation chronically. Future studies should integrate cell type-specific interventions, metabolic flux tracing, spatial multi-omics, and longitudinal cognitive assessments to establish causal donor-carrier-recipient-stage-outcome relationships.

Abstract

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a critical clinical disorder characterized by high incidence, high mortality, and numerous complications. AMOTL2: A key regulator of mechanotransduction in development, vascular disease and cancer.

ReviewCells Dev · Fonseca P, Zhang Y, Holmgren L · Full text
Abstract

Mechanical forces play a fundamental role in regulating cell behavior during development, tissue homeostasis, and disease. The angiomotin family protein AmotL2 has emerged as a central integrator of mechanotransduction, linking cell-cell adhesion, cytoskeletal tension, and nuclear architecture to transcriptional control. Through its ability to connect cadherin-based junctions to radial actin filaments and the nuclear lamina, AmotL2 establishes a continuous mechanical axis that enables cells to sense and respond to extracellular forces. This function is essential for epithelial organization, vascular morphogenesis, and endothelial responses to shear stress, in part through regulation of Hippo pathway effectors such as YAP and TAZ. Dysregulation of AmotL2 contributes to pathological states, including abdominal aortic aneurysm, where reduced AmotL2 impairs endothelial mechanotransduction, and cancer, where induction of the p60AmotL2 isoform disrupts polarity, alters nuclear mechanics, and promotes invasive behavior. Emerging evidence further suggests that AmotL2 regulates transcription through direct effects on nuclear structure and chromatin accessibility, positioning it as a key regulator of force-dependent gene expression. In this review, we summarize the structural and mechanistic basis of AmotL2 function and discuss its roles in development, vascular biology, and cancer, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target in diseases driven by altered mechanotransduction.

Developing structure and strategies for analysis of circulating biomarkers to detect and monitor abdominal aortic aneurysms.

Am J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol · Lindsey ML, Shivam P, Nde PN et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a multifactorial disease characterized by progressive remodeling of the aortic wall. Current clinical management includes screening ultrasound for diagnosis, and routine aortic imaging to monitor growth in patients with small AAA and surgical exclusion of larger AAA. With the evolution of a diverse array of multianalyte assessments from peripheral blood, comprehensive assessments of circulating biomarkers in patients with AAA is possible. In the context of this dynamic disease process, circulating markers - paired with clinical and imaging data - provide an opportunity to identify specific active biologic processes that influence the natural history of the disease. We propose a disease-specific framework for understanding relationships between circulating markers and imaging within a novel three class structure. Class I associations correlate with aortic size and presence of AAA and may be related to the mechanisms of initial formation. Class II associations track with change in aneurysm MTD and may be related to mechanisms of AAA growth and rupture risk. Class II may be clinically relevant for the identification of targets for modification that could alter the natural history of established AAA. Class III associations relate to static or dynamic morphologic features such as mural thrombus or calcifications that have less well-defined associations with clinical outcomes. Identified and appropriately classified markers can provide specific avenues for novel therapeutic and diagnostic options to reduce morbidity and mortality in patients with AAA.

Abstract

Inefficient aneurysmal aorta enrichment and vascular matrix penetration represent major challenges against siRNA delivery for the management of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). Herein, platelet membrane (PM)-reversibly camouflaged nanocomplexes (NCs) are developed to deliver Poldip2 siRNA (siPoldip2) into vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), which then restore vascular homeostasis by promoting phenotypic switching and mitigating oxidative stress. AI Applications in Aortic and Peripheral Artery Disease: Risk Prediction, Imaging, Procedural Support, and Longitudinal Surveillance.

ReviewMethodist Debakey Cardiovasc J · Robert JD, Bannikov S, Atkins AD et al. · Full text
Abstract

Aortic disease and lower-extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD) generate substantial morbidity, mortality, and longitudinal imaging burden. Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly studied for vascular risk prediction, image analysis, procedural support, and surveillance, but the maturity of these applications is heterogeneous. This review evaluates recent AI applications in aortic disease and PAD, emphasizing the distinction between algorithmic performance, external validation, demonstrated workflow impact, and proven patient benefit. Automated image segmentation and opportunistic abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) detection have the most mature evidence base, including retrospective external validation and, for AI-assisted AAA case finding, nonrandomized evidence of improved downstream evaluation and follow-up. Conversely, while machine-learning models for vascular risk prediction and disease progression often report high retrospective accuracy, their clinical readiness remains strictly investigational. Across most aortic and PAD applications, there is a persistent lack of independent external validation, decision-curve analysis, and prospective testing against standard care. Image-fusion guidance has clinical evidence for reducing procedural resource use, but this evidence should not be conflated with autonomous AI or robotic navigation. Cardiovascular digital twins remain largely mechanistic, computational, or early-development frameworks. AI can augment vascular imaging and risk assessment, but high retrospective accuracy is not equivalent to clinical utility. Current guideline thresholds for repair, revascularization, and surveillance should not be altered on the basis of investigational radiomics or prediction models. Translation requires multicenter external validation, prospective workflow evaluation, calibration and net-benefit assessment, transparent reporting, and trials demonstrating improved patient outcomes.

Machine Learning Versus Traditional Methods for Predicting Venous Thromboembolism After Surgery: A Systematic Review and Bayesian Network Meta-Analysis.

New todayReviewANZ J Surg · Chen S, Dhaliwal S, Papas C et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Postoperative venous thromboembolism remains a major cause of preventable morbidity after surgery, and established risk scores may perform inconsistently across surgical populations. METHODS: MEDLINE, Embase and the Cochrane Library were searched for studies comparing machine learning with traditional risk models for postoperative venous thromboembolism prediction. Risk-of-bias was assessed with PROBAST + AI. The primary outcome was discrimination measured by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. Bayesian random-effects network meta-analysis estimated mean differences versus Caprini with 95% credible intervals. Best and worst-version analyses explored the impact of within-study multiplicity, with subgroup analyses by cancer surgery and procedure type. RESULTS: Eighteen studies including 177 656 validation patients were included. Meta-ensembles showed the largest improvement compared with Caprini in the best-version analysis (mean difference in AUROC, 0.232; 95% credible interval, 0.129-0.334), with a similar finding in the worst-version analysis (0.237; 0.123-0.351). Several other machine learning classes also had higher AUROC estimates than Caprini, whereas most traditional scores and surgeon judgement did not. Geneva showed lower performance than Caprini. Meta-ensembles ranked first overall, while Caprini and Geneva ranked 14th and last, respectively. Meta-ensembles and boosting models had higher AUROC than Caprini in both cancer surgery analyses; in general surgery, no model class showed higher performance than Caprini in the worst-version analysis. CONCLUSIONS: Machine learning models, particularly meta-ensembles, showed higher reported discrimination than traditional scores, but evidence remains limited by heterogeneity, inconsistent calibration reporting, sparse decision-analytic evaluation and infrequent external validation. Clinical adoption requires external validation and demonstration of real-world net benefit.

Abstract

Thromboembolic complications in children with congenital heart disease (CHD) carry significant morbidity and mortality, particularly in patients with single ventricle physiology. Current management strategies, including systemic anticoagulation, thrombolytics, and surgical thrombectomy, each carry substantial risks in this population. Transcatheter aspiration thrombectomy represents an emerging alternative. We retrospectively reviewed all pediatric patients with CHD at our institution who underwent transcatheter aspiration thrombectomy using the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System between January 2024 and February 2025. In this limited case series, the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System demonstrated feasibility for transcatheter thrombus removal in pediatric patients across a spectrum of CHD. Further prospective studies are needed to define optimal patient selection, procedural techniques, and post-procedural management.

Abstract

By simulating spatial distribution patterns, PLT-pro2.0 reconstructs the original linear distribution on the slide and calculates the PLT:RBC ratio at the body-tail junction. A manual PLT count performed on each paired sodium citrate sample served as the reference standard. Amikacin deaggregation at 10, 30, 60, and 120 minutes was evaluated in parallel. RESULTS.—: YOLO-PLT distinguished independent PLTs and aggregates. Non-aggregated samples showed a linear gradient (Pearson r = 0.863), whereas EDTA-PTCP samples displayed nonlinear tail aggregation. Passing-Bablok analysis indicated no proportional bias (slope 95% CI, 0.9496-1.1921) and slight constant bias (intercept 95% CI, -33.1884 to -0.8571). Amikacin showed poorer agreement. CONCLUSIONS.—: PLT-pro2.0 provides a rapid, objective, and accurate WSI-based correction strategy for EDTA-PTCP, particularly for samples difficult to correct using conventional methods.

Residual Pure Intralymphatic Breast Carcinoma After Neoadjuvant Therapy: Clinicopathologic Analysis and Comparative Outcomes Across Published Studies.

Abstract

CONTEXT.—: Residual pure intralymphatic carcinoma (PIC), defined as residual tumor confined exclusively to lymphovascular spaces without stromal invasion after neoadjuvant therapy (NAT), is a rare, residual disease pattern. No standardized staging, pathology reporting protocol, or consensus classification exists within current frameworks. OBJECTIVE.—: To characterize the clinicopathologic features, post-NAT staging, Residual Cancer Burden (RCB) classification, and outcomes of patients with PIC at our institution, and to perform a pooled analysis of all published cohorts. DESIGN.—: This is a retrospective cohort study of consecutive patients with breast cancer treated with NAT and surgery at a large academic medical center from January 2015 to September 2025. Identified PIC cases were systematically characterized and compared against 5 previously published cohorts. RESULTS.—: Of 2000 patients screened, 13 (0.65%) had PIC (9 of 13 [69%] grade 3; 7 of 13 [54%] human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 [HER2]-positive). All had ypT0 disease (yp denotes pathologic stage after neoadjuvant therapy) on American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging, while 8 of 13 node-negative patients (62%) may have an RCB score of RCB-I or higher when intralymphatic tumor dimensions are entered-a framework mismatch rather than true pathologic complete response (pCR). At median follow-up of 59.2 months, overall survival was 100% (13 of 13) and disease-free survival, was 92% (12 of 13). Pooled analysis (N = 60) showed mortality decline from 83% (5 of 6) in the earliest (pre-2018) cohort to 0% to 20% post-2018. CONCLUSIONS.—: Current staging frameworks may misclassify PIC as pCR, implying incomplete standardization with potential clinical consequences. We propose an AJCC modifier, ypT0(LVI+), for residual lymphovascular invasion in the absence of residual in situ carcinoma or nodal metastasis, plus a dedicated College of American Pathologists synoptic field to ensure appropriate management.
