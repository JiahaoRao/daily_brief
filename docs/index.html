Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1-targeted redox remodeling by a mesoporous medium-entropy alloy nanozyme promotes ferroptosis and cuproptosis for tumor therapy.

New todayJ Colloid Interface Sci · Wang C, Chen C, Zhu J et al. · Full text
Abstract

Tumor cells exhibit robust redox adaptive capacity, limiting the effectiveness of ROS amplification alone in inducing persistent and irreversible oxidative damage. Accordingly, simultaneously enhancing oxidative stress and disrupting antioxidant defenses represents an effective strategy for improving catalytic cancer therapy. Here, we developed hyaluronic acid-functionalized NLG-919-loaded mesoporous PtPdRhCu medium-entropy alloy colloidal nanoparticles (PPRCNH), an interface-engineered platform integrating porous architecture, multimetallic electronic coupling, surface functionalization, and metabolic regulation. The mesoporous framework exposes abundant low-coordination catalytic sites, while interactions among Pt, Pd, Rh, and Cu promote H2O2 activation and sustained ROS production. HA functionalization facilitates the uptake of PPRCNH by CD44-overexpressing osteosarcoma cells, whereas NLG-919 inhibits indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1)-dependent tryptophan-to-kynurenine conversion, consequently impairing G6PD-mediated nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) regeneration. The acidic tumor microenvironment further accelerates Cu release and perturbs mitochondrial lipoylated protein homeostasis. Mechanistic studies demonstrated that PPRCNH enhanced ROS generation and suppressed antioxidant compensation dependent on glutathione (GSH) and NADPH, resulting in mitochondrial dysfunction and the simultaneous induction of ferroptosis and cuproptosis. In the osteosarcoma model, PPRCNH achieved a tumor growth inhibition rate of approximately 77.2%. This study establishes a catalytic-metabolic regulatory paradigm by integrating mesoporous medium-entropy alloy interfaces with metabolic checkpoint modulation to achieve sustained oxidative stress amplification.

From NAD+ elevation to clinical benefit: Tissue-specific regulation and translational barriers in aging and age-related disease.

ReviewPharmacol Res · Pei Z, Zhong M, Jiang Q et al. · Full text
Abstract

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) decline has been implicated in aging and age-related disease, and precursor supplementation with nicotinamide riboside (NR) or nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) has shown efficacy in preclinical models. Human trials, however, have produced inconsistent functional outcomes, and the sources of this translational gap remain poorly defined. We combined a bibliometric analysis of 3271 aging-focused NAD+ publications (2004-2025) with a critical synthesis of mechanistic and clinical evidence to localize where and why preclinical findings fail to translate. The bibliometric analysis revealed three structural features of the literature: (i) original research articles dominated the corpus, whereas the document-type classification cannot distinguish preclinical experiments from human observational or interventional studies; (ii) research is siloed across disease domains with limited cross-tissue comparison; and (iii) intervention-oriented keywords (NMN, NR, supplementation, drug delivery) have outpaced empirical clinical validation. Mechanistic synthesis showed that these structural gaps correspond to biological heterogeneity: NAD+ biosynthesis, consumption, redox balance, and subcellular compartmentalization are tissue- and compartment-specific, such that systemic precursor elevation does not uniformly restore functional NAD+ in disease-relevant tissues. Four subcellular pools-nuclear, cytosolic, mitochondrial, and axonal-each possess distinct rate-limiting bottlenecks, and hepatic metabolic reserve introduces an overlooked source of inter-individual variability. Across neurodegeneration, metabolic disease, cardiovascular aging, and sarcopenia, NAD+ dysregulation follows disease-specific mechanisms rather than a universal decline phenotype. We propose a precision-medicine framework that shifts the core question from whether NAD+ can be elevated to whether a defined intervention corrects a specific NAD+ defect in the appropriate tissue and subcellular compartment. This framework provides a structured basis for designing next-generation NAD+-targeted trials with tissue-specific biomarkers and function-oriented endpoints.

Acta Biomater · Eliathamby D, Ung L, Yap H et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: To clarify aortic microstructure-function relationships, we used improved, disease-motivated enzymatic digestion models of medial degeneration to evaluate individual extracellular matrix (ECM) components (i.e., elastin, collagen, and proteoglycans) and their influence on biomechanical properties of ascending aortic tissue. METHODS: Thin, 200 µm medial porcine ascending aortic sections were progressively digested with elastase or collagenase for selective degradation of these ECM components. Full thickness human aortic tissues were treated with chondroitinase, hyaluronidase, and heparinase to completely remove proteoglycans. Proteoglycan removal decreased energy loss and reduced strain-energy density and strain at onset of transition, and increased high strain modulus. CONCLUSIONS: Through targeted modelling of ECM degenerative features, we have delineated the overlapping effects of major matrix constituents, underscoring the complex, multifactorial nature of structure-function relationships in aortic disease. We also identified multiple microstructural pathways to increasing energy loss and the vulnerability of aortic tissue to failure. STATEMENT OF SIGNIFICANCE: Thoracic aortic dissection results from the progressive degeneration of the aortic wall extracellular matrix, yet the contributions of individual matrix constituents to this mechanical failure remain understudied. To address this gap, we developed a disease-motivated enzymatic model that enables controlled, graded modification of key extracellular matrix constituents in aortic tissue, better replicating the partial degeneration observed in vivo than existing approaches. Using this framework, we systematically evaluated the biomechanical consequences of elastin, collagen, and proteoglycan degeneration. Here, we revealed multiple microstructural pathways to altered viscoelastic behaviour, which is associated with increased susceptibility to aortic failure. These findings provide new mechanistic insight into structure-function relationships in aortic disease and help explain how matrix degeneration contributes to tissue vulnerability and dissection risk.

Front Immunol · Xu X, Wei Y, Wang M et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Coronary artery aneurysm is a major cardiovascular complication of Kawasaki disease (KD). The phenotype switch of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) plays a crucial role in pathological vascular wall remodeling in KD; however, the molecular mechanisms driving this process remain incompletely understood. Although the SPARC protein family has been implicated in various vascular diseases, the role of specific SPARC family members in mediating VSMC phenotypic transition during KD-associated vasculitis remains unknown. Here, we investigated the function and mechanistic role of SPOCK2, a SPARC family member, in vascular wall homeostasis under KD-induced inflammatory stress. The gut microbiome and vascular disease - A systematic review of mechanistic insights and implications for vascular surgery.

ReviewJVS Vasc Sci · Castro E Silva M, Silva E, Nunes C et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Observational studies repeatedly linked gut microbial features and metabolites to vascular disease, but it is unclear which associations are reproducible, which remain significant after confounder adjustment, and how they relate to experimental evidence. This review aims to determine which taxa and metabolites are consistently associated across independent human studies; whether the population, phenotype, method, and sample type explain divergent findings; and where the human data converge with, diverge from, or remain untested with the animal and interventional literature. METHODS: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library were searched for human studies of the gut microbiome composition or gut-derived metabolites reporting atherosclerotic, aneurysmal, peripheral arterial, calcific, or arterial stiffness outcomes from database inception to December 31, 2025. Study screening and duplicate article extraction were conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. The study design was classified explicitly, the covariates in each primary analysis were extracted, and the risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale or the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist. The findings were then compared with the animal and human interventional literature identified through targeted searching. RESULTS: All the 29 included reports were observational (19 cross-sectional, 3 case-control, and 7 cohort) studies, and none was interventional. The reproducible findings were the enrichment of Proteobacteria-affiliated genera (5 reports) and of oral-origin organisms (3 reports), and the depletion of butyrate producers in carotid disease. The carnitine pathway intermediates gamma-butyrobetaine and trimethyllysine were independently associated with cardiovascular death and major adverse limb events (hazard ratio, 1.93; 95% confidence interval, 1.35-2.76) in two independent cohorts. In addition, 16 of 29 reports fitted a multivariable model, and only three included renal function; the compositional findings were almost exclusively unadjusted. The risk of bias was good in 11 reports, moderate in 16, and low in 2. Animal models causally implicate TMAO in atherogenesis and aneurysm formation and show butyrate and Roseburia intestinalis to be protective, but the results of germ-free experiments and the only human fecal transplantation trial are discordant. CONCLUSIONS: A small number of gut microbial signals are reproducible, but most have not been adjusted for potential confounders, particularly renal function, which may partly explain the observed associations. The carnitine pathway metabolites with the strongest prognostic signal in surgical populations have never been tested in an animal vascular model. Microbiome profiling has no current role in vascular surgical practice.
