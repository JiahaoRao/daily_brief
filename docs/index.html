Brain renin-angiotensin system: Pathophysiological mechanisms and therapeutic targets in ischemic stroke.

New todayNeural Regen Res · Xie A, Li W, Chen B et al. · Full text
Abstract

This review aims to delineate the context-dependent, opposing actions of the angiotensin II type 1 receptor, angiotensin II type 2 receptor, and angiotensin-converting enzyme 2/ angiotensin-(1-7)/Mas receptor axis in ischemic stroke and to discuss their translational potential for neuroprotective therapy. Although recanalization cannot guarantee favorable functional outcomes for all patients with ischemic stroke, adjunctive neuroprotective targets remain limited. Dysregulation of the brain renin-angiotensin system is increasingly recognized as a potentially modifiable pathway in post-stroke brain injury. Cerebral ischemia shifts the physiological receptor balance toward angiotensin II type 1 receptor predominance, promoting vasoconstriction, blood-brain barrier disruption, neuroinflammation, excitotoxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis. Conversely, the angiotensin II type 2 receptor and Mas receptor may counter these detrimental processes through temporally distinct mechanisms. The angiotensin II type 2 receptor may provide early neuroprotection through endothelial nitric oxide synthase-mediated vasodilation, suppression of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase, and inhibition of nuclear factor kappa-B signaling. By comparison, Mas receptor activation may facilitate delayed brain repair through mitochondrial stabilization, nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2-mediated antioxidant responses, and preservation of blood-brain barrier integrity. This temporal framework, characterized by acute injury associated with angiotensin II type 1 receptor signaling, early protection associated with angiotensin II type 2 receptor signaling, and long-term recovery potentially supported by Mas receptor signaling, provides a rationale for time-sensitive therapeutic strategies. Preclinical evidence supports the neuroprotective effects of angiotensin II type 1 receptor blockers. Angiotensin II type 2 receptor agonists, such as compound 21, and glycosylated angiotensin-(1-7) peptide derivatives are promising approaches for engaging endogenous protective pathways.

Dual-Targeted Nanoparticle Chimeras Induce Bioenergetic Crisis by Coupling Lipid Droplet Degradation with Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide Reduction in Triple-Negative Breast Cancer.

New todayActa Biomater · Zhang Y, Wang K, Fang B et al. · Full text
Abstract

As the most prevalent malignancy in female population, triple-negative breast cancer is characterized by unfavorable prognosis and therapy resistance, driven by a significant and progressive accumulation of lipid droplets (LDs). As dynamic organelles, LDs play critical roles in lipid storage, oxidative stress mitigation, and metabolic buffering. Here, we developed a cationic lipid droplet-targeted nanoparticle chimera (LD·NanoTAC) functionalized with an LD-targeting moiety to achieve precise subcellular delivery. Meanwhile, the cationic charges boost autophagic flux thus promoting selective lipophagy and subsequent degradation of LDs. To interfere with tumor cell metabolism, we focused on nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT), a rate-limiting enzyme in the mammalian nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) salvage pathway. Also, extracellular NAMPT promotes tumor progression through non-enzymatic mechanisms. Hence, a NAMPT-targeted protein degrader was encapsulated in LD·NanoTAC to afford LD·NanoTACNAMPT, inducing targeted degradation of NAMPT, leading to NAD+ reduction and subsequent inhibition of Warburg effect in tumor cells. The dual degradation triggered a catastrophic energetic impairment in tumor cells. Concurrently, the compensatory mitochondrial β-oxidation of lipophagy-derived fatty acids was suppressed, leading to complete LDs clearance and a paradoxical state of substrate overload accompanied by bioenergetic failure. This cellular dysfunction subsequently initiated a cascade of events including reactive oxygen species accumulation, onset of mitochondrial dysfunction, and ultimately irreversible tumor cell death. In vivo, the dual-degradation strategy using LD·NanoTACNAMPT demonstrated potent antitumor efficacy in both orthotopic and metastatic 4T1 tumor models. It significantly suppressed primary tumor growth and pulmonary metastasis, and extended the median survival of 4T1 tumor-bearing mice. STATEMENT OF SIGNIFICANCE: Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains a major clinical challenge with poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options, largely driven by progressive accumulation of lipid droplets (LDs) that fuel tumor metabolic reprogramming. Existing protein degradation strategies face critical bottlenecks in subcellular precision and synergistic metabolic disruption. Here, we propose a cationic LD-targeted nanoparticle chimera (LD·NanoTAC) platform, which achieves concurrent targeted degradation of LDs and the key metabolic enzyme nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT). This dual-degradation paradigm triggers catastrophic energetic collapse, suppresses compensatory mitochondrial β-oxidation, and eliminates LDs completely, leading to irreversible TNBC cell death. The system demonstrates potent in vivo antitumor and anti-metastatic efficacy in orthotopic 4T1 models. This work establishes a subcellular-targeted dual degradation strategy, opening a new avenue for TNBC metabolic intervention.

Eragrostis (Poaceae): a case of mismatch between C4 biochemical subtype and Kranz anatomy traits.

Protoplasma · Voznesenskaya EV, Britski DA, Borisenko TA et al. · Full text
Abstract

Kranz anatomy traits have traditionally been used for the identification of C4 biochemical subtypes in C4 grasses. However, distinct mismatches have been reported within the genus Eragrostis. We investigated photosynthetic traits in 37 Eragrostis species, 16 of which were examined for the first time using light microscopy (LM) and 31 via transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Western blot analysis confirmed that all studied species belong to the NAD-ME biochemical subtype, regardless of whether they exhibit centripetal (cp.), centrifugal (cf.), or intermediate bundle sheath (BS) chloroplast positions. Despite this "PEP-CK-like" or intermediate Kranz anatomy, chloroplast ultrastructure, specifically the dimorphism in grana development, consistently matched the classical NAD-ME subtype. Mitochondria positioning strictly follows chloroplast location across all variants while their size and ultrastructure corresponded to NAD-ME biochemistry. Even a minimal spatial displacement of chloroplastic Rubisco and mitochondrial decarboxylases in BS cells from their optimal arrangement near the vascular tissues leads to BS cell wall suberization. Consequently, suberin lamella deposition in BS cell walls, which prevents CO2 leakage, is not rigidly determined by the C4 biochemical subtype. Macroecological analysis demonstrated a strong correlation between specific Kranz anatomy traits, such as BS chloroplast positioning (cp. versus non-cp.), and annual precipitation within the species habitats. Accordingly, species with cp. positioning are confined to drier habitats, suggesting that the geographic distribution of these grasses is primarily driven by specific Kranz anatomy modifications rather than their uniform NAD-ME biochemistry. This decoupling highlights a high evolutionary plasticity of Kranz anatomy in response to environmental gradients.

J Colloid Interface Sci · Wang C, Chen C, Zhu J et al. · Full text
Abstract

Tumor cells exhibit robust redox adaptive capacity, limiting the effectiveness of ROS amplification alone in inducing persistent and irreversible oxidative damage. Accordingly, simultaneously enhancing oxidative stress and disrupting antioxidant defenses represents an effective strategy for improving catalytic cancer therapy. Here, we developed hyaluronic acid-functionalized NLG-919-loaded mesoporous PtPdRhCu medium-entropy alloy colloidal nanoparticles (PPRCNH), an interface-engineered platform integrating porous architecture, multimetallic electronic coupling, surface functionalization, and metabolic regulation. Lesion-targeting and vascular matrix-penetrating siRNA nanotherapeutics for the treatment of abdominal aortic aneurysm.

New todayJ Control Release · Wang S, Zhu R, Lei Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Inefficient aneurysmal aorta enrichment and vascular matrix penetration represent major challenges against siRNA delivery for the management of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). Herein, platelet membrane (PM)-reversibly camouflaged nanocomplexes (NCs) are developed to deliver Poldip2 siRNA (siPoldip2) into vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), which then restore vascular homeostasis by promoting phenotypic switching and mitigating oxidative stress. The NCs comprise a cationic nanocore assembled from siPoldip2 and a guanidinated and fluorinated bifunctional helical polypeptide (8FP), a charge-reversal intermediate layer of poly(l-lysine)-cis-aconitic acid (PC), and an outer shell of PM. After systemic administration in CaCl2-induced AAA rats, the NCs could preferentially accumulate at the aneurysmal site due to PM-mediated recognition of RGD-containing extracellular matrix proteins. The exposed 8FP/siPoldip2 nanocore penetrates the vascular matrix due to fluorocarbon-assisted repulsion of collagen mesh adsorption, followed by effective internalization by VSMCs. Consequently, Poldip2 expression in VSMCs is downregulated, which restores the contractile phenotype, and improves the aortic elasticity as well as structural integrity. This study provides a promising strategy for overcoming vascular delivery barriers and highlights the therapeutic promise of siRNA-based medical intervention for AAA.

AI Applications in Aortic and Peripheral Artery Disease: Risk Prediction, Imaging, Procedural Support, and Longitudinal Surveillance.

New todayReviewMethodist Debakey Cardiovasc J · Robert JD, Bannikov S, Atkins AD et al. · Full text
Abstract

Aortic disease and lower-extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD) generate substantial morbidity, mortality, and longitudinal imaging burden. Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly studied for vascular risk prediction, image analysis, procedural support, and surveillance, but the maturity of these applications is heterogeneous. This review evaluates recent AI applications in aortic disease and PAD, emphasizing the distinction between algorithmic performance, external validation, demonstrated workflow impact, and proven patient benefit. Automated image segmentation and opportunistic abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) detection have the most mature evidence base, including retrospective external validation and, for AI-assisted AAA case finding, nonrandomized evidence of improved downstream evaluation and follow-up. Conversely, while machine-learning models for vascular risk prediction and disease progression often report high retrospective accuracy, their clinical readiness remains strictly investigational. Across most aortic and PAD applications, there is a persistent lack of independent external validation, decision-curve analysis, and prospective testing against standard care. Image-fusion guidance has clinical evidence for reducing procedural resource use, but this evidence should not be conflated with autonomous AI or robotic navigation. Cardiovascular digital twins remain largely mechanistic, computational, or early-development frameworks. AI can augment vascular imaging and risk assessment, but high retrospective accuracy is not equivalent to clinical utility. Current guideline thresholds for repair, revascularization, and surveillance should not be altered on the basis of investigational radiomics or prediction models. Translation requires multicenter external validation, prospective workflow evaluation, calibration and net-benefit assessment, transparent reporting, and trials demonstrating improved patient outcomes.

Abstract

BACKGROUND AND AIM: The molecular mechanisms underlying abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) progression remain incompletely understood and may vary across anatomically distinct regions of the diseased aorta. We hypothesised that the aneurysmal wall and aneurysm neck exhibit distinct molecular profiles, reflecting regional heterogeneity in AAA tissue. To this end, we characterized and compared targeted proteomic and transcriptomic profiles of these two anatomically distinct regions to identify molecular alterations associated with AAA pathology. METHODS: Paired aneurysmal wall and aneurysm neck tissues were obtained from 18 patients with AAA. Inflammatory proteins were quantified using the Olink Target 48 cytokine panel, with 45 proteins retained for analysis after quality-control filtering. Directional RNA sequencing was subsequently performed on matched tissue samples to characterize the expression of genes corresponding to the proteins included in the proteomic analysis and to assess concordance between transcriptomic and proteomic differences. CCL19 being the only concordant signal in both proteomic and transcriptomic analysis.

Estrogen attenuates thoracic aortic aneurysm progression through reduced extracellular matrix remodeling and wall stiffening in a severe Marfan syndrome mouse model.

New todayFront Physiol · Dwivedi KK, Wu Y, Wagenseil JE · Full text
Abstract

Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is a life-threatening cardiovascular disease characterized by progressive aortic dilation and fatal dissection or rupture. Marfan syndrome (MFS)-associated TAA exhibits sexual dimorphism, suggesting a role for sex hormones. Although 17β-estradiol attenuates aortic root dilation in male MFS mice, the contribution of endogenous estrogen to sex-specific TAA progression and its effects on aortic wall extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and wall stiffness remains poorly understood.
