Heart-brain co-ageing: phenotypic heterogeneity, molecular mechanisms and translational interventions.

New todayReviewAgeing Res Rev · Huang H, Zhang W, Hou C et al. · Full text
Abstract

Population ageing has increased the burden of cardiovascular disease, cognitive decline and frailty in older adults. These conditions are often studied separately, yet increasing evidence suggests that the ageing heart, vasculature and brain are biologically interconnected. Using a hybrid design that combines a systematic literature search with narrative synthesis, this review summarizes current knowledge on heart-brain co-ageing, with emphasis on phenotypic heterogeneity, molecular mechanisms and translational interventions. Heart-brain co-ageing is characterized by partially coupled but non-synchronous ageing trajectories of cardiac, vascular and brain function. Major clinical phenotypes include vascular-dominant ageing, cardiac-output-related brain vulnerability, arrhythmia-associated thromboinflammatory injury, frailty-cognitive decline and metabolic-inflammatory co-ageing. These phenotypes differ in risk profiles, dominant mechanisms and potential biomarkers. Candidate shared mechanisms include cellular senescence, chronic low-grade inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, endothelial impairment, blood-brain barrier disruption, epigenetic remodelling, telomere attrition, non-coding ribonucleic acid regulation, Klotho signalling, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide metabolism and microbiome-derived metabolic signalling. Advances in multimodal imaging, circulating biomarkers, biological ageing clocks and multi-omics profiling provide new opportunities for risk stratification, although clinical standardization remains limited. Heart-brain co-ageing should not be regarded as the simple coexistence of cardiovascular and neurological diseases, but as a dynamic multi-organ ageing process shaped by shared biological drivers, interorgan interactions and organ-specific vulnerability, and we propose it as a testable conceptual framework with explicit operational criteria that distinguish coupled from parallel organ ageing. Future research should integrate longitudinal phenotyping, mechanism-based biomarkers and stratified clinical trials to develop precision interventions that preserve both cardiovascular and brain health in older adults.

Ischemic myocardium-targeted NAD+ nanotherapy mitigates myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury.

J Control Release · Xie X, Zhang X, Zhou Z et al. · Full text
Abstract

Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury remains a major cause of cardiomyocyte loss and adverse remodeling following acute myocardial infarction, yet effective therapeutic options remain limited. Although nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) plays a central role in mitochondrial function and redox homeostasis, its therapeutic application is limited by poor membrane permeability and inefficient restoration of intracellular NAD+ pools. Here, we developed a cardiac-targeted lipid-polymer hybrid nanoparticle (LN-DPC) that enables efficient intracellular delivery of NAD+ to ischemic myocardium. The nanoparticale integrates boronate-guanidine-mediated NAD+ encapsulation with an ischemic myocardium-homing peptide (CSTSMLKAC), achieving stable loading, controlled release, and preferential accumulation in injured cardiac tissue. In vitro, LN-DPC enhanced cellular uptake and restored intracellular NAD+ levels, leading to improved mitochondrial function, reduced reactive oxygen species generation, and alleviation of calcium overload under OGD/R conditions. In vivo, the nanoparticles preferentially accumulated in the injured myocardium, attenuated early inflammation, significantly reduced infarct size, improved cardiac function, and markedly limited chronic fibrosis. Mechanistically, NAD+ delivery restored SIRT3 expression, improved mitochondrial homeostasis, and suppressed HMGB1-RAGE signaling to mitigate secondary myocardial injury; pharmacological SIRT3 inhibition attenuated the protective effects of LN-DPC both in vitro and in vivo, supporting a functional contribution of SIRT3 to LN-DPC-mediated cardioprotection. Collectively, this work establishes targeted NAD+ delivery as an effective strategy to restore metabolic homeostasis and protect against ischemia-reperfusion injury, and provides a nanomedicine platform for metabolic intervention in cardiovascular diseases.

Abstract

Microplastics and nanoplastics (MNPs) are pervasive environmental contaminants linked to reproductive and developmental health. Abstract

Novel applications of SIRT1 in Chronic Kidney Disease.

ReviewFront Med (Lausanne) · Han Y, Wang Q, Pan X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) has become a global public health issue, with progressive kidney fibrosis serving as a key pathological process leading to kidney failure, and current therapeutic approaches remain largely ineffective in reversing this progression. SIRT1, as an NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase, plays a central protective role in counteracting kidney fibrosis, inflammation, oxidative stress, and autophagic dysfunction by regulating multiple signaling pathways, and has emerged as a promising target in CKD treatment. This review systematically elaborates on the mechanisms regulating SIRT1 expression and activity in CKD, its protective roles in core pathological processes, and summarizes recent advances in targeting SIRT1 for CKD therapy. Functionally, SIRT1 antagonizes kidney fibrosis by deacetylating Smad3/Smad4 and inhibiting epithelial-mesenchymal transition; curbs inflammatory cascades through dual blockade of NF-κB transcriptional activity and NLRP3 inflammasome assembly; constructs a multi-layered antioxidant defense network by activating the Nrf2/ARE pathway, regulating FOXO transcription factors, and enhancing mitochondrial biogenesis; and restores autophagic flux to clear damaged organelles. In summary, SIRT1 integrates metabolic sensing, transcriptional regulation, and post-translational modification functions, serving as a key molecular hub linking upstream pathological signals to downstream organ damage in CKD. Precise modulation of SIRT1 activity may offer a novel multi-target synergistic therapeutic strategy for CKD.

Mouse Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm Model Induced by Perivascular Sheath Microinjection of Chymotrypsin.

J Vis Exp · Shen L, Zeng J, Huang R et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening degenerative vascular disease for which no effective pharmacological therapy currently exists. Reliable and technically accessible animal models are essential for mechanistic and preclinical investigations. However, existing murine platforms are limited by technical complexity, prolonged operative time, or incomplete recapitulation of human AAA pathology. Here, this study presents a novel murine AAA model established by direct perivascular sheath microinjection of chymotrypsin. After midline laparotomy and identification of the infrarenal abdominal aorta, chymotrypsin working solution is injected directly into the perivascular sheath space under direct vision using a microscale needle. To determine the optimal enzyme concentration, a pilot dose-finding study was conducted using 0.125% (w/v; 1.25 mg/mL), 0.25% (w/v; 2.50 mg/mL), and 0.375% (w/v; 3.75 mg/mL) chymotrypsin (n = 5 per group), with PBS as negative control and topical PPE as positive control. Based on aneurysm incidence, aortic diameter progression, and mortality, 0.25% chymotrypsin was selected for the main study. The chymotrypsin group was directly compared with the PPE positive control group to benchmark the pathological features of this novel model. Serial Doppler ultrasonography confirmed a 100% aneurysm induction rate at day 14 in chymotrypsin-treated mice, whereas no dilation occurred in PBS-injected controls. Histological analyses revealed pronounced medial thickening, elastic fiber fragmentation, and collagen deposition, together with markedly elevated immunohistochemical expression of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-2 and MMP-9 in aortic sections. Quantitative morphometry demonstrated significant increases in aortic wall thickness and collagen volume fraction, and a corresponding reduction in elastic fiber content in chymotrypsin-treated animals compared with controls. This protocol thus provides a technically streamlined, highly reproducible, and translationally relevant murine AAA model. Because the procedure avoids aortic dissection, arteriotomy, or specialized microsurgical training, it is well suited to laboratories investigating aneurysm pathobiology or evaluating novel pharmacological interventions.

Context-dependent roles of resistin-like molecule β in mucosal defense and inflammatory diseases.

ReviewFront Immunol · Ai Q, Chen W, Chen W et al. · Full text
Abstract

Resistin-like molecule β (RELMβ) is a cysteine-rich secreted protein predominantly produced by intestinal goblet cells and regulated by microbial colonization, epithelial transcriptional programs, and immune signals. At the intestinal mucosal surface, recombinant murine and human RELMβ permeabilize susceptible bacterial membranes, while genetic studies in mice support its role in maintaining spatial separation between the microbiota and the epithelium. Murine studies further implicate RELMβ in defense against selected gastrointestinal nematodes, epithelial repair, intestinal inflammation, and the microbiota-dependent establishment of oral tolerance. Beyond the intestine, RELMβ has been associated with pulmonary vascular and airway remodeling, metabolic liver disease, atherosclerosis, abdominal aortic aneurysm, and selected cancers. However, the strength of this evidence varies: most causal findings derive from rodent genetic or intervention models, whereas human evidence is based largely on tissue associations and cultured-cell experiments. Mechanistically, RELMβ may act through at least two non-exclusive modes: direct membrane disruption and modulation of host-cell responses, but the relationship between these activities remains unclear. Its physiological oligomeric state, context-specific molecular interaction partners, and cellular source in the circulation and extraintestinal lesions also remain unresolved. This review distinguishes direct RELMβ evidence from inferences based on other RELM-family proteins and proposes a compartment-interaction system framework that considers protein source, route of exposure, molecular assembly, local physicochemical conditions, and available interaction partners as potential determinants of biological outcome. This framework is presented as a testable synthesis for defining experimental priorities and assessing the biological and therapeutic relevance of RELMβ.

Adamantinoma-like Ewing sarcoma (ALES) is a cytokeratin-positive member of the Ewing sarcoma (ES) family with a predilection for the head and neck (HN). Its rarity and overlapping immunophenotype with carcinomas contribute to frequent misclassification, while its biologic behavior and response to standard ES therapy remain incompletely defined. We conducted a 25-year retrospective review to characterize the clinicopathologic spectrum of ALES and compare HN ALES with non-HN ALES and HN ES, incorporating histologic, immunophenotypic, molecular, and clinical outcome data. Twenty-three cases were identified; ALES (n=20) and HN ES (n=3). ALES occurred in older patients (median age 49 vs. 29 years) and demonstrated a marked HN predilection (17/20), with a substantial proportion involving glandular sites. In contrast, HN ES arose in the skull base and sinonasal tract. ALES showed basaloid, epithelioid, or round cell morphology, with occasional palisading and rare squamous differentiation. Diffuse cytokeratin expression was present in all ALES, with frequent p40/p63 positivity, whereas ES showed only focal cytokeratin staining. Nodal metastases occurred in 9/20 ALES cases. EWSR1 rearrangement was identified in all ALES and ES cases, with FLI1 fusion identified in 18/20 ALES and 1/3 ES cases. With a mean follow-up of 60.6 months, outcomes varied by site: salivary gland ALES showed a more favorable course (4/5 in remission), whereas thyroid (6/8) and non-HN ALES (3/3) more often progressed. HN ES demonstrated poor outcomes. ALES is a distinct entity that frequently mimics carcinoma and extends beyond classic morphologic patterns. Site-specific differences observed in our study may aid in diagnosis; however, accurate recognition remains difficult, and a high index of suspicion at specific anatomical sites may be important for diagnosis and management.

Genomic and Transcriptomic Characterization of Angiosarcomas: Focus on Cardiac Origin.

New todayMod Pathol · Lu T, Liu Y, Yuan M et al. · Full text
Abstract

Cardiac angiosarcoma is a rare and aggressive malignancy with limited therapeutic options, and its molecular features remain poorly defined. This study sought to characterize the genomic and transcriptomic landscape of cardiac angiosarcoma and to explore molecular features associated with clinical outcomes, chemotherapy response, and potential therapeutic vulnerabilities. We retrospectively analyzed 35 patients with cardiac angiosarcoma who underwent whole-exome sequencing (n = 11) and/or whole-transcriptome sequencing (n = 33). An additional 35 patients with angiosarcoma from noncardiac or unknown primary sites were included for comparative genomic analyses. Cardiac angiosarcoma frequently harbored alterations in KDR (55%), PLCG1 (45%), and CCND1 (36%), with recurrent amplifications at 8p23.1, 8p23.2, and 4q12 and recurrent deletions at 17q21.31, 15q13.2, 8p23.1, and 2q11.2. Transcriptomic analyses identified differential expression patterns associated with relapse, mortality, and chemotherapy response. Tumors from the low-relapse-risk group were enriched for axon guidance, whereas those from the high-relapse-risk group were enriched for cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction. Approximately one-third of patients exhibited high infiltration of most immune cell populations. Potentially actionable genomic alterations were identified in 71.7% of angiosarcomas. Compared with noncardiac angiosarcomas, cardiac tumors showed higher frequencies of KDR, CCND1, MCL1, and PLCG1 alterations and unique enrichment of the apelin signaling and vascular smooth muscle contraction pathways. Genomic and transcriptomic profiling of cardiac angiosarcoma revealed a distinct molecular landscape with candidate prognostic and therapeutic markers, providing a foundation for future precision oncology strategies in this rare disease.

Abstract

Dermal nerve sheath myxoma (DNSM) is a rare benign mesenchymal tumor with presumed nerve sheath differentiation with distinctive morphologic and immunophenotypic features; however, its molecular pathogenesis remains poorly understood. We investigated a series of nine morphologically classic DNSMs and performed molecular testing to identify recurrent genetic alterations. The cohort included five female and four male patients (median age: 56 years; range: 23-75 years). Tumors arose predominantly on the extremities and presented as multinodular dermal-based myxoid neoplasms composed of uniform spindled to epithelioid cells arranged in reticular cords within abundant myxoid stroma. These findings further support the classification of DNSM as a distinct neoplastic entity and highlight the potential utility of molecular testing as a diagnostic adjunct in morphologically challenging cases. Abstract

Klippel-Trénaunay syndrome (KTS) is a rare congenital vascular-overgrowth disorder within the broader spectrum of PIK3CA-related overgrowth syndromes. Although clinical, radiologic, and orthopedic manifestations are well described, the histologic spectrum of KTS-associated pathology remains under-characterized. Clinicopathologic findings from 21 patients with clinically established KTS were reviewed, including 18 with longitudinal clinical follow-up and 3 consultation cases of rare malignant mesenchymal neoplasms. Abstract

Fibrous papule (FP) is a common benign dermal mesenchymal lesion presenting as a solitary, dome-shaped papule mostly located on the nose. Abstract

A wide variety of mesenchymal lesions arise in the vulvovaginal region, some relatively site-specific. We report 4 cases of a novel vulvovaginal mesenchymal lesion, which we term vulvovaginal epithelioid mesenchymal neoplasm with "deciduoid" morphology, reflecting the predominant morphologic feature of epithelioid cells with a granular glassy "deciduoid" appearance. The tumors arose in women aged 34 to 48 years and involved the vagina (2 cases) or the vulval Bartholin's gland region (2 cases). Ex vivo fluorescence confocal microscopy for real-time margin assessment of nonmelanoma skin cancer in Mohs surgery: A prospective interventional study.

Abstract

New-generation ex vivo fluorescence confocal microscopy (EVFCM) generates hematoxylin-eosin-like images directly comparable to conventional histopathology. Its interobserver performance with a novel acquisition mode in non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC) micrographically controlled surgery remains to be defined. In this prospective interventional study, 42 tumors from 39 patients undergoing Mohs surgery between January and October 2025 were examined with EVFCM. Specimens were orthogonally sectioned, stained, and acquired in digital-stain mode. Peripheral and deep margins were independently and blindly reviewed by a dermatologist and a pathologist, both trained in EVFCM interpretation. Concordant negative margins were considered tumor-free, whereas positive, discordant, or equivocal findings prompted re-excision and re-evaluation. Diagnostic agreement with histopathology was assessed using Cohen's κ statistic. A total of 261 margins were evaluated. Inter-reader agreement between the dermatologist and pathologist was almost perfect for both margin assessment (κ=0.863; SE 0.067; p<0.001; observed agreement 98.6%) and histotype assessment (κ=0.882; SE 0.031; p<0.001; observed agreement 97.6%). Compared with histopathology, EVFCM showed high performance, with an overall diagnostic accuracy of 96.4%. The AUC for the combined assessment was 0.875 (SE 0.074; 95% CI 0.730-1.000). Only two discrepancies were observed between EVFCM and histopathology: one false-negative margin assessment and one discordance attributable to differences in sample processing. These findings support EVFCM as a reliable tool for intraoperative margin assessment in NMSC. Comparable performance between dermatologist and pathologist readers supports its potential integration into Mohs surgery workflows; however, potential workflow advantages of the standardized sample-processing approach require prospective validation using quantitative time metrics.

Parathyroid Pathology Uncovered: Tiny Glands, Big Questions.

Abstract

Parathyroid neoplasms encountered in routine practice are most often diagnostically straightforward and can usually be classified on the basis of conventional histomorphology without the need for extensive biomarker work-up. This relative simplicity has contributed to the perception that pathologic assessment of parathyroid tumors is largely uncomplicated. Yet this view rapidly changes when a lesion exhibits atypical features beyond those expected in a conventional adenoma, particularly when the differential diagnosis includes parathyroid carcinoma, an exceptionally rare but potentially fatal malignancy. In these difficult cases, the endocrine pathologist carries substantial responsibility in separating misleading and overinterpreted atypical findings from lesions that truly harbor malignant potential or an increased risk of recurrence. Beyond classification, pathology also has a central role in identifying molecular abnormalities of diagnostic or therapeutic relevance in parathyroid carcinoma, as well as recognizing morphologic or immunophenotypic clues that may point to an underlying hereditary syndrome. This review provides a focused analysis of the major histopathologic challenges in primary hyperparathyroidism. Particular emphasis is placed on distinguishing ordinary adenoma from normal glands, identifying atypical parathyroid tumors and parathyroid carcinoma while avoiding overdiagnosis, integrating immunohistochemical and molecular findings into diagnostic practice, and identifying features suggestive of hereditary disease. Our aim is to provide a practical framework that supports more accurate, reproducible, and clinically relevant pathologic assessment of parathyroid tumors.

Diagnostic Utility of USP6 RNA Chromogenic In Situ Hybridization in the Differential Diagnosis of Nodular Fasciitis and Aneurysmal Bone Cyst.

Am J Surg Pathol · de Toro MM, Tan J, Li PL et al. · Full text
Abstract

Recurrent USP6 gene rearrangements underlie a subset of mesenchymal neoplasms, including nodular fasciitis and aneurysmal bone cyst. These rearrangements place USP6 under the regulatory control of another gene's constitutively active promoter, resulting in USP6 overexpression. Recently, it has been demonstrated in a limited number of cases that detection of USP6 overexpression by RNA chromogenic in situ hybridization (CISH) can serve as a surrogate marker for USP6-rearranged tumors. To further assess the diagnostic utility of USP6 RNA CISH, we evaluated its application in 1210 bone and soft tissue tumors, using a combination of whole tissue sections and tissue microarrays. USP6 overexpression, defined as RNA CISH staining in >1% of lesional cells, was observed in 92% (36/39) of nodular fasciitis cases and in 85% (17/20) of primary aneurysmal bone cysts. All examples of nodular fasciitis (15/15) and primary aneurysmal bone cyst (8/8) with USP6 fusion confirmed by molecular genetic testing were positive for overexpression. Although USP6 RNA CISH proved to be highly specific for USP6-rearranged lesions when assessed in 1145 negative control cases reflecting 53 other tumor types, positivity was rarely seen in isolated cases of angiosarcoma (1/16, 6%), malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor (1/80, 1%), and carcinosarcoma (1/7, 14%). Taken together, these findings indicate that USP6 RNA CISH represents a useful diagnostic tool; careful interpretation remains critical to its successful implementation, given that isolated examples of non-USP6-rearranged tumors rarely stain positive.

Clinicopathological Characteristics of Merkel Cell Carcinoma in Younger Patients: A Retrospective Study.

Abstract

Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) is a rare and aggressive cutaneous neuroendocrine carcinoma that predominantly affects older adults. MCC arising in younger patients is exceedingly rare, and its clinicopathologic features remain poorly described. We retrospectively reviewed clinical records, histopathology, and outcomes of patients aged 18 to 50 years diagnosed with MCC at a single tertiary care center between 2009 and 2025. A total of 13 patients were identified with a median age of 40 years (range: 18 to 50 y); 61.5% were female. Of 11 patients with available data, 36.4% had pre-existing immune suppression. Tumors arose most commonly on the extremities (46.2%), followed by the head and neck (23.1%), trunk (15.4%), and unknown primary (15.4%).
