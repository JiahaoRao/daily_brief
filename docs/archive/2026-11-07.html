From NAD+ elevation to clinical benefit: tissue-specific regulation and translational barriers in aging and age-related disease.

New todayReviewPharmacol Res · Pei Z, Zhong M, Jiang Q et al. · Full text
Abstract

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) decline has been implicated in aging and age-related disease, and precursor supplementation with nicotinamide riboside (NR) or nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) has shown efficacy in preclinical models. Human trials, however, have produced inconsistent functional outcomes, and the sources of this translational gap remain poorly defined. We combined a bibliometric analysis of 3,271 aging-focused NAD+ publications (2004-2025) with a critical synthesis of mechanistic and clinical evidence to localize where and why preclinical findings fail to translate. The bibliometric analysis revealed three structural features of the literature: (i) original research articles dominated the corpus, whereas the document-type classification cannot distinguish preclinical experiments from human observational or interventional studies; (ii) research is siloed across disease domains with limited cross-tissue comparison; and (iii) intervention-oriented keywords (NMN, NR, supplementation, drug delivery) have outpaced empirical clinical validation. Mechanistic synthesis showed that these structural gaps correspond to biological heterogeneity: NAD+ biosynthesis, consumption, redox balance, and subcellular compartmentalization are tissue- and compartment-specific, such that systemic precursor elevation does not uniformly restore functional NAD+ in disease-relevant tissues. Four subcellular pools-nuclear, cytosolic, mitochondrial, and axonal-each possess distinct rate-limiting bottlenecks, and hepatic metabolic reserve introduces an overlooked source of inter-individual variability. Across neurodegeneration, metabolic disease, cardiovascular aging, and sarcopenia, NAD+ dysregulation follows disease-specific mechanisms rather than a universal decline phenotype. We propose a precision-medicine framework that shifts the core question from whether NAD+ can be elevated to whether a defined intervention corrects a specific NAD+ defect in the appropriate tissue and subcellular compartment. This framework provides a structured basis for designing next-generation NAD+-targeted trials with tissue-specific biomarkers and function-oriented endpoints.

Mitoxantrone-integrated Pt/Ti₃C₂ MXene nanozyme for metabolism-regulating mild-temperature photothermal-sensitized apoptosis-ferroptosis therapy in renal cell carcinoma.

Colloids Surf B Biointerfaces · Mao X, Wang W, Zhu X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is characterized by metabolic plasticity and redox dysregulation, yet therapeutic outcomes remain constrained by adaptive resistance. Herein, a Pt/Ti₃C₂ MXene-based multifunctional nanozyme (TP@MH) was developed to enhance mild-temperature photothermal therapy (mPTT) through combined catalytic/redox regulation and metabolic intervention. Platinum nanoparticles were in situ anchored onto Ti₃C₂ nanosheets to construct a catalytic heterostructure, followed by mitoxantrone (MTO) loading and hyaluronic acid (HA)-mediated CD44-targeting. The obtained TP@MH exhibited peroxidase and GSH-depleting capability, promoting ROS generation and perturbing tumor redox homeostasis. Upon near-infrared irradiation, TP@MH nanozyme not only modulated the apparent POD-like kinetics under the tested conditions but also perturbed intracellular NAD⁺/NADH metabolism and reduced ATP availability, which was associated with decreased HSP70 expression and potentially weakened cellular thermal adaptation. MTO provided an additional chemotherapeutic component, while TP@MH treatment induced mitochondrial dysfunction, enhanced lipid peroxidation, reduced glutathione peroxidase 4 expression, and promoted apoptosis and ferroptosis. Both in vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated that TP@MH achieved efficient antitumor efficacy through the integrated effects of mild photothermal activation, redox regulation, metabolic intervention, and chemotherapy. Collectively, this work provides a multifunctional nanozyme strategy for integrating catalytic, metabolic, photothermal, and chemotherapeutic interventions in RCC therapy.

PARP-CD38-Driven NAD+ Loss in Immune Aging: An Emerging Mechanistic Framework.

Aging Dis · Ali U, Tan R, Ding S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Immune aging is characterized by inflammaging, progressive immune decline, and compromised tissue surveillance. Although researchers have recognized an association between PARP hyperactivation and immune aging (emerging and context-dependent), a unifying framework for how PARP members beyond PARP1 orchestrate immune dysfunction via NAD+ metabolism is lacking. We propose a context-dependent PARP-CD38-NAD+-inflammaging hierarchy. PARP1 is the most abundant nuclear PARP, responsible for 85-90% of nuclear PARP activity and dominant NAD+ consumption under acute DNA damage; CD38, the principal NAD+ glycohydrolase, is upregulated in inflamed aged tissues; NAD+ is compartmentalized (cytosolic, mitochondrial) with distinct regulators (NAMPT, NMNAT1/2/3, SLC25A51). Recent human data challenge the universal NAD+ decline hypothesis, showing that an 85% muscle NAD+ reduction does not impair function, and whole-blood NAD+ is age-invariant. Thus, NAD+ decline is tissue- and compartment-specific; blood NAD+ is not a reliable biomarker. Beyond PARP1, PARP2 (cardiomyocyte aging in mouse), PARP3 (intestinal aging), PARP16 (vascular aging), and PARP9/14 (Alzheimer's models) act as tissue-specific regulators, but their roles in human immune aging remain unquantified. PARP inhibitors and NAD+ precursors show promise but face risks: genomic instability, hematopoietic toxicity, senescence promotion, and inconsistent efficacy. Unresolved questions, whether PBMC PARP activity predicts immune aging, sex differences, and whether partial inhibition reduces NAD+ consumption without compromising DNA repair. By rethinking PARPs as context-dependent integrators, we provide a framework for immunogerontology.

Mitochondrial metabolic remodeling and blood-brain barrier dysfunction after repetitive mild traumatic brain injury.

ReviewFront Mol Neurosci · Gao L, Xue X, Deng X et al. · Full text
Abstract

Repetitive mild traumatic brain injury (rmTBI) may arise when concussive or subconcussive impacts recur before physiological recovery is complete. Mitochondrial dysfunction and blood-brain barrier (BBB) impairment are shared features across the traumatic brain injury (TBI) spectrum; in rmTBI, repetition may alter their magnitude, duration, and temporal coupling. This focused narrative review evaluates evidence that mitochondrial metabolic remodeling contributes to neurovascular-unit dysfunction after rmTBI. We discuss energy failure, mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, succinate, fumarate, alpha-ketoglutarate, NAD + depletion, and lactate as candidate links between metabolic stress and BBB injury. Available studies support a model in which recurrent metabolic stress, redox imbalance, inflammatory signaling, and impaired mitochondrial quality control converge on endothelial and multicellular neurovascular-unit dysfunction. Metabolism-directed interventions are promising but require validation across rigorously defined rmTBI paradigms and clinical cohorts.

Acta Biomater · Eliathamby D, Ung L, Yap H et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: To clarify aortic microstructure-function relationships, we used improved, disease-motivated enzymatic digestion models of medial degeneration to evaluate individual extracellular matrix (ECM) components (i.e., elastin, collagen, and proteoglycans) and their influence on biomechanical properties of ascending aortic tissue. METHODS: Thin, 200 µm medial porcine ascending aortic sections were progressively digested with elastase or collagenase for selective degradation of these ECM components. Full thickness human aortic tissues were treated with chondroitinase, hyaluronidase, and heparinase to completely remove proteoglycans. Low- and high-strain modulus, transition-zone behaviour, strain-energy density, and energy loss were derived from planar biaxial tensile testing. Degradation of elastin was captured with two photon excitation fluorescence imaging. Degradation of collagen was captured with picrosirius red staining and polarized light microscopy. Proteoglycan content and organization was confirmed by Alcian blue staining. RESULTS: Degradation of elastin fibers reduced low-strain modulus, strain-energy density, transition zone stress, and energy loss. Collagen degradation similarly reduced strain-energy density and decreased strain at the onset of transition, with reductions in high strain modulus and energy loss with increased degradation. Proteoglycan removal decreased energy loss and reduced strain-energy density and strain at onset of transition, and increased high strain modulus. CONCLUSIONS: Through targeted modelling of ECM degenerative features, we have delineated the overlapping effects of major matrix constituents, underscoring the complex, multifactorial nature of structure-function relationships in aortic disease. We also identified multiple microstructural pathways to increasing energy loss and the vulnerability of aortic tissue to failure. STATEMENT OF SIGNIFICANCE: Thoracic aortic dissection results from the progressive degeneration of the aortic wall extracellular matrix, yet the contributions of individual matrix constituents to this mechanical failure remain understudied. To address this gap, we developed a disease-motivated enzymatic model that enables controlled, graded modification of key extracellular matrix constituents in aortic tissue, better replicating the partial degeneration observed in vivo than existing approaches. Using this framework, we systematically evaluated the biomechanical consequences of elastin, collagen, and proteoglycan degeneration. Here, we revealed multiple microstructural pathways to altered viscoelastic behaviour, which is associated with increased susceptibility to aortic failure. These findings provide new mechanistic insight into structure-function relationships in aortic disease and help explain how matrix degeneration contributes to tissue vulnerability and dissection risk.

Abstract

OBJECTIVE: To characterise territory-specific sex-associated vascular phenotypes in genetically confirmed vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome and assess whether associations persisted after accounting for COL3A1 molecular mechanism. The gut microbiome and vascular disease - A systematic review of mechanistic insights and implications for vascular surgery.

New todayReviewJVS Vasc Sci · Castro E Silva M, Silva E, Nunes C et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Observational studies repeatedly linked gut microbial features and metabolites to vascular disease, but it is unclear which associations are reproducible, which remain significant after confounder adjustment, and how they relate to experimental evidence. This review aims to determine which taxa and metabolites are consistently associated across independent human studies; whether the population, phenotype, method, and sample type explain divergent findings; and where the human data converge with, diverge from, or remain untested with the animal and interventional literature. METHODS: PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library were searched for human studies of the gut microbiome composition or gut-derived metabolites reporting atherosclerotic, aneurysmal, peripheral arterial, calcific, or arterial stiffness outcomes from database inception to December 31, 2025. Study screening and duplicate article extraction were conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses 2020 guidelines. The study design was classified explicitly, the covariates in each primary analysis were extracted, and the risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale or the Joanna Briggs Institute checklist. The findings were then compared with the animal and human interventional literature identified through targeted searching. RESULTS: All the 29 included reports were observational (19 cross-sectional, 3 case-control, and 7 cohort) studies, and none was interventional. The reproducible findings were the enrichment of Proteobacteria-affiliated genera (5 reports) and of oral-origin organisms (3 reports), and the depletion of butyrate producers in carotid disease. The carnitine pathway intermediates gamma-butyrobetaine and trimethyllysine were independently associated with cardiovascular death and major adverse limb events (hazard ratio, 1.93; 95% confidence interval, 1.35-2.76) in two independent cohorts. Trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) was outcome-specific; TMAO levels were associated with 5-year mortality in peripheral arterial disease (adjusted hazard ratio, 2.06; 95% confidence interval, 1.36-3.11) and with ankle-brachial-index-defined disease in diabetes (odds ratio, 1.051), but TMAO levels were lower in large-artery stroke than in controls and were unrelated to carotid or limb outcomes once renal function was modelled. In addition, 16 of 29 reports fitted a multivariable model, and only three included renal function; the compositional findings were almost exclusively unadjusted. The risk of bias was good in 11 reports, moderate in 16, and low in 2. Animal models causally implicate TMAO in atherogenesis and aneurysm formation and show butyrate and Roseburia intestinalis to be protective, but the results of germ-free experiments and the only human fecal transplantation trial are discordant. CONCLUSIONS: A small number of gut microbial signals are reproducible, but most have not been adjusted for potential confounders, particularly renal function, which may partly explain the observed associations. The carnitine pathway metabolites with the strongest prognostic signal in surgical populations have never been tested in an animal vascular model. Microbiome profiling has no current role in vascular surgical practice.

Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening disease characterized by chronic vascular inflammation and extracellular matrix degradation. This study aimed to investigate the effects of DNA methyltransferase 1 (DNMT1) inhibitor 5-azacytidine (5-aza) on AAA development and its underlying mechanisms. These findings identify epigenetic regulation as a key mechanism in AAA pathogenesis. Given that 5-azacytidine is already clinically available, targeting DNA methylation may represent a potential translational strategy to inhibit AAA formation, although optimization of dosing and safety will be required.

New todayEur J Vasc Endovasc Surg · Zhang Y, Zhang ZW, Huang XT et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: The aim of this study was to develop and validate a multimodal deep learning (DL) model integrating clinical and computed tomography venography (CTV) features for predicting concomitant acute pulmonary embolism (PE) risk in patients with lower extremity deep venous thrombosis (LEDVT). METHODS: This multicentre retrospective study enrolled patients with LEDVT undergoing both computed tomography pulmonary angiography and lower extremity CTV. Centre 1 data were used for model training and internal validation, while data from centres 2 and 3 were used for external testing. Decision curve analysis indicated higher net benefit for the SVM combined model, alongside superior calibration, and positive net reclassification improvement and integrated discrimination improvement values. CONCLUSION: The SVM combined model integrating clinical, imaging, and DL features demonstrated robust performance, offering a promising tool for predicting concomitant acute PE in patients with LEDVT.
