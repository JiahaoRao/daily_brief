Brain renin-angiotensin system: Pathophysiological mechanisms and therapeutic targets in ischemic stroke.

Neural Regen Res · Xie A, Li W, Chen B et al. · Full text
Abstract

This review aims to delineate the context-dependent, opposing actions of the angiotensin II type 1 receptor, angiotensin II type 2 receptor, and angiotensin-converting enzyme 2/ angiotensin-(1-7)/Mas receptor axis in ischemic stroke and to discuss their translational potential for neuroprotective therapy. Although recanalization cannot guarantee favorable functional outcomes for all patients with ischemic stroke, adjunctive neuroprotective targets remain limited. Dysregulation of the brain renin-angiotensin system is increasingly recognized as a potentially modifiable pathway in post-stroke brain injury. Cerebral ischemia shifts the physiological receptor balance toward angiotensin II type 1 receptor predominance, promoting vasoconstriction, blood-brain barrier disruption, neuroinflammation, excitotoxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptosis. Conversely, the angiotensin II type 2 receptor and Mas receptor may counter these detrimental processes through temporally distinct mechanisms. The angiotensin II type 2 receptor may provide early neuroprotection through endothelial nitric oxide synthase-mediated vasodilation, suppression of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase, and inhibition of nuclear factor kappa-B signaling. By comparison, Mas receptor activation may facilitate delayed brain repair through mitochondrial stabilization, nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2-mediated antioxidant responses, and preservation of blood-brain barrier integrity. This temporal framework, characterized by acute injury associated with angiotensin II type 1 receptor signaling, early protection associated with angiotensin II type 2 receptor signaling, and long-term recovery potentially supported by Mas receptor signaling, provides a rationale for time-sensitive therapeutic strategies. Preclinical evidence supports the neuroprotective effects of angiotensin II type 1 receptor blockers. Angiotensin II type 2 receptor agonists, such as compound 21, and glycosylated angiotensin-(1-7) peptide derivatives are promising approaches for engaging endogenous protective pathways.

Dual-targeted nanoparticle chimeras induce bioenergetic crisis by coupling lipid droplet degradation with nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide reduction in triple-negative breast cancer.

Acta Biomater · Zhang Y, Wang K, Fang B et al. · Full text
Abstract

As the most prevalent malignancy in female population, triple-negative breast cancer is characterized by unfavorable prognosis and therapy resistance, driven by a significant and progressive accumulation of lipid droplets (LDs). As dynamic organelles, LDs play critical roles in lipid storage, oxidative stress mitigation, and metabolic buffering. Here, we developed a cationic lipid droplet-targeted nanoparticle chimera (LD·NanoTAC) functionalized with an LD-targeting moiety to achieve precise subcellular delivery. Meanwhile, the cationic charges boost autophagic flux, thus promoting selective lipophagy and subsequent degradation of LDs. To interfere with tumor cell metabolism, we focused on nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT), a rate-limiting enzyme in the mammalian nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) salvage pathway. Also, extracellular NAMPT promotes tumor progression through non-enzymatic mechanisms. Hence, a NAMPT-targeted protein degrader was encapsulated in LD·NanoTAC to afford LD·NanoTACNAMPT, inducing targeted degradation of NAMPT, leading to NAD+ reduction and subsequent inhibition of Warburg effect in tumor cells. The dual degradation triggered a catastrophic energetic impairment in tumor cells. Concurrently, the compensatory mitochondrial β-oxidation of lipophagy-derived fatty acids was suppressed, leading to complete LD clearance and a paradoxical state of substrate overload accompanied by bioenergetic failure. This cellular dysfunction subsequently initiated a cascade of events including reactive oxygen species accumulation, onset of mitochondrial dysfunction, and ultimately irreversible tumor cell death. In vivo, the dual-degradation strategy using LD·NanoTACNAMPT demonstrated potent antitumor efficacy in both orthotopic and metastatic 4T1 tumor models. It significantly suppressed primary tumor growth and pulmonary metastasis, and extended the median survival of 4T1 tumor-bearing mice. STATEMENT OF SIGNIFICANCE: Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) remains a major clinical challenge with poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options, largely driven by progressive accumulation of lipid droplets (LDs) that fuel tumor metabolic reprogramming. Existing protein degradation strategies face critical bottlenecks in subcellular precision and synergistic metabolic disruption. Here, we propose a cationic LD-targeted nanoparticle chimera (LD·NanoTAC) platform, which achieves concurrent targeted degradation of LDs and the key metabolic enzyme nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT). This dual-degradation paradigm triggers catastrophic energetic collapse, suppresses compensatory mitochondrial β-oxidation, and eliminates LDs, leading to irreversible TNBC cell death. The system demonstrates potent in vivo antitumor and anti-metastatic efficacy in orthotopic 4T1 models. This work establishes a subcellular-targeted dual degradation strategy, opening a new avenue for TNBC metabolic intervention.

Eragrostis (Poaceae): a case of mismatch between C4 biochemical subtype and Kranz anatomy traits.

Protoplasma · Voznesenskaya EV, Britski DA, Borisenko TA et al. · Full text
Abstract

Kranz anatomy traits have traditionally been used for the identification of C4 biochemical subtypes in C4 grasses. However, distinct mismatches have been reported within the genus Eragrostis. We investigated photosynthetic traits in 37 Eragrostis species, 16 of which were examined for the first time using light microscopy (LM) and 31 via transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Western blot analysis confirmed that all studied species belong to the NAD-ME biochemical subtype, regardless of whether they exhibit centripetal (cp.), centrifugal (cf.), or intermediate bundle sheath (BS) chloroplast positions. Despite this "PEP-CK-like" or intermediate Kranz anatomy, chloroplast ultrastructure, specifically the dimorphism in grana development, consistently matched the classical NAD-ME subtype. Mitochondria positioning strictly follows chloroplast location across all variants while their size and ultrastructure corresponded to NAD-ME biochemistry. Even a minimal spatial displacement of chloroplastic Rubisco and mitochondrial decarboxylases in BS cells from their optimal arrangement near the vascular tissues leads to BS cell wall suberization. Consequently, suberin lamella deposition in BS cell walls, which prevents CO2 leakage, is not rigidly determined by the C4 biochemical subtype. Macroecological analysis demonstrated a strong correlation between specific Kranz anatomy traits, such as BS chloroplast positioning (cp. versus non-cp.), and annual precipitation within the species habitats. Accordingly, species with cp. positioning are confined to drier habitats, suggesting that the geographic distribution of these grasses is primarily driven by specific Kranz anatomy modifications rather than their uniform NAD-ME biochemistry. This decoupling highlights a high evolutionary plasticity of Kranz anatomy in response to environmental gradients.

Developing structure and strategies for analysis of circulating biomarkers to detect and monitor abdominal aortic aneurysms.

New todayAm J Physiol Heart Circ Physiol · Lindsey ML, Shivam P, Nde PN et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a multifactorial disease characterized by progressive remodeling of the aortic wall. Current clinical management includes screening ultrasound for diagnosis, and routine aortic imaging to monitor growth in patients with small AAA and surgical exclusion of larger AAA. With the evolution of a diverse array of multianalyte assessments from peripheral blood, comprehensive assessments of circulating biomarkers in patients with AAA is possible. In the context of this dynamic disease process, circulating markers - paired with clinical and imaging data - provide an opportunity to identify specific active biologic processes that influence the natural history of the disease. We propose a disease-specific framework for understanding relationships between circulating markers and imaging within a novel three class structure. Class I associations correlate with aortic size and presence of AAA and may be related to the mechanisms of initial formation. Class II associations track with change in aneurysm MTD and may be related to mechanisms of AAA growth and rupture risk. Class II may be clinically relevant for the identification of targets for modification that could alter the natural history of established AAA. Class III associations relate to static or dynamic morphologic features such as mural thrombus or calcifications that have less well-defined associations with clinical outcomes. Identified and appropriately classified markers can provide specific avenues for novel therapeutic and diagnostic options to reduce morbidity and mortality in patients with AAA.

Lesion-targeting and vascular matrix-penetrating siRNA nanotherapeutics for the treatment of abdominal aortic aneurysm.

J Control Release · Wang S, Zhu R, Lei Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Inefficient aneurysmal aorta enrichment and vascular matrix penetration represent major challenges against siRNA delivery for the management of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). Herein, platelet membrane (PM)-reversibly camouflaged nanocomplexes (NCs) are developed to deliver Poldip2 siRNA (siPoldip2) into vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), which then restore vascular homeostasis by promoting phenotypic switching and mitigating oxidative stress. The NCs comprise a cationic nanocore assembled from siPoldip2 and a guanidinated and fluorinated bifunctional helical polypeptide (8FP), a charge-reversal intermediate layer of poly(l-lysine)-cis-aconitic acid (PC), and an outer shell of PM. After systemic administration in CaCl2-induced AAA rats, the NCs could preferentially accumulate at the aneurysmal site due to PM-mediated recognition of RGD-containing extracellular matrix proteins. The slightly acidic aneurysmal microenvironment subsequently triggers charge reversal of PC from negative to positive, which promotes the detachment of both PM and PC layers. The exposed 8FP/siPoldip2 nanocore penetrates the vascular matrix due to fluorocarbon-assisted repulsion of collagen mesh adsorption, followed by effective internalization by VSMCs. Consequently, Poldip2 expression in VSMCs is downregulated, which restores the contractile phenotype, and improves the aortic elasticity as well as structural integrity. This study provides a promising strategy for overcoming vascular delivery barriers and highlights the therapeutic promise of siRNA-based medical intervention for AAA.

AI Applications in Aortic and Peripheral Artery Disease: Risk Prediction, Imaging, Procedural Support, and Longitudinal Surveillance.

ReviewMethodist Debakey Cardiovasc J · Robert JD, Bannikov S, Atkins AD et al. · Full text
Abstract

Aortic disease and lower-extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD) generate substantial morbidity, mortality, and longitudinal imaging burden. Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly studied for vascular risk prediction, image analysis, procedural support, and surveillance, but the maturity of these applications is heterogeneous. This review evaluates recent AI applications in aortic disease and PAD, emphasizing the distinction between algorithmic performance, external validation, demonstrated workflow impact, and proven patient benefit. Automated image segmentation and opportunistic abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) detection have the most mature evidence base, including retrospective external validation and, for AI-assisted AAA case finding, nonrandomized evidence of improved downstream evaluation and follow-up. Conversely, while machine-learning models for vascular risk prediction and disease progression often report high retrospective accuracy, their clinical readiness remains strictly investigational. Across most aortic and PAD applications, there is a persistent lack of independent external validation, decision-curve analysis, and prospective testing against standard care. Image-fusion guidance has clinical evidence for reducing procedural resource use, but this evidence should not be conflated with autonomous AI or robotic navigation. Cardiovascular digital twins remain largely mechanistic, computational, or early-development frameworks. AI can augment vascular imaging and risk assessment, but high retrospective accuracy is not equivalent to clinical utility. Current guideline thresholds for repair, revascularization, and surveillance should not be altered on the basis of investigational radiomics or prediction models. Translation requires multicenter external validation, prospective workflow evaluation, calibration and net-benefit assessment, transparent reporting, and trials demonstrating improved patient outcomes.

Estrogen attenuates thoracic aortic aneurysm progression through reduced extracellular matrix remodeling and wall stiffening in a severe Marfan syndrome mouse model.

Front Physiol · Dwivedi KK, Wu Y, Wagenseil JE · Full text
Abstract

Thoracic aortic aneurysm (TAA) is a life-threatening cardiovascular disease characterized by progressive aortic dilation and fatal dissection or rupture. Marfan syndrome (MFS)-associated TAA exhibits sexual dimorphism, suggesting a role for sex hormones. Although 17β-estradiol attenuates aortic root dilation in male MFS mice, the contribution of endogenous estrogen to sex-specific TAA progression and its effects on aortic wall extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and wall stiffness remains poorly understood. Using a severe MFS mouse model (Fbn1mgR/mgR ) with TAA formation and reduced lifespan, male mice received 17β-estradiol or placebo pellets and female mice underwent bilateral ovariectomy (OVX) or sham surgery, starting at one month of age. Abstract

UNLABELLED: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening inflammatory vascular disease with no effective medical therapies to mitigate its progression and rupture. Although interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) plays a central role in AAA pathobiology, the specific impact of its inhibition on progression and rupture remains unclear. This study evaluated whether selective antibody-mediated IL-1β inhibition can reduce aneurysm progression in a rupture-prone AAA model in rats. Infrarenal AAAs were induced in male and female Sprague-Dawley rats (N = 30). The 14-day cohort included n = 5/sex/treatment group, whereas the day 6 inflammatory analyses included male rats only (n = 5/treatment group). AAAs were induced using intraluminal porcine pancreatic elastase alongside oral β-aminopropionitrile administration to promote AAA rupture. After 3 days, rats received a neutralizing anti-IL-1β antibody or a control immunoglobulin G twice weekly. Aneurysm growth, rupture incidence, and histopathology were assessed over 14 days. These findings suggest that IL-1β plays a multifaceted role in maintaining counter-regulatory immune responses in established AAA, indicating that treatment with anti-IL-1β antibody did not demonstrate measurable structural benefit under the experimental conditions examined. CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening condition with no effective medical treatments to prevent its progression and rupture. Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) is essential for AAA formation, as evidenced by the significant attenuation of aneurysm formation in IL-1β receptor knockout models. However, our study demonstrated that treatment with a neutralizing anti-IL-1β antibody did not significantly alter AAA growth or rupture rates in a rupture-prone rat model. These results indicate that anti-IL-1β antibody treatment initiated after aneurysm induction may have limited efficacy in this short-term rupture-prone model, but these findings do not exclude a potential effect of IL-1β inhibition during earlier stages of aneurysm formation. However, as pharmacodynamic target engagement was not directly quantified, incomplete IL-1β neutralization cannot be excluded.

Transcatheter Thrombus Removal With the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System in Children With Congenital Heart Disease.

New todayCase reportCatheter Cardiovasc Interv · Balachandran AN, Hill KD, Chamberlain RC et al. · Full text
Abstract

Thromboembolic complications in children with congenital heart disease (CHD) carry significant morbidity and mortality, particularly in patients with single ventricle physiology. Current management strategies, including systemic anticoagulation, thrombolytics, and surgical thrombectomy, each carry substantial risks in this population. Transcatheter aspiration thrombectomy represents an emerging alternative. We retrospectively reviewed all pediatric patients with CHD at our institution who underwent transcatheter aspiration thrombectomy using the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System between January 2024 and February 2025. Patient demographics, cardiac diagnosis, thrombus location, procedural details, and clinical outcomes were recorded. Five patients (age 5 months to 9 years; weight 6.3-25.9 kg) with diverse CHD diagnoses underwent six thrombectomy procedures. Thrombus locations included the Fontan circuit, inferior vena cava, pulmonary arteries, and internal jugular veins. Technical success with angiographic restoration of antegrade flow and no residual gradient across the segment of interest, was achieved in all cases (100%). Blood loss ranged from 6 to 23 mL/kg; all patients required packed red blood cell transfusion. No significant intraprocedural hemodynamic instability occurred. Three patients were discharged home with confirmed vessel patency on follow-up imaging (echocardiography and/or vascular ultrasound at 4-6 months post intervention). Two patients, both on extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) support prior to the procedure, subsequently died from causes unrelated to the thrombectomy intervention. In this limited case series, the Penumbra Indigo Lightning System demonstrated feasibility for transcatheter thrombus removal in pediatric patients across a spectrum of CHD. Given the limited number of available catheter systems in this population, this approach may be considered as a first-line or adjunct therapy in select patients when conventional medical management is insufficient or contraindicated. Further prospective studies are needed to define optimal patient selection, procedural techniques, and post-procedural management.

Variation in Inferior Vena Cava Filter Retrieval Across U.S. Veterans Health Administration Facilities.

J Vasc Interv Radiol · Trivedi P, Wells K, Puello Baron M et al. · Full text
Abstract

PURPOSE: Inferior vena cava (IVC) filters prevent pulmonary embolism (PE) in patients unable to receive anticoagulation but require prompt retrieval once protection is no longer needed. Despite national safety advisories and multidisciplinary guidelines, IVC filter retrieval remains low in the United States. How much variation underlies low retrieval across Veterans Affairs (VA) facilities has not been studied. MATERIALS AND METHODS: This retrospective longitudinal cohort study included Veterans who underwent IVC filter implantation at VA facilities between 2016 and 2023. Veterans who died within 90 days of implantation or lacked 1 year of follow-up were excluded. IVC filter implantation and retrieval were identified using procedure codes in the VA Corporate Data Warehouse. A multivariable Bayesian mixed-effects model estimated risk-adjusted retrieval rates across facilities. Logistic regression was performed to identify specific patient- and facility-level factors associated with non-retrieval. RESULTS: Among 3,670 Veterans (median age 69 years; 95.9% male), retrieval occurred in 10.5% within 90 days, 22.2% within 1 year, and 24.7% overall. Risk-adjusted 1-year retrieval varied widely across facilities: 0-60%. Older veteran age (> 75 years), facility academic affiliation, and Southern census region were associated with lower retrieval, whereas implantation at higher-complexity facilities and pre-COVID years were associated with higher retrieval.
