Prospects for adiponectin-based therapeutics in myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury.

ReviewPharmacol Ther · Bahit MC, Gibson CM, Lei Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Adiponectin is an adipocyte-derived hormone with broad cardiometabolic benefits, circulating at high levels in healthy individuals but reduced in obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Low adiponectin concentrations are strongly associated with coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI), and heart failure. MIRI remains a major unmet need in contemporary ST-segment-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) care. Although reperfusion is essential in limiting ischemic myocardial necrosis, it causes deleterious effects such as impaired carbohydrate oxidation, excessive fatty-acid oxidation, intracellular acidosis, calcium (Ca2+) overload, oxidative/nitrative stress, and mitochondrial fragmentation, that expand infarct size, worsen microvascular obstruction, and fuel adverse ventricular remodeling. Activation of the adiponectin-adiponectin receptor (AdipoR) axis directly counteracts these processes by stimulating adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-dependent metabolic rebalancing, restoring mitochondrial fusion-fission homeostasis, enhancing oxidative capacity, reducing inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS-) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase-mediated injury, improving endothelial nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability, and enhancing microvascular perfusion. In animal models, adiponectin or its mimetics consistently reduced infarct size, improved myocardial function, and attenuated apoptosis, inflammation, and no-reflow. A growing array of therapeutic strategies has emerged, each bringing its own pharmacologic nuances and implications for translation to the clinic. This review summarizes current strategies to enhance adiponectin action, highlights the progress in peptide and small-molecule mimetics, and discusses future directions for clinical translation in cardiometabolic diseases.

Mitochondria-targeted drug delivery systems for aging-related diseases: Mechanisms, strategies, and advances.

ReviewJ Control Release · Zhang F, Ma H, Zhou S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Mitochondrial dysfunction, characterized by increased oxidative stress, impaired energy metabolism, disrupted mitochondrial dynamics, abnormal quality control, and activation of apoptosis, represents a critical pathological basis for a wide range of aging-related diseases (ARDs), including neurodegenerative diseases, cardiovascular disorders, sarcopenia, and osteoarthritis. Mitochondria-targeted drug delivery systems increase the effective concentration of bioactive molecules within these dysfunctional organelles, offering novel strategies for the precise modulation of mitochondrial function. This review summarizes structural and functional changes in mitochondria during aging and their associations with major ARDs. We comprehensively review key advances in the design principles, mechanisms of action, and applications of passive targeting, stimuli-responsive systems, active targeting, and biomimetic drug delivery platforms. In addition, emerging interventions such as NAD+ supplementation, mitochondrial gene delivery, EV-mediated mitochondrial delivery and mitochondrial transplantation are discussed. Despite the demonstrated potential of these systems to restore mitochondrial homeostasis and tissue functionality in various disease models, their clinical translation remains constrained by obstacles including unstable targeting efficacy under pathological conditions, insufficient long-term safety of materials, manufacturing complexity, and a lack of standardized evaluation criteria. Future efforts should focus on integrating disease heterogeneity, advanced materials, and multi-omics technologies to advance mitochondria-targeted therapies toward standardization, individualization, and diagnostic-therapeutic integration.

Metabolic and Immunometabolic Neuroprotection in Glaucoma: Mitochondrial Dysfunction, Neuroinflammation, and Emerging Therapeutic Strategies.

ReviewMolecules · Martucci A, Anselmi C, Romano E et al. · Full text
Abstract

Glaucoma is increasingly recognized as a multifactorial neurodegenerative optic neuropathy in which intraocular pressure (IOP) is one of several stress amplifiers rather than a sufficient etiological explanation. Vascular dysregulation, mitochondrial and metabolic vulnerability, oxidative stress, excitotoxicity, glial dysregulation, and persistent neuroimmune activation can converge on retinal ganglion cell (RGC) and optic nerve injury. This broader framework is particularly relevant to normal-tension glaucoma (NTG), which represents a large proportion of primary open-angle glaucoma in Asian populations, and to patients who continue to progress despite IOP being maintained within the target range. Consequently, mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolic impairment, oxidative stress, and the associated neuroinflammatory response are not merely downstream adjunctive targets: they are core disease mechanisms and potential treatment targets in pressure-independent and pressure-resistant glaucoma. Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), nicotinamide, pyruvate, and citicoline are emerging metabolic and neuroprotective agents whose primary actions involve mitochondrial bioenergetics, redox homeostasis, membrane integrity, and cellular stress responses, with potential secondary effects on neuroinflammatory pathways. Chronic activation of microglia and astrocytes promotes the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species, and apoptotic mediators, leading to RGC degeneration. CoQ10 may counteract these mechanisms through antioxidant activity and stabilization of mitochondrial function; nicotinamide, a precursor of NAD+, may enhance mitochondrial resilience and suppress inflammatory cascades associated with energy failure; pyruvate supports aerobic metabolism and may limit oxidative, inflammatory, and excitotoxic injury; and citicoline supports membrane homeostasis, phospholipid synthesis, neuronal repair, and visual-pathway function. Combined targeting of these interconnected pathways may enhance RGC resilience independently of, and in addition to, IOP reduction. However, current clinical evidence remains insufficient to establish sustained neuroprotection or disease-modifying efficacy. This review examines the interplay among these mechanisms in glaucomatous neurodegeneration and critically discusses the experimental and clinical evidence supporting metabolic and immunometabolic neuroprotective strategies as potential adjuncts to conventional intraocular pressure-lowering therapy.

Integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing identify emodin as a candidate modulator of STAT3 signaling in abdominal aortic aneurysm.

Vascul Pharmacol · Liu Y, Xu Z, Deng L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease lacking effective pharmacological therapies. To identify potential therapeutic targets and candidate compounds, we performed integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing by combining Mendelian randomization (MR), summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR), and Bayesian colocalization. This framework prioritized multiple druggable candidates, among which STAT3 emerged as a key regulatory node. Subsequent functional studies demonstrated that emodin suppressed vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation, migration, phenotypic switching, and inflammatory responses. These effects were accompanied by reduced STAT3 phosphorylation and nuclear localization. Molecular docking suggested a potential binding mode of emodin within the STAT3 SH2 domain, while cellular thermal shift assays demonstrated thermal stabilization of STAT3 by emodin in intact cells, consistent with target engagement. Notably, emodin and the selective STAT3 inhibitor STAT3-IN-13 produced comparable effects, and co-treatment did not yield additive efficacy, suggesting overlapping mechanisms. In vivo, emodin attenuated aneurysm development and reduced vascular inflammation in experimental models. Collectively, these findings identify emodin as a candidate modulator of AAA progression acting through suppression of STAT3-associated signaling pathways, and demonstrate the potential of genetic evidence-guided drug repurposing strategies in complex vascular diseases.

The emerging molecular landscape of abdominal aortic aneurysms: non-coding RNAs as diagnostic and therapeutic targets.

ReviewMol Biol Rep · Skórka P, Brelik J, Kiełbowski K et al. · Full text
Abstract

An abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a progressive, often asymptomatic condition whose rupture is associated with a high mortality rate. Ultrasonography and computed tomography are established diagnostic methods, while molecular biomarkers may provide additional information about disease activity. This review discusses the potential role of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) as diagnostic and therapeutic tools in AAAs. These molecules, including microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs), are strongly involved in the regulation of gene expression. Through binding to their target mRNAs, miRNAs primarily suppress translation or promote mRNA degradation. Some lncRNAs and circRNAs act as miRNA sponges, affecting their availability. Here, these mechanisms are discussed by pathological function, distinguishing human tissue and circulating studies from animal, cellular and computational findings. Particular attention is given to miR-21, H19, GAS5, circCdyl and miR-26b-3p, which have functional experimental evidence or human biomarker data beyond expression profiling. Abnormal expression can be detected in peripheral blood or aortic tissue, but these findings do not necessarily predict aneurysm growth or rupture. Integration of ncRNA profiling with imaging and clinical parameters may improve the assessment of AAAs. However, prospective validation and further studies of delivery and safety are needed before these molecules can be used in clinical practice.

Matrix metalloproteinase imaging and survival prospects with aging in murine aortic aneurysm.

EJNMMI Res · Ghim M, Varli O, Ahmad AA et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are elevated in aortic aneurysm (AA), where older age is a risk factor. The relationship among aging, aortic MMP activity, and AA development has not been systematically evaluated. This study leveraged MMP-targeted molecular imaging to investigate how aging affects aortic MMP activity, aneurysm development, and survival. RESULTS: AA development and animal survival were monitored for 28 days after Angiotensin (Ang)-II infusion in 8-10-week-old (young) and > 51-week-old (old) apolipoprotein E (Apoe)-/- mice. Old animals' survival at 28 days was significantly lower than that of young Ang-II-infused Apoe-/- mice (P < 0.05). 64Cu-RYM2 PET/CT showed significantly higher aortic MMP activation before and 1 week after Ang-II infusion in old compared to young Apoe-/- mice. MMP-targeted molecular imaging provides insights regarding aneurysm survival prospects in this exploratory cohort. New todayJ Cardiothorac Surg · Zhao W, Hu Y, Yan X et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a severe cardiovascular disease involving various genetic and metabolic factors. Micronutrients play critical roles in cardiovascular health, yet their specific impacts on AAA remain unclear. This study aims to explore the effects of micronutrients calcium and zinc on AAA risk mediated by plasma proteins using the two-sample Mendelian Randomization (MR) approach. METHODS: Utilizing large-scale GWAS data from the UK Biobank; FinnGen databases and Decode database, we assessed the causal relationships between 16 micronutrients concentration and AAA risk and analyzed potential mechanisms mediated by plasma proteins. COL10A1+ fibroblasts define rupture-prone abdominal aortic aneurysms and enable molecular risk stratification.

J Transl Med · Zhuo H, Ou C, Duan M et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) rupture remains the leading cause of AAA-related mortality, while current clinical management relies primarily on aneurysm diameter, which inadequately predicts rupture risk. Increasing evidence suggests that stromal cell heterogeneity critically shapes vascular remodeling; however, the specific fibroblast states associated with AAA rupture predisposition remain poorly defined. METHODS: We integrated cross-species single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets spanning the full spectrum of AAA progression to systematically characterize fibroblast heterogeneity and state transitions. Pseudotime trajectory analysis, transcriptional regulatory network inference, and ligand-receptor interaction modeling were applied to delineate fibroblast-associated microenvironmental programs. Based on these findings, a fibroblast-derived molecular signature was constructed and validated in an independent human AAA cohort. RESULTS: Single-cell analysis revealed a progressive transition from homeostatic IGFBP6+ fibroblasts to a disease-associated COL10A1+ fibroblast state during AAA progression. Translationally, leveraging this fibroblast state enabled the development of a minimal two-gene risk score (COL10A1-IGFBP6), which stratified AAA patients by molecular rupture risk independently of aneurysm diameter in the validation cohort. CONCLUSIONS: This study identifies a COL10A1+ fibroblast-associated transcriptional state that defines a rupture-prone vascular microenvironment in abdominal aortic aneurysm. The derived COL10A1-IGFBP6 signature establishes a biologically informed framework for molecular risk stratification, complementing anatomical size-based assessment and providing a foundation for future development of mechanism-guided strategies to refine AAA risk evaluation.

Endothelial indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 drives high-tryptophan diet-mediated thrombosis in cancer-bearing mice.

New todayBlood Vessel Thromb Hemost · Jose A, Lotfollahzadeh S, Natrakul A et al. · Full text
Abstract

Cancer-associated venous thromboembolism is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with solid tumors and is driven, in part, by tumor-induced metabolic reprogramming. The tryptophan (Trp)-kynurenine (Kyn) pathway, regulated by indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1), is frequently upregulated in cancer and generates bioactive metabolites with immunomodulatory and vascular effects. Although pharmacologic inhibition of IDO1 reduces thrombosis in tumor-bearing mice, the endothelial-specific contribution of IDO1 to cancer-associated thrombogenesis has not been genetically defined. Here, we used IDO1 global knockout and inducible IDO1 endothelial-specific knockout mice in a syngeneic MC38 tumor model combined with dietary Trp modulation to delineate the vascular role of IDO1. Abstract

Aspiration Thrombectomy for High-Risk Pulmonary Embolism With Life-Threatening Anemia and Active Uterine Bleeding: A Case Report.

Case reportAm J Case Rep · Saoudi O, Wang B · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND High-risk pulmonary embolism (PE) carries significant mortality, and management becomes complex when it coexists with severe anemia and active bleeding, as thrombolysis is then contraindicated. We report the case of a patient with high-risk PE complicated by severe anemia (hemoglobin 41 g/L) and active uterine bleeding from adenomyosis. CASE REPORT A 48-year-old woman presented with haemodynamic instability (blood pressure 74/47 mm Hg), critical anemia (hemoglobin 41 g/L), and a 3-year history of irregular vaginal bleeding from adenomyosis. Computed tomography (CT) pulmonary angiography confirmed bilateral PE with right pulmonary artery main-trunk occlusion; echocardiography showed severe pulmonary hypertension, and duplex ultrasound showed extensive left lower-limb deep vein thrombosis. Thrombolysis was contraindicated. Emergency percutaneous aspiration thrombectomy was performed using large-bore catheters (22F, 20F, 16F) with concurrent inferior vena cava filter placement, reducing pulmonary artery pressure from 51/23 to 34/12 mm Hg; the estimated blood loss, quantified from filtered aspirate, was approximately 100 mL. Adjuncts included transfusion of 13 units of packed red cells, dilatation and curettage, gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist therapy, and titrated anticoagulation. At discharge after a 24-day hospitalization she was hemodynamically stable, and free of dyspnea, with resolved edema and hemoglobin 72 g/L, on oral rivaroxaban. CONCLUSIONS In this thrombolysis-contraindicated patient, aspiration thrombectomy with inferior vena cava filter placement was a feasible rescue strategy that achieved rapid hemodynamic and angiographic improvement without thrombolysis, with palliative rather than curative intent. As a single case, it cannot establish comparative safety or efficacy, and broader conclusions regarding efficacy cannot be drawn. It illustrates a potentially useful approach in similarly complex scenarios where coordinated multidisciplinary care is essential.

Abstract

BACKGROUND: Cancer patients undergoing surgery are at significantly increased risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE). Although extended thromboprophylaxis is recommended, the optimal anticoagulant type and duration remain unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the comparative efficacy and safety of different extended thromboprophylaxis in this population.
