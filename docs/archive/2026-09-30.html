Mitochondrial-targeted DNA nanostructures deliver NAD+ to attenuate mitochondrial oxidative injury in renal ischemia-reperfusion.

New todayJ Mater Chem B · Ma J, Zeng X, Li L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is a leading cause of acute kidney injury (AKI) and is associated with substantial morbidity, delayed recovery, and impaired quality of life. Its progression is closely linked to mitochondrial oxidative stress, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) depletion, and insufficient tubular repair. NAD+ is a key cofactor that sustains mitochondrial bioenergetics and redox homeostasis and supports sirtuin-dependent repair signaling. Therefore, restoring NAD+ metabolism is an attractive therapeutic strategy for IRI. Nevertheless, direct NAD+ administration is limited by poor membrane permeability, rapid metabolic turnover, and insufficient tissue exposure. Tetrahedral DNA nanostructures (TDNs), as programmable and biocompatible DNA nanomaterials, provide an attractive platform for precision drug delivery and chemical functionalization. Here, we constructed TDN-TPP@NAD+, a triphenylphosphonium (TPP)-functionalized TDN carried NAD+ to improve its delivery context in renal IRI. TDN-TPP@NAD+ promoted intracellular delivery of NAD+ in hypoxia-injured HK-2 cells and sustained renal retention after systemic administration. The delivered NAD+ alleviated mitochondrial damage and was associated with restoration of SIRT1-PGC1α-mtTFA signaling, while the TDN scaffold also contributed to the reduction of excessive ROS through its intrinsic antioxidant properties. Thus TDN-TPP@NAD+ reduced tubular pathological injury, and improved renal biochemical indices in vivo. TDN-TPP@NAD+ provides a promising nanotherapeutic strategy for IRI-induced AKI and mitochondrial metabolic disorder-related diseases.

Abstract

Metabolism of Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide+ in Atherosclerosis: Mechanisms and Nutritional Modulation.

Nutr Rev · Zhu J, Li J, Qin Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

This narrative review aims to provide a vascular-oriented and nutrition-relevant synthesis of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide+ (NAD+) metabolism in atherosclerosis and to clarify how dietary and lifestyle factors may modulate NAD+ homeostasis in vascular disease. Although NAD+ has emerged as a central regulator of vascular redox balance, mitochondrial function, inflammation, and DNA repair, its role in atherosclerosis remains conceptually fragmented and has not yet been fully examined within the context of nutrition-relevant research frameworks. We searched PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, and Google Scholar and synthesized evidence from mechanistic, preclinical, and clinical studies. Current evidence suggests that NAD+ network remodeling in atherosclerosis reflects the interplay of precursor availability, enzymatic consumption, and plaque microenvironment stressors. Human studies have consistently demonstrated systemic NAD-related metabolite engagement following precursor supplementation; however, convincing evidence for direct antiatherosclerotic benefit remains limited, and circulating NAD+ measures may not adequately represent vascular NAD+ status. By integrating vascular biology with nutrition-based modulation strategies, this review provides a practical conceptual framework for interpreting current evidence and guiding future research. Well-designed trials incorporating vascular-specific biomarkers and clinically meaningful cardiovascular endpoints are needed to determine the therapeutic relevance of NAD+-targeted nutritional interventions.
