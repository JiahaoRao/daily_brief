Biomaterials Targeting Mitochondrial NAD+ Metabolism for Tissue Regeneration.

New todayReviewAdv Healthc Mater · Ma X, Li M, Ren T et al. · Full text
Abstract

Mitochondrial nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) has emerged as a pivotal regulator in tissue regeneration, central to cellular energy metabolism and redox homeostasis. It regulates key mitochondrial processes-including energy production, reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging, and cell death-primarily through the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TAC), oxidative phosphorylation, and sirtuin deacetylase activity. This coordinated regulation fosters a favorable metabolic and immune microenvironment conducive to tissue repair. Strategies to increase mitochondrial NAD+ levels, such as delivery, precursor supplementation (e.g., NR or NMN), catalytic conversion, and inhibition of NAD+-consuming enzymes, have demonstrated significant efficacy in promoting regeneration across diverse tissues, including bone, cartilage, tooth, skin, myocardium, muscle, nerve, and vascular. There is no review that systematically addresses the latest progress in the advances of biomaterials targeting mitochondrial NAD+ metabolism for tissue regeneration. This review fills the gap by outlining the central role of biomaterials targeting mitochondrial NAD+ in tissue regeneration. We introduce the biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of mitochondrial NAD+, highlight the intervention strategies to boost NAD+ levels, and summarize the applications across diverse regenerative contexts. Finally, we discuss prevailing challenges and future prospects, providing new theoretical and interventional insights for regenerative medicine.

Sirtuin-1 in Hypertension: Linking Oxidative Stress and Mitochondrial Dysfunction to Vascular Aging and Therapeutic Potential.

New todayReviewAntioxidants (Basel) · Budziak S, Kloza M, Baranowski P et al. · Full text
Abstract

Hypertension is a multifactorial cardiovascular disorder characterized by oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, chronic inflammation, mitochondrial impairment, and accelerated vascular aging, all of which contribute to hypertension-mediated target organ damage. Among the molecular pathways involved in these processes, sirtuin-1 (SIRT1), a nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+)-dependent deacetylase, has emerged as a potential regulator of vascular and metabolic homeostasis. This review summarizes current evidence on the role of SIRT1 in the pathophysiology of hypertension, with particular emphasis on its interactions with oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, endothelial integrity, inflammation, fibrosis, and cellular senescence. Experimental studies suggest that SIRT1 modulation can improve endothelial function and mitochondrial homeostasis, attenuate oxidative stress, inflammation, and vascular remodeling, and limit organ damage in several models. Effects on blood pressure are less consistent and appear to depend on the model and intervention used. Although clinical evidence remains limited, altered SIRT1 expression or activity has been reported in association with hypertension and preeclampsia, while preliminary studies suggest that interventions proposed to activate SIRT1 may improve vascular function and inflammatory status. However, changes in SIRT1 expression might not necessarily reflect changes in its enzymatic activity, and the effects of pharmacological interventions cannot always be attributed specifically to SIRT1. Overall, SIRT1 represents a potential therapeutic target whose value as a biomarker remains uncertain; however, well-designed clinical studies are required to establish its prognostic value and determine whether SIRT1 modulation can improve long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

Prospects for adiponectin-based therapeutics in myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury.

ReviewPharmacol Ther · Bahit MC, Gibson CM, Lei Y et al. · Full text
Abstract

Adiponectin is an adipocyte-derived hormone with broad cardiometabolic benefits, circulating at high levels in healthy individuals but reduced in obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease. Low adiponectin concentrations are strongly associated with coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI), and heart failure. MIRI remains a major unmet need in contemporary ST-segment-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) care. Although reperfusion is essential in limiting ischemic myocardial necrosis, it causes deleterious effects such as impaired carbohydrate oxidation, excessive fatty-acid oxidation, intracellular acidosis, calcium (Ca2+) overload, oxidative/nitrative stress, and mitochondrial fragmentation, that expand infarct size, worsen microvascular obstruction, and fuel adverse ventricular remodeling. Activation of the adiponectin-adiponectin receptor (AdipoR) axis directly counteracts these processes by stimulating adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK)-dependent metabolic rebalancing, restoring mitochondrial fusion-fission homeostasis, enhancing oxidative capacity, reducing inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS-) and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) oxidase-mediated injury, improving endothelial nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability, and enhancing microvascular perfusion. In animal models, adiponectin or its mimetics consistently reduced infarct size, improved myocardial function, and attenuated apoptosis, inflammation, and no-reflow. A growing array of therapeutic strategies has emerged, each bringing its own pharmacologic nuances and implications for translation to the clinic. This review summarizes current strategies to enhance adiponectin action, highlights the progress in peptide and small-molecule mimetics, and discusses future directions for clinical translation in cardiometabolic diseases.

Tissue Cell · Jiang J, Xu J, Wu T et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Pulmonary vascular remodelling induced by the abnormal proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells (PASMCs) is a core pathological feature of hypoxic pulmonary hypertension (HPH), but its exact pathogenesis remains poorly understood. This study aimed to elucidate the functional role and intrinsic regulatory mechanisms of an alveolar epithelial cell (AEC) senescence-associated phenotype in the pathogenesis of HPH. METHODS: A 4-week hypoxia-induced HPH rat model and an in vitro hypoxia-induced AEC senescence-associated phenotype model were established. The resulting increase in ceramide accumulation was associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and the senescence-associated phenotype in AECs. In this preclinical setting, AECs with this phenotype may further contribute to PASMC proliferation and HPH development. MOTS-c attenuates placental vascular endothelial cell apoptosis in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy by inhibiting K33-linked deubiquitination of BACH1.

New todayCell Commun Signal · Li S, Ai L, Ge L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is a gestational liver disorder characterized by impaired placental vascular architecture, leading to placental dysfunction and fetal growth restriction. Our prior work demonstrated that the transcription repressor BACH1 modulates SLC25A51 expression to regulate placental injury under ICP conditions. MOTS-c, a mitochondria-originated peptide, participates in vascular homeostasis, yet its biological functions within the placental vasculature during ICP have not been fully characterized. Clinical analyses revealed significantly reduced MOTS-c protein levels in both maternal serum and placental tissues from ICP patients. Collectively, these findings establish MOTS-c as a placenta-targeted endogenous regulator that ameliorates ICP-associated placental vascular pathology and adverse perinatal outcomes via modulation of the BACH1/SLC25A51 signaling axis.

Abstract

Aortic aneurysm and dissection (AAD) is the major cause of mortality in patients with Marfan syndrome (MFS), with no currently available effective therapies. FBN1 mutations disrupt extracellular matrix homeostasis through impaired microfibril function, leading to aberrant matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) activity and compensatory lysyl oxidase (LOX) upregulation. Integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing identify emodin as a candidate modulator of STAT3 signaling in abdominal aortic aneurysm.

Vascul Pharmacol · Liu Y, Xu Z, Deng L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening vascular disease lacking effective pharmacological therapies. To identify potential therapeutic targets and candidate compounds, we performed integrative genetic analyses for drug repurposing by combining Mendelian randomization (MR), summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR), and Bayesian colocalization. The Diabetes Paradox in Aortic Aneurysms: An Unexpected Inverse Association.

New todayReviewBiomedicines · Orchi P, Saltarocchi S, Spunticchia F et al. · Full text
Abstract

Aortic aneurysms are associated with substantial morbidity and mortalitbecause of the risk of progressive dilation, rupture, and related acute aortic complications. Although diabetes mellitus (DM) is a major cardiovascular risk factor, epidemiological studies have consistently reported an inverse association between DM and the prevalence and progression of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). More limited evidence suggests an inverse association between diabetes and thoracic aortic aneurysm. Accordingly, this review focuses primarily on AAA; where available, evidence concerning TAA and aortic dissection is discussed separately. This review examines the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying the inverse association between DM and aortic aneurysm development. Key mechanisms include the effects of hyperglycemia on extracellular matrix remodeling, particularly through modulation of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP-2 and MMP-9) activity, increased formation of advanced glycation end products (AGEs), and enhanced collagen cross-linking. Experimental and translational evidence suggests that diabetes may influence inflammatory cell states and mural neoangiogenesis, although these effects appear to be context- and model-dependent. Antidiabetic therapies, including metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, thiazolidinediones, sulfonylureas, and SGLT2 inhibitors, may also contribute to the observed association through glucose-lowering and/or pleiotropic vascular effects. Furthermore, diabetes-related changes in aortic wall biomechanics, including increased wall thickness and potentially altered wall stress, may contribute to aneurysm stabilization. Overall, DM is associated with a lower prevalence and slower progression of AAA, potentially through a combination of metabolic, inflammatory, structural, and treatment-related mechanisms. These observations should not be interpreted as evidence that diabetes or chronic hyperglycemia is clinically beneficial; rather, they may help identify biological pathways relevant to aneurysm development and progression.

Abstract

Background/Objectives: Direct renal artery compromise after standard endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) is rare and may result from mechanical ostial coverage or atheroembolism. Methods: We retrospectively reviewed all consecutive elective standard infrarenal EVAR procedures performed at a single center from March 2016 to June 2025, excluding repairs requiring planned renal incorporation. Abstract

Background: Renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is the leading cause of acute kidney injury (AKI). It is associated with reduced blood flow as encountered in vascular surgery, including aortic aneurysm repair and kidney transplantation. In this study, we investigated whether pre- and/or post-administration of sodium thiosulfate (STS), an FDA-approved hydrogen sulfide donor drug, attenuated IRI-induced AKI in rats. Methods: AKI was induced in male rats by clamping both renal arteries for 60 min. At 30 min before clamping, and 30 min after reperfusion, STS was administered at a weigh-based dose to achieve a circulating concentration of 150 μM, after which the rats were kept in metabolic cages for urine and blood collection on postoperative days (POD) 3, 5, and 7.
