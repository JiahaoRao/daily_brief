Magnesium Phosphate Composite Hydrogel Dressing Promotes Diabetic Wound Repair Through Anti-Inflammatory, Antioxidant, Angiogenic, and Metabolic Regulatory Mechanisms.

Adv Healthc Mater · Wang X, Zuo Y, Liu S et al. · Full text
Abstract

Chronic non-healing diabetic wounds pose a significant clinical challenge, attributed to a pathogenic microenvironment characterized by persistent inflammation, excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species, impaired angiogenesis, and mitochondrial dysfunction. Herein, we reported a novel multifunctional hydrogel dressing aimed at promoting diabetic wound healing through the synergistic action of anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, angiogenic, and metabolic regulatory mechanisms. First, nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)-templated magnesium phosphate nanoparticles (NMP NPs) were synthesized through a biomimetic mineralization strategy. Subsequently, the NMP NPs were incorporated into a double-network hydrogel that formed by dynamic Schiff base crosslinking and photocuring of methacrylated gelatin and oxidized dextran, resulting in a multifunctional composite hydrogel (NMP/GO) with tissue adhesion properties and multiple biological activities. The NMP/GO hydrogel dressing slowly releases Mg2+ ions and NMN within the diabetic microenvironment, where Mg2+ modulates inflammatory responses and promotes angiogenesis, while NMN restores redox balance and ameliorates mitochondrial dysfunction. Owing to this synergistic effect, the NMP/GO hydrogel dressing significantly promotes diabetic wound healing by reducing inflammation, mitigating oxidative stress, restoring mitochondrial function, and enhancing angiogenic potential. Consequently, the NMP/GO hydrogel dressing presents a promising therapeutic strategy for advanced diabetic wound management with high translational potential.

Mitochondrial-targeted DNA nanostructures deliver NAD+ to attenuate mitochondrial oxidative injury in renal ischemia-reperfusion.

J Mater Chem B · Ma J, Zeng X, Li L et al. · Full text
Abstract

Renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is a leading cause of acute kidney injury (AKI) and is associated with substantial morbidity, delayed recovery, and impaired quality of life. Its progression is closely linked to mitochondrial oxidative stress, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) depletion, and insufficient tubular repair. NAD+ is a key cofactor that sustains mitochondrial bioenergetics and redox homeostasis and supports sirtuin-dependent repair signaling. Therefore, restoring NAD+ metabolism is an attractive therapeutic strategy for IRI. Nevertheless, direct NAD+ administration is limited by poor membrane permeability, rapid metabolic turnover, and insufficient tissue exposure. Tetrahedral DNA nanostructures (TDNs), as programmable and biocompatible DNA nanomaterials, provide an attractive platform for precision drug delivery and chemical functionalization. Here, we constructed TDN-TPP@NAD+, a triphenylphosphonium (TPP)-functionalized TDN carried NAD+ to improve its delivery context in renal IRI. TDN-TPP@NAD+ promoted intracellular delivery of NAD+ in hypoxia-injured HK-2 cells and sustained renal retention after systemic administration. The delivered NAD+ alleviated mitochondrial damage and was associated with restoration of SIRT1-PGC1α-mtTFA signaling, while the TDN scaffold also contributed to the reduction of excessive ROS through its intrinsic antioxidant properties. Thus TDN-TPP@NAD+ reduced tubular pathological injury, and improved renal biochemical indices in vivo. TDN-TPP@NAD+ provides a promising nanotherapeutic strategy for IRI-induced AKI and mitochondrial metabolic disorder-related diseases.

Abstract

Aortic valve repair is a complex and evolving field that requires multidisciplinary teamwork among heart specialists to diagnose and treat various causes and mechanisms of aortic regurgitation and proximal thoracic aortic aneurysms across all age groups. New todayEur J Vasc Endovasc Surg · Guéroult AM, Holt PJ, Juszczak M et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Global Collaborators on Advanced Stent Graft Techniques for Aneurysm Repair (GLOBALSTAR) is a UK registry of complex endovascular aneurysm repair. This study aimed to examine differential outcomes after fenestrated endovascular aortic repair (FEVAR) for the following clinically relevant subgroups: women, octogenarians, and salvage FEVAR procedures to rescue previous failed repairs. METHODS: This was a retrospective cohort study of GLOBALSTAR. Standard comparative statistics were applied to peri-operative complications; Kaplan-Meier to survival; and cumulative incidence with competing risks to re-intervention and endoleaks. Log rank and Fine-Gray regression tests compared subgroup time to event functions. RESULTS: Over follow up of 1 651 FEVARs, salvage FEVARs experienced a significantly higher risk of re-intervention (subdistribution hazard ratio [sHR] 1.79, 95% confidence interval [95% CI] 1.37 - 2.34; p < .001) and graft related endoleaks type 1a or 1b (sHR 2.37, 95% CI 1.45 - 3.88; p = .001) and type 3 (sHR 2.12, 95% CI 1.36 - 3.32; p = .001) than primary FEVAR. There were more secondary ruptures following salvage FEVAR (4.0% vs. 1.7%; p = .073). Women experienced double the incidence of major complication Clavien-Dindo grades 3 - 5 (22.0% vs. 11.9%; p < .001), threefold incidence of major adverse cardiovascular event (5.9% vs. 2.1%; p = .004), and peri-operative death (5.9% vs. 2.1%; p = .006) compared with men. Advanced age alone may not be a strong contraindication to FEVAR and effective case selection may yield acceptable outcomes for octogenarians. This study is limited by its retrospective design but is valuable in terms of its large real world cohort with extended follow up. The results highlight the need for dedicated prospective studies for the three subgroups, to elucidate mechanistic determinants.

Cell Signal · Han Y, Wang J, Li R et al. · Full text
Abstract

BACKGROUND: Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) represents a widespread vascular disorder associated with high significant mortality rates, and the phenotypic transformation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) acts as a crucial pathological characteristic during the advancement of this condition. Recent studies suggest that metabolic reprogramming, particularly lactate accumulation, is important in modulating cellular processes in cardiovascular diseases. However, the mechanisms by which lactate influences VSMC dysfunction have not been fully elucidated. METHODS: An AngII-induced VSMC injury model was established by treating VSMCs with angiotensin II (AngII). Lactate production was measured using a colorimetric assay. Fluorometric assay was conducted to analyze oxygen consumption rate (OCR). Seahorse XF Glycolysis Stress Test Kit was used to analyze extracellular acidification rate (ECAR). mRNA levels were quantified by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, and protein expression was assessed by Western blotting. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were measured by flow cytometry. Cell apoptosis was evaluated using flow cytometry and TUNEL assays. To detect the presence of senescent cells, SA-β-gal staining and immunofluorescence assays were employed. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to quantify the levels of interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and interleukin-1β (IL-1β). Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) and dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed to examine the interactions between histone H3 lysine 18 lactylation (H3K18la), CCAAT enhancer binding protein beta (CEBPB), and target genes (CXCL12, p21, CDKN1A). RESULTS: In AAA patient tissues and AngII-treated VSMCs, mRNA and protein levels of p21 and CXCL12 were significantly upregulated. Silencing p21 or CXCL12 attenuated AngII-induced VSMC apoptosis. AngII treatment increased ROS production, SA-β-gal-positive cells, senescence-related P16 expression, and SASP factors (IL-6, CCL2, MMP2, CCL7), which were reversed by 2-DG treatment or p21 knockdown. AngII also promoted pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, which was alleviated by 2-DG treatment or CXCL12 silencing. CXCL12 knockdown further reduced AngII-induced the promotion of M1 macrophage polarization and the inhibition of M2 polarization. H3K18 lactylation levels were elevated in AAA tissues and AngII-treated VSMCs. Lactate treatment increased CXCL12, p21, and CEBPB expression and H3K18 lactylation level in VSMCs. ChIP assays revealed that H3K18la was enriched at the promoter region of the CEBPB gene, upregulating its expression. The upregulated CEBPB directly bound to the promoters of CXCL12 and p21, enhancing their transcription, and this binding was further enhanced by lactate treatment. Further, overexpression of p21 or CXCL12 rescued the effects of CEBPB knockdown. CONCLUSION: In an AngII-induced VSMC injury model, histone lactylation-upregulated CEBPB transcriptionally activated CXCL12 and p21, driving VSMC senescence, inflammation, and macrophage polarization in vitro. Together with correlative findings in human AAA tissues, these results identify the lactate-CEBPB-CXCL12/p21 axis as a candidate mechanism whose contribution to AAA formation and progression requires validation in in vivo aneurysm models.

Abstract

Adv Sci (Weinh) · Wu Z, Qian Y, Xu W et al. · Full text
Abstract

Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a life-threatening condition lacking effective pharmacological treatments, with emerging evidence highlighting the fundamental role of ferroptosis in its progression. Nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4), known to mediate ferritinophagy and contribute to ferroptosis, is upregulated in macrophages from both angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced murine AAA models and human AAA tissues. Global deletion of Ncoa4 in mice suppressed Ang II-induced AAA formation. New todayFront Bioeng Biotechnol · Li ZY, Fan JF, Wang XN et al. · Full text
Abstract

OBJECTIVE: Endoleaks are major complications of endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR) for treating abdominal aortic aneurysms. Sac embolization is a crucial therapeutic method, in which cell transplantation offers advantages. To attain improved survivability and adaptability, vascular wall-like tissues derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) were fabricated, and the feasibility of their transplantation for the treatment of endoleaks following EVAR was investigated. Phlegmasia alba dolens caused by external iliac vein compression from inflatable penile prosthesis reservoir malposition.

New todayCase reportJ Vasc Surg Cases Innov Tech · Attia M, Hayson A, Brumberg R · Full text
Abstract

Phlegmasia alba dolens (PAD) is a rare manifestation of extensive iliofemoral deep venous thrombosis characterized by near-complete venous outflow obstruction, resulting in severe pain, diffuse limb swelling, and pallor, whereas arterial inflow remains preserved. PAD is a vascular emergency as progression to phlegmasia cerulea dolens and venous gangrene may occur without prompt restoration of venous outflow. A 62-year-old man with a past medical history of prostate cancer status post prostatectomy presented with dyspnea, chest pain, and acute right lower extremity swelling 1 week after inflatable penile prosthesis implantation. He denied a history of prior pelvic radiation or leg swelling. Computed tomography pulmonary angiography demonstrated bilateral pulmonary emboli with right heart strain. Duplex ultrasound imaging confirmed thrombosis of the right common femoral and profunda femoris veins. Cross-sectional imaging revealed the prosthetic reservoir compressing the right external iliac vein. The patient underwent catheter-directed mechanical thrombectomy for pulmonary emboli, followed by venography via right popliteal access, demonstrating extensive iliofemoral thrombosis with near-complete iliac venous outflow obstruction. After thrombectomy of the iliac venous system, persistent 90% stenosis of the right external iliac vein was identified because of extrinsic compression from the prosthetic reservoir. Balloon angioplasty was ineffective. Intravascular ultrasound imaging-guided placement of two self-expanding venous stents (Abre 16 × 120 mm and 16 × 60 mm) restored brisk flow into the inferior vena cava. An inferior vena cava filter was placed. The patient improved clinically and was discharged on anticoagulation with planned urologic revision. Extrinsic compression of the external iliac vein by penile prosthesis reservoir malposition is a rare but important cause of PAD. Endovascular thrombectomy and stenting can successfully restore venous outflow and prevent limb loss.

Analysis of the distribution of somatic NF1 variants across functional domains further revealed significant enrichment in the GRD in benign tumors, whereas variants involving the LRD, including the Sec14-PH subdomain, showed a trend toward enrichment in premalignant and malignant tumors. Notably, multi-lesion analysis of spatially and temporally distinct tumors from 30 individuals revealed pronounced intrapatient somatic heterogeneity: despite a shared germline NF1 background, individual tumors typically carried distinct NF1 second-hit events, consistent with lesion-specific molecular divergence rather than a single shared somatic driver across lesions. In contrast, microdissected histologically distinct components within individual tumors shared underlying NF1 alterations and showed sequential acquisition of additional alterations involving CDKN2A and EED, supporting stepwise molecular evolution during malignant progression. Together, these data reveal lesion-specific molecular divergence across distinct tumors and stepwise molecular evolution within individual tumors, underscoring the value of lesion-specific genomic evaluation in NF1-associated PNSTs.

A Complementary Role for Immunohistochemistry and NGS for Detection of MTAP Loss in Patients with Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer.

Abstract

Methylthioadenosine phosphorylase (MTAP) is an enzyme encoded by MTAP gene on chromosome 9p21. MTAP inactivation introduces a metabolic vulnerability with implications for targeted therapy. MTAP loss may be detected at the protein or DNA level, but characterization of clinical assays for detection of this event in non-small cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC) is limited. Clinicopathologic and genomic correlates of MTAP gene deletion were examined via retrospective review of an institutional cohort of 5,233 NSCLC cases with available next generation sequencing (NGS) data for 447 genes, including full coverage of the MTAP coding sequence. A subset of 105 cases were examined to assess the correlation between MTAP deletion by NGS and MTAP expression by immunohistochemistry (IHC). Homozygous deletion of MTAP was detected in 5.6% of NSCLC by NGS and was significantly more common in lung adenocarcinomas from patients with never/light smoking history (34.8%), and concurrent ALK fusions (8.2%) or EGFR mutations (45.9%). Approximately 13% of MTAP "loss" cases showed a heterogenous staining pattern by IHC. Concordance of NGS and IHC for detection of MTAP deficiency in NSCLC was 97.3%, following careful quality control (including recognition of a single case with partial deletion of 3' MTAP) and exclusion of NGS results from tumors with ≤30% tumor content. The remaining two discordant cases included one case with non-diploid genome and multiple copy states resulting in an incorrect NGS interpretation and one case with copy neutral NGS result and complete loss of protein expression by IHC. MTAP protein loss was therefore observed in the absence of a sequencing alteration in <1% of cases. MTAP gene deletion appears enriched in NSCLC with certain molecular drivers. NGS and IHC can show excellent concordance for detection of MTAP loss in NSCLC, with IHC showing superior sensitivity in the context of low tumor content or subclonal events.

Dual-Foundation-Model Ensemble Predicts Gene Expression in Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer for Patient Outcome Stratification.

Abstract

Muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) exhibits significant heterogeneity in clinical outcomes and treatment responses. While gene expression profiling has provided important biological and prognostic insight, transcriptomic assays are not routinely incorporated into standard clinical practice, in part due to cost. AI models have been demonstrated to predict gene expression from H&E-stain whole-slide images (WSIs) potentially offering a scalable approach for gathering transcriptomic information for patient stratification and biomarker discovery. Assessment of Embryonic MicroRNAs and DNA Methylation Suggests That Chemotherapy Induces Differentiation of Germ Cell-Derived Trophoblastic Tumors.

Abstract

Unusual trophoblastic tumors of germ cell origin-epithelioid trophoblastic tumor (ETT), unclassified trophoblastic tumor (UTT), cystic trophoblastic tumor (CTT), and monophasic choriocarcinoma (MChorio)-are rare and mostly encountered postchemotherapy. Their relationship to other germ cell tumor types and gestational tumors remains uncertain.
